# Steering — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1962–63 supplement states that the 1963 passenger-car steering system
retains the 1961 design and that procedures absent from the supplement remain
applicable. It changes the adjustment measurement, adds service for the worm
bearing cup and steering arms, and introduces 1963 tie-rod sockets and idler
seals/washers for extended lubrication. The Corvair 95 and Greenbrier
commercial procedures are excluded.

Source files: `4-steer-61.pdf` (base, physical pp. 1–26, combined pp.
184–209; passenger procedures on printed pp. 4-1–4-13) and
`4-steer-62-63.pdf` (supplement, physical pp. 1–4, combined pp. 645–648;
passenger procedures on printed pp. 4-1–4-3).

## Passenger-car steering layout

<a id="p184"></a> **[PDF p.184](../page-images/p0184.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The 500, 700, and 900 series use a recirculating-ball steering gear and a
relay-type linkage. Pitman and idler arms connect to the relay rod; adjustable
tie rods run from the relay rod to the steering arms. Spring-loaded ball-and-
socket tie-rod joints compensate for normal internal wear. The gear-only
ratio is **18:1** and the gear-plus-linkage ratio is **23.5:1** in the
supplement's passenger-car specifications.

See the [linkage and gear diagram](../diagrams/p0185-steering-linkage-and-gear.png).

<a id="p645"></a> **[PDF p.645](../page-images/p0645.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For 1963, revised tie-rod sockets and idler-arm seals and washers permit
extended lubrication. Follow the 1963 intervals in
[Lubrication](02-lubrication.md#chassis-steering-bearings-and-universal-joints).

For source scope, the base manual's printed pp. 4-14–4-24 and the
supplement's p. 4-4 describe Corvair 95/Greenbrier commercial steering and
are omitted from this passenger-car chapter. The general trouble chart and
tool list on base pp. 4-25–4-26 are usable with the 1963 adjustments below.

## Steering-gear lubrication and adjustment

<a id="p185"></a> **[PDF p.185](../page-images/p0185.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The steering gear is factory-filled with an all-season lubricant. Check its
level at the [1963 lubrication interval](02-lubrication.md), add steering-gear
lubricant when needed, and do not drain the housing for a seasonal change.
Lubricate the four tie-rod ball-and-socket fittings with chassis lubricant at
the 1963 interval; the base manual's 1,000-mile schedule is superseded.

<a id="p645-adjust"></a> **[PDF p.645](../page-images/p0645.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-1 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Before using the adjusters to correct shimmy, loose or hard steering, or
road shock, check front alignment, shock absorbers, wheel balance, and tire
pressure. Adjust worm-bearing preload before sector lash. The supplement
replaces the base manual's inch-pound torque-wrench check at the steering
shaft nut with **pull scale J-5178 at the steering-wheel rim**.

See the [factory pull-scale setup](../diagrams/p0645-steering-pull-scale.png).

<a id="p646"></a><a id="p646-adjust"></a> **[PDF p.646](../page-images/p0646.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

1. Remove the pitman-arm nut and lock washer. Pull the arm from the sector
   shaft with tool **J-6627**, following the base procedure on printed p. 4-2.
2. Loosen the sector lash-adjuster locknut and back the screw out about
   **2½–3½ turns** to remove high-point loading.
3. With J-5178 attached at the wheel rim, measure the effort required to
   **keep the steering wheel moving**. The worm-bearing range is
   **7⁄16–11⁄16 lb pull**. Loosen the worm-bearing adjuster locknut, adjust
   until the reading is in range, snug the locknut after each change, and
   recheck with the locknut fully tightened. A lumpy feel after adjustment
   indicates likely bearing damage requiring gear disassembly and repair.
4. With worm bearings correctly adjusted, locate the steering gear's center
   by turning gently from stop to stop, counting the turns, then returning
   exactly halfway. Keep the wheel spokes horizontal. Turn the sector lash
   screw **clockwise** until J-5178 reads **1⅛–1½ lb pull** while the wheel
   traverses **¼ turn on each side** of straight ahead. Approach the travel
   stops gently; striking a stop with force can damage the gear. Tighten the
   lash-adjuster locknut and recheck. Make the final lash adjustment while
   turning the screw clockwise.
5. With steering wheel and front wheels straight ahead, install the pitman
   arm on the sector shaft.

The supplement's passenger-car specification table on printed p. 12-3
confirms both pull ranges. Its commercial-car column has different values
and does not apply here.

| Steering-gear adjustment, passenger car | 1963 pull at steering-wheel rim |
|---|---|
| Worm-bearing preload | 7⁄16–11⁄16 lb. |
| Sector lash at high point | 1⅛–1½ lb. |

## Steering wheel and column alignment

<a id="p186"></a> **[PDF p.186](../page-images/p0186.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Drive briefly on a straight path to establish the wheel position that gives
straight-ahead travel. With the front wheels straight, the coupling sawcut
and wormshaft high-point mark should align at the shaft bottom (**6 o'clock**).
If the gear is off high point, loosen both tie-rod sleeve clamps and rotate
both sleeves equally in the same direction until centered. Unequal or
opposite rotation changes front toe. Tighten the clamps afterward.

<a id="p187"></a> **[PDF p.187](../page-images/p0187.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Tie-rod clamp bosses face forward and clamp bolts point down. The steering
shaft, gear wormshaft, and coupling alignment marks must coincide. Shift
the lower mast jacket in its oversize bolt holes until it is concentric with
the steering shaft, then tighten its attaching bolts. For steering-wheel
removal, pry out the horn button, remove the receiver cup and bushing-spacer
screws and Belleville spring, remove the wheel nut and washer, then use
puller **J-2927**. Preserve the spring and seat beneath the wheel. On
installation, align the wheel and shaft marks. Steering-wheel retaining-nut
torque is **35–40 ft. lb.**

Set turn-signal housing clearance to **.080 in.** at the steering wheel by
loosening the housing lock screw and sliding the housing on the mast jacket.
Keep the Bowden-cable wire in the turn-signal switch; retighten the screw.
Use the [suspension alignment procedure](03-suspension.md#alignment-preparation-and-rear-toe-in)
for front toe adjustment. The 1963 front toe value is unresolved in the
source scan and is withheld in [Specifications](12-specifications.md).

## Steering gear overhaul and worm-bearing cup

<a id="p646-overhaul"></a> **[PDF p.646](../page-images/p0646.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After overhaul, lubrication, and assembly, Chevrolet recommends installing
the gear in the car before adjusting it by the procedure above. To replace
the worm-bearing cup with the gear dismantled and seal removed, support the
housing on a wood block and tap the old cup out with punch and hammer without
damaging the housing. Clean the cup bore. Use the old cup over the new cup
and an arbor press to press the new cup into the housing. Install a new seal
in the position shown by the base manual's gear illustration on printed
p. 4-7. The full base teardown and reassembly sequence remains to be
reconstructed here.

## Steering-arm replacement

<a id="p646-arm"></a> **[PDF p.646](../page-images/p0646.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Use this operation when collision or other damage requires a steering-arm
replacement. Disconnect its tie-rod stud. Remove the front wheel, hub, and
brake drum as a unit after removing the hub cap, dust cap, spindle cotter
pin, and spindle nut. Back off the brake adjustment if the drum will not
clear. Remove the two steering-arm retaining nuts and the arm. Refit with
the **longer retaining bolt in the forward hole** and use only the specified
special locknuts. Pack the front bearings, refit the wheel/hub/drum assembly,
and use the [1963 front-wheel bearing adjustment](03-suspension.md#front-wheel-bearing-adjustment).
Reconnect the tie-rod stud with its dust cover and cotter pin; check
alignment afterward.

## 1963 idler arm and linkage

<a id="p647-idler"></a> **[PDF p.647](../page-images/p0647.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=4-3 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1963 idler-to-relay-rod connection has new sealing for the extended
lubrication interval. For removal, take out the cotter pins and back both
idler-arm end nuts off until their castellated faces are flush with the stud
ends. Separate the relay-rod joint using the base manual's ball-stud
method, preserving the washer and seal between relay rod and idler bushing.
Repeat for the idler-to-bracket joint. Reverse the operation to install and
tighten nuts at the bracket and relay rod to **15–22 ft. lb.**

Replace the **complete idler assembly** if pivoting joints have excessive
play or forged parts show damage. For replacement, disconnect its relay-rod
ball stud as above, remove the frame-member retaining bolts, and remove the
idler-lever-to-frame spacer. Reinstall with the upper frame bolt first, then
the lower bolt and spacer; tighten frame bolts to **15–22 ft. lb.** Place
the seal, insert the ball stud in the relay-rod boss, and tighten its
castellated nut to **29–43 ft. lb.** Continue tightening to the next cotter
notch and insert the cotter pin.

## Steering gear removal and overhaul carried forward

<a id="p188"></a> **[PDF p.188](../page-images/p0188.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base exploded diagram identifies the wormshaft bearings and adjuster,
ball nut and its **48 balls**, ball guides, pitman/sector shaft and bushings,
side cover, lash adjuster and shim, and seals. Use the diagram to identify
parts before teardown; preserve the relative positions of coupling, shaft,
and gear marks.

See the [numbered exploded view](../diagrams/p0188-steering-gear-exploded.png).

<a id="p189"></a> **[PDF p.189](../page-images/p0189.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

### Remove and dismantle the steering gear

1. Support the car on a hoist or stands. Pull the pitman arm with **J-6627**.
   Remove the three gear-to-frame bolts and washers. Remove the shaft-
   coupling bolts and nuts; slide the gear forward and down out of the car.
2. Keep parts clean and work over clean paper or rags. Back off the sector
   lash adjuster several turns, then back off the worm-bearing adjuster.
   Catch lubricant while removing the three side-cover bolts and washers.
3. Withdraw side cover and pitman shaft together. If the sector does not
   clear, turn the wormshaft by hand until it passes the housing opening.
   Remove the worm-bearing adjuster, locknut, and lower bearing. Withdraw
   the wormshaft/ball-nut unit and remove the upper bearing.
4. Keep the ball nut away from both ends of the worm. Running it hard against
   an end damages the ball guides. Remove the lash-adjuster locknut; turn
   the adjuster clockwise out of the side cover and slide its shim from the
   pitman-shaft slot.

If the ball nut turns freely without binding, leave it assembled. If it
binds, remove guide-retainer screws and clamp, lift out the guides, invert
the nut, and rotate the worm back and forth over a clean pan until all
**48 balls** are recovered. Pull the empty nut endwise off the worm.

<a id="p190"></a> **[PDF p.190](../page-images/p0190.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

### Inspect and repair the gear

Wash dismantled parts in solvent, dry with clean rags, and examine the
bearings, cups, worm/nut grooves, and every ball under magnification for
indentation, chipping, or surface breakdown. Replace damaged parts with
matching service parts. Inspect the wormshaft seal, sector-shaft wear and
fit in the housing bushings, guide ends where they pick up balls, and the
wormshaft for bending or damage. The side-cover pilot bushing is serviced
with the complete side-cover assembly.

For the housing's sector-shaft bushing, support the housing in an arbor
press, drive the old bushing out from below with **J-8366-2**, and press the
new bushing in with **J-8366-1**. Service bushings are diamond-bored and
do not need reaming. Press replacement wormshaft or sector-shaft seals
squarely into the housing, using a socket on the seal's outer diameter.
The supplement's [worm-bearing cup replacement](#steering-gear-overhaul-and-worm-bearing-cup)
also applies.

### Assemble the 48-ball recirculating nut

<a id="p190-ball"></a> **[PDF p.190](../page-images/p0190.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

1. Lay the wormshaft flat and slide the nut on with guide holes up and the
   shallow end of rack teeth left as viewed from steering-wheel position.
   Align the worm and nut grooves through the guide holes.
2. Count **48 balls**. Drop **18 balls into each of two guide holes on the
   same side** of the nut (36 total), gradually turning the worm away from
   the hole being filled. Never use two holes on the same end. If balls stop
   at the worm end, hold inserted balls down with a clean blunt rod and
   work the worm backward until the circuit can be filled.

<a id="p191"></a> **[PDF p.191](../page-images/p0191.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

3. Place the remaining **12 balls**, six in each guide half, then mate the
   guide halves. Petroleum jelly at the open ends holds balls in place
   during installation. Push guides into the nut, tapping lightly only if
   needed. Install their clamp with a lock washer and tighten its screw.
4. Rotate the nut on the worm to confirm smooth travel. Keep it off the
   thread ends; sticking indicates damage missed during inspection.

See the [ball-guide assembly figures](../diagrams/p0191-ball-nut-assembly.png).

### Assemble the housing and adjust in the car

<a id="p191-housing"></a> **[PDF p.191](../page-images/p0191.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After washing out all factory lubricant, coat the adjuster threads,
side-cover bolts, and lash-adjuster threads with a non-drying, oil-resistant
sealant to prevent leakage. Keep sealant off female threads and away from
wormshaft bearings. Grease the worm bearings, pitman-shaft bushing, and ball-
nut teeth. With seals and bearing cups installed, put the upper bearing on
the wormshaft, feed the wormshaft/ball-nut unit into the housing, and install
the lower bearing in the adjuster cup with adjuster and locknut.

<a id="p192"></a> **[PDF p.192](../page-images/p0192.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Install the lash adjuster and shim in the sector-shaft slot. Its end
clearance must be **no more than .002 in.**; Chevrolet shim set **5673960**
contains .063, .065, .067, and .069 in. shims. Start the shaft pilot into
the side cover and turn the adjuster counterclockwise through the cover
opening to pull the pilot fully in. Center the ball nut on the worm. Fit a
new side-cover gasket, engage the sector's center tooth with the ball nut's
center tooth space, confirm some gear-tooth lash, and tighten the cover.

The base manual's bench adjustment uses obsolete measurement values for
this composite chapter. Fill the gear with steering-gear lubricant to the
filler-hole level, align the coupling sawcut with the wormshaft mark at
6 o'clock, install the gear, then use the
[supplement's wheel-rim pull-scale procedure](#steering-gear-lubrication-and-adjustment).

<a id="p193"></a> **[PDF p.193](../page-images/p0193.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Clean the serrations on shaft ends and inside the coupling. Replace
corroded or battered parts. Do not repair steering parts by welding or
bending or substitute ordinary bolts for the **special coupling bolts**.
Feed the gear from below the front of the car; align shaft mark with coupling
sawcut and install gear-to-frame bolts loosely. Fit the special coupling
bolts, nuts, and lock washers, then tighten coupling bolts to
**22–27 ft. lb.** Tighten the frame bolts. Recheck shaft/coupling marks and
adjust the mast jacket if needed.

## Mast jacket and steering linkage carried forward

<a id="p193-mast"></a> **[PDF p.193](../page-images/p0193.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove steering wheel and the spring and seat immediately beneath it. Unplug
the direction-signal switch and horn-wire connectors.

<a id="p194"></a> **[PDF p.194](../page-images/p0194.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Disconnect the parking-brake return spring and cable clevis; thread cable
and ball past the pulley. If needed, remove the pulley's cotter pin and
wheel. Remove the instrument-panel/bracing bolts and toe-pan mounting bolts,
preserving any shim at the right-hand panel attachment. Slide the jacket
carefully off the shaft. Pry out and replace the upper bearing/horn-wire
assembly if damaged; pry out and cement in a new lower seal if needed.
Refit triangular gasket, jacket, bearing and lower seal over shaft, restore
all saved shims and fasteners, center jacket on shaft, and tighten bolts.

### Tie rods

<a id="p194-tie"></a> **[PDF p.194](../page-images/p0194.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Each side has a three-piece tie rod: a center rod and two threaded end
assemblies, with opposite-hand threads for adjustment. Replace an end when
its stud has excessive vertical movement, lost motion, or end play. For
removal, remove cotter pins and castellated nuts; free each ball stud from
its arm or relay-rod boss using the base manual's backed-up striking
method. Loosen the clamps and unscrew the end assemblies if needed.

<a id="p195"></a> **[PDF p.195](../page-images/p0195.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Thread replacement ends equally into the center rod. Clean stud and nut
threads, fit neoprene seals, place studs in steering arms and relay rod,
tighten nuts securely, and install cotter pins. Lubricate per the 1963
schedule. Set [front toe](03-suspension.md#alignment-preparation-and-rear-toe-in)
with each joint centered in its travel to prevent binding. Position outer
clamp bolts at right angles to the sleeve slots. Inner clamp flanges face
forward and may rotate down as needed to clear the crossmember; set their
bolts as near right angles to the slots as clearance allows.

### Relay rod and idler

<a id="p195-relay"></a> **[PDF p.195](../page-images/p0195.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Disconnect the tie-rod inner ends from the relay rod. Remove cotter pins
and nuts at the pitman- and idler-arm connections and free the rod. Clean
off grease and dirt and inspect for damage or wear. For its pitman-end
bushing, drive out the pivot bolt with a soft hammer, then press out the
rubber and sleeve using a suitable socket.

<a id="p196"></a> **[PDF p.196](../page-images/p0196.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Support the relay rod on a deep socket and press in its new bushing with
**J-8357**. Reconnect pitman and idler arms in the alignment shown by the
source's **Figure 4-31**; hold each bolt's hex head while tightening so its
rubber bushing is not twisted. Apply the same care at the idler bracket.
Then reconnect tie rods, center the gear, and check front toe. For idler
removal, sealing, replacement, and attaching torques, use the
[1963 idler procedure above](#1963-idler-arm-and-linkage).

See the [factory linkage alignment drawing](../diagrams/p0196-steering-linkage-alignment.png).

## Troubleshooting and tools

<a id="p208"></a> **[PDF p.208](../page-images/p0208.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-25 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base trouble chart calls for checking these likely causes before
replacing parts. Apply the 1963 lubrication intervals and steering-gear
pull-scale settings in this chapter when it refers to those operations.

| Symptom | Factory checks and remedies |
|---|---|
| Hard steering | Check steering and front-joint lubrication, turn-signal housing clearance, tire inflation, gear preload, shaft/mast-jacket interference, and front alignment. Lubricate, adjust, or replace damaged parts as indicated. |
| Loose steering | Check gear preload, wheel bearings, knuckle ball joints, sector-shaft bushings, linkage wear, and gear-to-body attachment. Adjust or replace the fault. |
| Shimmy | Balance front wheels; check wheel bearings, wheel nuts, front brakes, and tie-rod ends. Correct the identified defect. |
| Road wander | Check tire inflation, gear preload, wheel bearings, and worn tie-rod ends. Correct the identified defect. |

<a id="p209"></a> **[PDF p.209](../page-images/p0209.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=4-26 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The illustrated factory tools include **J-6627** pitman-arm puller,
**J-5178** steering-wheel pull scale, **J-2927A** steering-wheel puller,
**J-8366-1/J-8366-2** sector-shaft bushing press tools, and **J-8357**
relay-rod bushing installer. The figure also labels worm-bearing cup
removal/installation tools **J-5822/J-5755**. Inspect the
[original tool illustration](../page-images/p0209.png) before selecting a
tool. Steering dimensions and torques elsewhere in the base tool and
specification pages do not supersede the 1963 supplement.

## Unresolved 1963 values

  changed angles 1962 while its table gives the same angles without a year

  table cannot yet be read reliably from the available scan; see
