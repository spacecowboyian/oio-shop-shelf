# Rear Axle — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1962–63 supplement retains the base differential service except for
its revised axle bearing, service-gear-set marking rule, and optional
Positraction unit. This chapter applies to **1963 passenger cars** only;
Corvair 95 procedures and 1962-only specifications are excluded. Source
files, hashes and original page mapping are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json).

## Differential arrangement and service access carried forward

<a id="p321"></a> **[PDF p.321](../page-images/p0321.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The passenger rear axle has a straddle-mounted hypoid differential carrier
rigidly attached to the engine, with independently suspended axle shafts
and universal joints. The lower control arms transmit drive thrust while
the outer bearings and crossmember bushings take lateral load. The base
manual separates **500/700/900 passenger** service from Corvair 95 and
Greenbrier commercial service.

<a id="p322"></a> **[PDF p.322](../page-images/p0322.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

A hollow drive pinion carries the engine output shaft through the
differential to the transmission. The ring gear bolts to a case supported
by side tapered-roller bearings; splined side gears receive the universal-
joint yokes. Manual-transmission and Powerglide differential carriers
have different pinion lengths, seals, and clutch/converter support. Manual
transmission and rear axle share lubricant across their housings but have
**separate filler openings**. Powerglide fluid and axle lubricant remain
separate behind their seals. For 1963 ratio and capacity selection, use
the **1963** rows in Section 12; the ratio examples on this base page
describe 1961 equipment and are not assigned to a 1963 car.

Check rear-axle lubricant level and change interval using Chapter 2's
1963-applicable schedule. Do not use lubricant containing **active sulphur**
in the Corvair axle. Inspect and lubricate universal joints periodically,
and check wheel-fastener tightness.

<a id="p323"></a> **[PDF p.323](../page-images/p0323.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory manual-transmission sectional view identifies pinion front
and rear bearings, differential side bearings and adjusting sleeves,
ring gear, side gears, universal joints, axle shafts, clutch-release
bearing shaft, speedometer gears, and seals. Use the drawing to keep
manual-transmission seal locations distinct from Powerglide parts.

<a id="p324"></a> **[PDF p.324](../page-images/p0324.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The separate Powerglide sectional view identifies its stator and converter
seals, pinion-shaft seals, governor gears, differential side-bearing sleeves,
and carrier filler/drain openings. Match the seal and bearing procedure
to transmission type.

<a id="p325"></a> **[PDF p.325](../page-images/p0325.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

With the carrier **still in the car**, the axle shaft/bearing/universal
joint assembly, side-bearing sleeve seal and speedometer driven gear can
be serviced. Internal differential and pinion operations require removing
the complete power train and separating the carrier. For axle-shaft
removal, take off wheel and drum, remove the four bearing-retainer nuts
through the axle-flange access opening, move the backing plate enough to
free its retainer, and draw the axle outward until the U-joint splines
clear the side gear. Remove the U-joint/yoke fasteners.

<a id="p326"></a> **[PDF p.326](../page-images/p0326.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Pull the U-joint yoke with J-5504 and withdraw the shaft through the lower
control arm. The **base bearing replacement method is superseded** by the
1962–63 supplement's correct 1963 bearing procedure below. For universal-
joint overhaul, remove bearing lock rings, support the yoke and drive
bearings out using a soft drift. Clean and inspect each bearing and
trunnion; repack with high-melting-point wheel-bearing grease, press the
bearings onto trunnion hubs, fit the lock rings, and seat bearings against
the rings.

<a id="p327"></a> **[PDF p.327](../page-images/p0327.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Reinstall the axle through the control arm, fit the U-joint yoke on its
splines and secure it with the original fasteners. Attach the U-joint with
its U-bolts, align the bearing retainer to the backing-plate studs, and
guide the inner splines through the side-sleeve seal into the side gear.
Tighten the four retainer nuts evenly and restore drum and wheel. The
factory notes the **left U-joint shaft is approximately one inch longer**
than the right; keep the shafts on their respective sides.

## Seals, carrier removal, and pinion setup

<a id="p328"></a> **[PDF p.328](../page-images/p0328.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For an in-car side-bearing sleeve seal, remove the U-joint, pry out the
old seal, and drive the new seal flush with its **lips inward**. The
speedometer driven-gear assembly comes out after disconnecting its cable
and removing the locking-tab bolt. For internal work, remove the power
train as in Section 6, drain and fixture the carrier, then remove driven
gear, cover/gasket, both side-bearing adjusting sleeves and pinion
adjusting sleeve. Lift the pinion/bearings through the cover opening;
shift and turn the differential case to remove it through the same opening.

<a id="p329"></a> **[PDF p.329](../page-images/p0329.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect tapered rollers/cups for damage, pinion and side-gear splines for
wear, ring/pinion teeth for scoring or cracks, pinion shaft and carrier
for damage, and Powerglide converter/stator seals where applicable. A
uniform frosted wear pattern can be normal on preloaded tapered bearings;
do not condemn them for that alone. If bearings or ring/pinion are
replaced, re-establish pinion mounting distance. Press old bearings from
the pinion with J-8331/J-358-1 and retain the location of the existing
selective shim(s). Seat the rear bearing in the carrier for depth gauging.

<a id="p330"></a> **[PDF p.330](../page-images/p0330.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The exploded factory drawing identifies carrier, manual/automatic pinion
variants, bearing sleeves, seals, ring gear and differential components.
Keep the correct transmission-specific pinion and seal components together
during rebuilding.

<a id="p331"></a> **[PDF p.331](../page-images/p0331.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Center gauge plate J-6266-5 accurately over the seated rear-bearing bore
with adapter J-6266-25, then seat gauge cylinder J-6266-01 across the side
bearing bores in its adapters. Center its movable plunger against the gauge
plate and measure that plunger with a micrometer. **A cocked bearing or
off-center gauge invalidates the shim measurement.** For a pinion bearing
or gear-set replacement, select shim thickness from the chart on the next
page, using the actual pinion marking when present. For an unmarked
post-November-1961 service gear set, the supplement directs use of the
**14 column**.

Install selected shims against the rear face of the pinion gear and press

<a id="p333"></a> **[PDF p.333](../page-images/p0333.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Drive an old pinion-front bearing race from its adjusting sleeve, then
seat the new race with J-7137/J-7079-2. On Powerglide, remove and replace
the adjacent oil seal with J-8340. Side-bearing sleeve seals likewise
seat with lips inward. For a side-bearing **race** replacement, the base
procedure starts by marking two opposite drilling points on the sleeve;
continue with the following page's extraction and repair steps.

## Differential case, seals, and bearing service

<a id="p334"></a> **[PDF p.334](../page-images/p0334.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-14 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For a side-bearing race, drive the old race out through the drilled sleeve
holes with a small punch; drive the replacement flush with J-8148 or a
flat plate. Seal both drill holes with lead balls at least **.225 in.**
diameter as the factory shows. Replace a side bearing **with its race as a
set**, pulling the old bearing with J-7112/J-8107-2. For standard
differential disassembly, remove the six ring-gear/cover bolts, cover,
side gear and thrust washer. Tap the ring gear off evenly with a soft
hammer if it needs replacement. The optional Positraction case uses the
different clutch-pack procedure below.

<a id="p335"></a> **[PDF p.335](../page-images/p0335.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-15 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the **standard differential**, drive out the roll pin, then withdraw
the pinion-gear shaft, both pinions and their thrust washers. Inspect case
and cover for cracks/flatness, side-gear splines and teeth, pinion teeth
and shaft fit, and all thrust washers. Fit new side bearings with J-8359
as needed. Reassemble the side gear and washer, two pinions and washers,
pinion shaft and its retaining pin. Keep case, cover and ring gear aligned
by their factory marks.

<a id="p336"></a> **[PDF p.336](../page-images/p0336.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-16 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Place the second side gear and thrust washer, align cover and case punch
marks, use temporary guide pins to position the ring gear, then install
all six ring-gear bolts/lockwashers. Tighten them alternately to
**40–60 ft. lb.** On a manual-transmission carrier, remove the split ring
and old clutch-release-bearing shaft seal; fit the new seal with its
**open side inward**, drive it to its seat, and restore the split ring.

<a id="p337"></a> **[PDF p.337](../page-images/p0337.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-17 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the manual carrier, press out the clutch-release-bearing shaft and
pinion rear-bearing race together. Replace the shaft's inner seal and
outer seal ring, lubricate the ring, support the carrier at the shaft
boss, and press the shaft/race back until the race sits flush at the
inner surface. For **Powerglide only**, J-8340 installs the pinion-front
seal to its tool stop and the converter-hub seal flush. Coat the seals'
outer surfaces with nonhardening sealer before installation.

<a id="p338"></a> **[PDF p.338](../page-images/p0338.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-18 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The Powerglide stator-shaft pinion-rear seal is installed with J-8448-1
until it bottoms. For a worn pinion-shaft inner bushing, remove it without
gouging the bore and press the replacement to J-8333's built-in stop.
Press the stator shaft and rear-bearing cup from the carrier together
for replacement as required.

<a id="p339"></a> **[PDF p.339](../page-images/p0339.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-19 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

On a Powerglide carrier, fit a new outer stator-shaft seal ring, align its
notch with the carrier drain-back boss, support the carrier **at the stator
boss only**, and press the shaft and rear-bearing race in together. To
reassemble either carrier, install the side-bearing cones on the
differential, position the case in the carrier, engage the pinion through
the cover opening, and loosely install side and pinion adjusting sleeves
with fresh O-rings. Protect Powerglide seal lips during pinion insertion.
Bring sleeves just into bearing/race contact without preload, leaving
enough backlash for smooth rotation before adjustment.

## Ring-and-pinion bearing preload and backlash

<a id="p340"></a> **[PDF p.340](../page-images/p0340.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-20 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Oil bearings before adjustment. Turn the **right** side sleeve while
rocking the case until ring/pinion backlash reaches zero. Mark that point,
back the sleeve off three or four notches to release O-ring wind-up, then
retighten to **one notch loose** from the zero-backlash mark. Tighten the
**left** side sleeve until lateral play is gone; mark it, back off three
or four notches, then retighten to the mark plus **two to three notches**
for side-bearing preload, aligning a lock-tab notch. Fit its lock tab.

Seat the pinion bearings with their sleeve, back off to remove pinion
preload, and measure the *existing* side-bearing turning torque at the
pinion with J-8362 and an inch-pound wrench. Increase that reading by
**4–6 in. lb. with used pinion bearings**, or **9–11 in. lb. with new
pinion bearings**. Record the starting reading rather than substituting
the example number printed in the base manual. Lock the pinion sleeve
and refit the speedometer driven gear.

| Added pinion-bearing rotating torque over measured side-bearing drag | Factory increment |
|---|---|
| Used pinion bearings | 4–6 in. lb. |
| New pinion bearings | 9–11 in. lb. |

<a id="p341"></a> **[PDF p.341](../page-images/p0341.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-21 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Measure backlash with a dial indicator against the back of a ring-gear
tooth while rocking the gear. The factory range is **.003–.010 in.**,
with **.005–.008 in. preferred**. Move the case **left/toward the pinion**
to reduce backlash, right/away to increase it. Move left and right side
adjusting sleeves by the **same number of notches** in opposite directions
to preserve side-bearing preload; one notch changes backlash about
**.003 in.** After adjustment, clean the teeth and apply a thin, even
marking-compound/oil film to the ring gear for a contact-pattern check.

| Ring-gear-to-pinion backlash | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Permitted range | .003–.010 in. |
| Preferred range | .005–.008 in. |

<a id="p342"></a> **[PDF p.342](../page-images/p0342.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-22 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Hold the pinion against rotation and rock the ring gear under load to
develop a visible marking-compound tooth-contact pattern. Compare both
drive and coast sides with Figure 6C-54. A pattern centered between
**heel/toe** and **face/flank** is desirable. Toe contact calls for moving
the ring gear away from the pinion (increase backlash); heel contact
calls for moving it toward the pinion (decrease backlash). High **face**
contact calls for a thicker pinion-depth shim, initially .003 in. thicker;
low **flank** contact calls for a thinner shim, initially .003 in. thinner.
After each shim or sleeve change, recheck backlash and pattern; maintain
side-bearing preload. Clean off marking compound, oil gears and bearings,
and refit the carrier cover with a new gasket. The base page's **three-pint
fill** is a 1961 value and is superseded by the explicit 1963 capacity
below. See the [factory contact-pattern chart](../diagrams/p0342-gear-contact-patterns.png).

<a id="p343"></a> **[PDF p.343](../page-images/p0343.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-23 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For excessive drive-line backlash, inspect wheel fasteners, universal
joints, pinion/transmission splines, ring/pinion adjustment and side gears.
A clunk while weight shifts side-to-side can indicate axle-shaft end play
and bearing/retainer wear. Drive or coast axle noise points to ring/pinion
adjustment, pinion bearings or end play; noise in both conditions adds
side bearings, axle bearings, U-joints and damaged gear teeth to the check.
For lubricant leaks, inspect side-bearing sleeve seals/O-rings and carrier
cover gasket. Identify the sound's source before adjusting the gear set.

<a id="p344"></a> **[PDF p.344](../page-images/p0344.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-24 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

On a smooth road, compare the sound under drive, float and coast and at
different speeds. Gear tooth noise tends to vary in pitch over a narrow
speed band; rough bearing noise is more constant. A wheel bearing may
change noise during left/right loading in a turn, while differential side-
bearing noise generally does not. Check tires, engine, transmission and
U-joints before assigning a noise to the rear axle. The base specifications
line defers to Section 12; **1963** Section 12 governs dimensions,
capacities and ratio availability.

<a id="p345"></a> **[PDF p.345](../page-images/p0345.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6C-25 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

adjusting-sleeve wrenches, depth gauge, bearing race drivers/pullers,

following base page 6C-26 begins **Corvair 95 commercial** service and is
excluded.

## 1963 axle shaft bearing replacement

<a id="p669"></a> **[PDF p.669](../page-images/p0669.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The **1962 and 1963 passenger axle bearings do not interchange**; select
the correct 1963 bearing before pressing one onto a shaft. Support the axle
on a press bed with J-8619-1 beneath its puller ring, the plate's flat lip
against that ring. Press off the oil deflector, bearing, shield and ring.
The supplement's first bearing illustration depicts the **1962 design**
for comparison; it does not authorize that bearing for a 1963 axle.
Chevrolet Service News, August 1961, [p. 1](https://corvanatics.org/files/maintenance-repair/repair-manuals-bulletins/chevy-service-news/CSN-33-8.pdf#page=1),
identifies **J-8619-1** in its passenger-car bearing-removal procedure and
figure, and says it supersedes the reworked J-5741. The later supplement's
step 1 prints **J-8916-1**, contrary to its own note, figure, and special-tools
list. That isolated step is a factory text conflict; use the corroborated
J-8619-1 designation when identifying the press plate.

Press a **new** puller ring and correct 1963 bearing onto the axle with
J-8619-1 or J-5741, using the **old ring against the bearing inner race**
as the pressing contact. If using J-5741 as the removal plate, the factory
describes a modified lip; consult Figure 6C-3 before modifying a tool.
Pack the cavity between bearing race and shield with high-melting-point
wheel-bearing grease, press the shield on with suitable tubing, bend its
tangs over, and fit the oil deflector. The separate **Corvair 95** bearing
procedure on this page does not apply to a passenger axle.

## Pinion service gear sets and Positraction arrangement

<a id="p671"></a> **[PDF p.671](../page-images/p0671.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-3 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The base pinion-depth shim procedure remains applicable. Chevrolet stopped
marking some service ring-and-pinion gear sets after November 1961. Those
unmarked sets are cut to nominal **“14”** range; use the **14 column** of
the base Corvair pinion-depth shim chart for initial shim selection, then
verify the final contact pattern and backlash under the base procedure.

Optional passenger-car **Positraction** has a multiple-plate clutch pack in
the right side of its differential case. A Belleville plate/disc pair
provides constant preload in addition to separating force at the side
gears. The unit still differentiates during ordinary cornering.

<a id="p672"></a> **[PDF p.672](../page-images/p0672.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-4 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

If one-wheel slip suggests insufficient Positraction preload, raise **both**
rear wheels, remove one wheel, connect J-8617 and adapter J-2619-4 to its
studs, increase brake running clearance, and have an assistant hold the
opposite wheel. With the manual transmission in neutral, rotate the axle
using a torque wrench. The factory requires **at least 50 ft. lb.** to
rotate. To account for brake drag, alternatively measure torque with the
other wheel free and subtract that reading from the held-wheel reading.
If preload remains below limit, remove and inspect the clutch pack.

| Optional Positraction preload check | Factory minimum |
|---|---|
| Axle rotation, opposite wheel held, brake drag accounted for | 50 ft. lb. |

Remove the differential from its carrier using the base procedure. The
**right** side-bearing adjusting sleeve and right axle-shaft deflector
have shallower sections than standard parts to clear the Positraction unit;
keep those parts identified. See the [factory Positraction cross-section
and exploded view](../diagrams/p0672-positraction.png).

## Positraction clutch-pack service

<a id="p673"></a> **[PDF p.673](../page-images/p0673.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Pull both differential side bearings with J-7112/J-8107-2; remove the six
ring-gear bolts if taking off the gear. **Mark case and cover alignment**
before separation. Two factory temporary flat-head screws may be present
under the ring gear; remove them before separating the halves. Take out the
right side gear, preload spacer and clutch pack. Inspect plates, discs,
spacer, side-gear thrust faces and the case's spacer seat for heat damage,
scoring, cracks or wear. Chevrolet services the **clutch pack as an
assembly**, not as individual friction pieces.

Lightly oil the replacement pack and install, in order: **Belleville
clutch plate; Belleville clutch disc; flat clutch plate; flat clutch disc;
flat clutch plate; preload spacer with chamfer up**. Engage the side-gear
splines, align the case/cover marks, draw the halves together using the six
ring-gear bolts and guide pins, then tighten those bolts to **40–60 ft.
lb.** The temporary factory flat-head screws need not be reinstalled.
See the [clutch-pack sequence illustration](../diagrams/p0673-positraction-clutch-pack.png).

<a id="p674"></a> **[PDF p.674](../page-images/p0674.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Install new side-bearing assemblies if needed. For a bench preload check,
protect the assembled case in a vise, engage a scrap axle yoke in one side
gear, pass a one-inch bar through it, and apply a pull scale **12 in. from
the differential centerline** as Figure 6C-15 shows. Rotation torque must
reach **50 ft. lb. minimum**. Reinstall the unit in its carrier using the
base tooth-contact, bearing-preload and backlash adjustments. Fill the
carrier to the filler-opening level with the specified rear-axle lubricant.

<a id="p675"></a> **[PDF p.675](../page-images/p0675.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6C-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The supplement's tool plate identifies the axle-bearing press plate,
J-8617 axle-shaft puller, J-2619-4 adapter and torque wrench. Its
rear-axle specification note directs the reader to **Section 12**; use
the 1963 capacity and gear-ratio rows there rather than 1962 values.
