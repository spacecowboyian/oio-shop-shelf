# Dimensions and Gauges — Shop Reference for the 1963 Corvair

These are the general conversion and gauge tables printed with the 1961
factory manual. They carry forward as shop references when servicing a 1963
passenger car. They are **not** vehicle dimensions, drill instructions, or
model-specific clearance specifications. Select a drill or wire only from
the applicable procedure and specification elsewhere in this manual.

The three original pages are unnumbered. Each page link points to the scan,
and the [source-page map](../data/source-page-map.jsonl) identifies its
original PDF, physical page, URL, and SHA-256 hash.

## Gauge thicknesses

<a id="p531"></a> **[PDF p.531](../page-images/p0531.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=unnumbered years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger variant=shop-reference -->

The source labels these as **U.S. Standard Gage** approximate thickness
and **American Wire or B & S Gage** thickness. The standards differ;
use the column named by the relevant procedure. A dash means the source
prints no value. The source title has an asterisk after U.S. Standard
Gage, but no corresponding footnote is visible on the supplied page.
The independent U.S. Bureau of Standards [*Circular 391*, p. 5,
Table 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/circ/nbscircular391.pdf#page=9)
matches the Chevrolet column's inch values to its **steel and
open-hearth iron approximate thickness** column. This confirms the
column's numerical basis, but does not reveal what Chevrolet's missing
asterisk was intended to say. Chevrolet's later
[1974 Passenger Car and Light Truck Overhaul Manual, “Gages” appendix](https://www.73-87chevytrucks.com/techinfo/7387CKMans/Unit_Repair/ST_333_74_1974_Chevrolet_Passenger_Car_and_Light_Truck_Overhaul_Manual.pdf#page=545)
reprints the same table and asterisk with no footnote on that page or the
following page. That supports a long-standing omitted note, but cannot
recover its wording. Neither table should be treated as a
material tolerance for a specific body panel.
