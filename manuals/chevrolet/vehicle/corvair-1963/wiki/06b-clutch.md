# Clutch — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1963 supplement retains the base clutch service procedures and changes
the pedal mounting support. It shows the clutch-pedal over-center assist
spring **eliminated** on late 1961 and 1962 cars; do not install the early
spring on a 1963 pedal. The turbocharged Monza Spyder requires its special
pressure plate. This chapter covers passenger-car manual-transmission
clutches; Corvair 95 commercial linkage and pedal procedures are excluded.
Original files, hashes and page mapping are in the [source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json).

## Clutch arrangement and adjustment

<a id="p307"></a> **[PDF p.307](../page-images/p0307.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The manually shifted passenger car uses a single-plate diaphragm-spring
clutch at the flywheel, inside the flywheel housing. Its driven disc is
solid mounted; torsional compliance is in the transmission input shaft.
The release fork **pulls** the release bearing toward the diaphragm fingers.
A cable, three pulleys, cross-shaft and fork pull rod connect the pedal to
the fork. The engine/transaxle assembly is removed as a unit for clutch
service; see the factory power-train procedure in Section 6.

<a id="p308"></a> **[PDF p.308](../page-images/p0308.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For linkage adjustment, first verify that the cable is anchored at the
pedal and seated in all pulleys. Pedal travel before the release bearing
touches the diaphragm must be **at least ¾ in.** Disconnect the fork pull
rod from the outboard cross-shaft lever. Attach the return spring to its
lower hole and adjust the cable clevis until the outboard lever has
**⅛–⅜ in. clearance** to the front engine mount, as Figure 6B-2 shows.
Pull the fork rod by hand until bearing-to-finger slack is gone; align its
swivel with the lever's upper hole, back the swivel off **three full turns**,
and clip it to the lever. Recheck pedal travel and release.

| Passenger clutch linkage check | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Pedal travel before release-bearing contact | At least ¾ in. |
| Cross-shaft outboard lever to front engine mount | ⅛–⅜ in. |
| Fork-rod swivel after aligning with lever hole | Back off three turns |

The [factory linkage-adjustment figures](../diagrams/p0308-clutch-linkage-adjustment.png)
identify the measured gap and the two swivels.

## Clutch removal, inspection, and reassembly

<a id="p309"></a> **[PDF p.309](../page-images/p0309.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After separating the engine and axle/transmission assembly, take the fork
off its ball stud and slide the release bearing off the axle-housing sleeve.
Support the clutch with pilot tool J-5824. Loosen the **six** cover bolts
uniformly, one turn each pass, until diaphragm load is released; then lift
off the cover and disc. Mark pressure plate and cover positions before
removing the three drive-strap bolts and retracting springs. The diaphragm,
pivot rings and cover are riveted together and replaced as an assembly if
damaged. Wash metal parts, **not the permanently lubricated release
bearing**. Inspect flywheel and pressure-plate faces for scoring or warp,
drive straps for looseness, bearing for roughness/free sliding, disc for
oil or worn facings/rivets, and hub/input-shaft splines for free motion.

<a id="p310"></a> **[PDF p.310](../page-images/p0310.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect the oil-impregnated crankshaft pilot bushing when the clutch is
removed. Pull a damaged one with J-1448; drive a new one with J-1522,
placing its bore radius against the tool shoulder. Reassemble the pressure
plate to the cover in the marked orientation, fit retracting springs and
drive straps, and tighten strap bolts to **15–20 ft. lb.** Position the
driven disc with its **cushion springs toward the flywheel**. Support the
cover with pilot J-5824, install alternate cover bolts first, draw them
down evenly, install the other three, and remove the pilot. The base page's
pedal exploded view depicts the superseded assist spring; use the revised
1963 pedal support and spring deletion below.

<a id="p311"></a> **[PDF p.311](../page-images/p0311.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Apply a **small** amount of high-melting grease to the fork ball seat and
graphite grease to the release-bearing collar recess/fork groove. Seat the
fork fully on its ball stud and refit the bearing to the axle housing.
Rejoin engine and transaxle and install the power train as in Section 6.
Figure 6B-9 identifies cable, cross-shaft, fork rod, seals, spring and
ball-stud hardware. The following early pedal-assist-spring removal and
installation text does **not** apply to the 1963 pedal. For pedal service,
remove the snap ring from its shaft and unbolt the bumper and shaft-support
brackets; inspect the nylon sleeve bushings. The supplement's Figure 6B-2
governs the revised master-cylinder/pedal support configuration.

<a id="p312"></a> **[PDF p.312](../page-images/p0312.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For passenger cable/pulley replacement, unhook the cross-shaft return
spring, fork-rod swivel and cable clevis, open the body-tunnel access
covers, remove the clutch pulley and parking-brake/clutch pulley support,
then unhook cable at pedal. Refit the cable through every pulley and adjust
the linkage as above. For cross-shaft removal, disconnect spring, fork rod
and cable clevis; remove its body-bracket hardware and ball-stud seat,
then lift it off the front-engine-mount ball stud. Lubricate the ball studs,
reassemble seats/retainers, position the body-bracket ball stud at the
**front end of its slot**, connect spring and cable, and readjust. The
early assist-spring installation step in the base pedal procedure is
superseded for 1963.

## Troubleshooting and special tools

<a id="p317"></a> **[PDF p.317](../page-images/p0317.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For clutch slip, check linkage adjustment, disc oil/wear or separated
facing, splines, flywheel/plate flatness, and diaphragm condition. For
grabbing, check facings, splines, engine mounts and clutch faces. For
rattle/noise, inspect retracting springs, fork seating and release bearing.
For high pedal effort or poor return, inspect pulley bearings, cable route
and pivot points before changing the clutch assembly. For inadequate
release on **500/700/900 passenger models**, the factory calls for at
least **.825 in. fork-rod motion** during **5.6 in. full pedal travel**;
less travel points to cable, pulley or support faults. The alternative
R-series commercial instruction on this page is excluded.

| Passenger release diagnostic | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Minimum clutch-fork pull-rod motion | .825 in. |
| Full clutch-pedal travel used for this test | 5.6 in. |

<a id="p318"></a> **[PDF p.318](../page-images/p0318.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6B-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The special-tools plate identifies J-1522 pilot-bearing driver, J-5824
clutch-pilot support and J-1448 pilot-bearing puller. The source PDF repeats
the same troubleshooting and special-tools pages at **PDF pp.319–320**;
those duplicate scans add no second procedure or specification.

## 1963 pedal and high-performance clutch changes

<a id="p668"></a> **[PDF p.668](../page-images/p0668.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6B-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The over-center pedal spring was deleted before 1963. The brake-master-
cylinder change also revised the clutch/brake pedal support; use the
supplement's Figure 6B-2 for bracket recognition and placement. The
supplement explicitly carries forward the remaining clutch service and
adjustment procedures.

The **turbocharged Monza Spyder** requires the special pearlitic malleable
or nodular-iron pressure plate with **six large cast lugs** around its
outside. Its complete assembly may also be used with the Super Turbo-Air
engine. Do not substitute the ordinary plate on a Spyder. See the
[factory high-performance clutch illustration](../diagrams/p0668-spyder-clutch.png).

## 1963 passenger clutch specifications

<a id="p630"></a> **[PDF p.630](../page-images/p0630.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=12-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1963 specifications distinguish the regular passenger clutch from
the high-performance RPO 649/651 application. The source also includes
1200-series commercial data, omitted here.

| 1963 passenger clutch application | Disc diameter |
|---|---|
| 500/700/900 regular | 9⅛ in. |
| RPO 649/651 high performance, including Spyder | 8 in. |

The factory's *total pressure* entries use the printed unit “ft. lbs.”;
because that is ambiguous for diaphragm load, those values are withheld
from the 1963 specification lookup pending confirmation from another
factory source.
<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: Supplement Section 12 p. 12-7 labels clutch diaphragm total pressure 900–1050 and 1500 as "ft. lbs.". Confirm intended unit before indexing or applying these pressure figures; do not silently change to pounds-force. -->

The release bearing is a sealed ball bearing and the crankshaft pilot is
an oil-impregnated bushing. The clutch-housing pilot runout limit is
**.015 in. TIR**; flywheel face runout is **.020 in. TIR**, and outside-
diameter runout is **.010 in. TIR**. The supplement repeats the passenger
cross-shaft lever gap of **⅛–⅜ in.**
