# Automatic Transmission — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1963 passenger Powerglide uses the base shop-manual service procedures
with the 1962–63 supplement's planet-carrier, front-pump test, band-access
and gasket corrections. Original files, hashes and combined PDF page
numbers are in the [source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json).

specifications did not change in 1962–63** and refers back to the 1961

the 1963 passenger reference, subject to the source conflicts flagged

## Operation, fluid and controls

<a id="p375"></a> **[PDF p.375](../page-images/p0375.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Powerglide joins a two-speed planetary transmission and air-cooled torque
converter to the differential carrier. The converter sits at the engine;
the pump and turbine shafts pass through the carrier to the transmission.
The selector has **Reverse, Neutral, Drive and Low**, with no Park detent.
For the 1963 passenger instrument-panel selector, use **L-D-N-R from top to
bottom**. The [1963 Chevrolet *Finger Tip Facts*, printed p. Corvair-31](https://northern-california-corvairs.com/63FTF.pdf#page=31)
and the 1963 GM kit's Power Trains p. 16 and AMA transmission chart give
L-D-N-R. The [1963 owner's guide, printed p. 6](https://corvanatics.org/files/business-office/OwnMan-OwnPP/63OM-E3.pdf#page=5)
shows D, N, and R descending on the dashboard quadrant. The base Section 6E
p. 6E-1 R-N-D-L description is a conflicting base-manual statement, not the
1963 selector layout.
<!-- RESOLVED SOURCE CONFLICT: The 1963 AMA selector-pattern chart and Chevrolet Finger Tip Facts give L-D-N-R. The 1963 owner-guide dashboard illustration shows D, N, R descending. Base 6E-1 instead prints R-N-D-L. -->
The manual-low inhibitor prevents an unsafe downshift at high road speed.
The opening page also lists changes already built into the 1961 unit;
do not treat those as additional 1963 modifications. The inherited
ratios are listed above; the diagnostic shift-point chart is on p.401.

<a id="p376"></a> **[PDF p.376](../page-images/p0376.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The [factory Powerglide cross-section](../diagrams/p0376-powerglide-cross-section.png)
maps the converter, front pump,
clutch drum, low band, planet carrier, rear pump/reverse piston, valve
body and low servo. The labeled component key continues on the next page.

<a id="p377"></a> **[PDF p.377](../page-images/p0377.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The service page describes automatic transmission fluid **Type A** with
an **AQ-ATF** mark; the inherited Section 12 specification above adds
**suffix A**. Check level with the fluid at operating temperature,
engine idling and selector in **Neutral**. Add fluid near the ADD mark;
FULL to ADD represents **one pint**. Do not overfill: the planetary gear
unit can churn and aerate excess fluid. Trace repeated losses at the
front pump, pan, filler tube, governor, carrier joint and modulator;
a ruptured modulator diaphragm can draw transmission fluid into the
engine. No periodic fluid drain is prescribed by this source. For a
repair refill, open the filler-tube nut at the pan to drain, reinstall,
add **four pints**, idle in Neutral for **3–5 minutes**, then add only to
FULL; the source estimates approximately **six pints** when the welded
converter was not drained. The dated 1963 GM specification above gives an
**approximately 13-pint dry capacity**; final
level still follows the dipstick.

<a id="p378"></a> **[PDF p.378](../page-images/p0378.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check cable adjustment at idle by moving Neutral slowly toward Reverse:
the reverse clutch should apply between the selector tooth peak and the
full Reverse detent. Repeat toward Drive; the low band should apply
between its tooth peak and Drive detent. If either fails, drain/remove
the pan, put the cabin selector in Drive, insert factory gauge **J-8365**
in the manual-valve bore with tab up against the forward valve-body
port, and move the valve levers forward. Correct adjustment holds the
gauge level without support. Loosen the lock screw to reset, recheck,
then replace pan and refill. After selector/cable service, adjust the
neutral-safety switch so cranking occurs **only in Neutral**. Follow
Section 9 for throttle-valve linkage adjustment.

<a id="p379"></a> **[PDF p.379](../page-images/p0379.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The supplement deletes the base page's parcel-compartment access to the
low-band screw and says **no periodic low-band adjustment** is recommended.
If band service requires adjustment with the transmission accessible,
the base setting is **40 ±5 in-lb**, then back off **exactly four full
turns** and hold the screw while tightening its locknut. The base
service-location guide groups range selector/cable, modulator, governor,
valve body and low servo as in-car work; most internal units require
power-train removal. To remove the dash selector, disconnect cable eye
and sheath, remove instrument cluster, unplug neutral-safety switch and
quadrant light, and remove the two selector screws.

<a id="p380"></a> **[PDF p.380](../page-images/p0380.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The range selector is serviced as an assembly. Reinstall it on the
instrument cluster with the sheath flat flush in its bracket; secure
the cable eye and retainer, then check both shift-linkage and neutral-
safety-switch adjustments. For cable removal, uncover the tunnel,
release its toe-pan/tunnel/kick-up clips and rear grommet plate, detach
throttle rods, rotate the transmission TV lever fully counterclockwise
to release the cable ball from the manual-valve lever, and withdraw the
cable. Replace cable and captive grommets together. On installation,
route the cable **under the parking-brake cable and over the brake pipe**
to keep the parking-brake cable from rubbing through it.

## Modulator, governor, valve body and low servo

<a id="p381"></a> **[PDF p.381](../page-images/p0381.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Finish routing the cable in its protected clips and rear grommet plate.
Lubricate its O-ring, attach the cable ball with the TV lever fully
counterclockwise, seat the seal and confirm that the TV lever's notched-
arm hole lies **below the pan rail**. Then check shift linkage. The vacuum
modulator is serviced from below at the right of the case: detach its
vacuum hose, unscrew it and remove the valve. Inspect valve for burrs and
the modulator for vacuum leakage; a failed diaphragm can cause smoky
exhaust and repeated transmission-fluid loss. Replace a leaking
modulator as an assembly.

<a id="p382"></a> **[PDF p.382](../page-images/p0382.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Install the modulator valve, center a **new gasket** with petroleum
jelly, tighten the modulator and reconnect its balance-tube hose. The
governor is accessible on the left; remove its lock screw and pull it
out. Its driven gear can be replaced by driving out the pin, drilling
a new pin hole **90° from the original**, fitting the gear and pin,
then installing a new governor O-ring.

For valve-body/servo removal, drain fluid at the filler tube, disconnect
TV rods, remove pan and pickup pipe, and attach the temporary sheet-metal
strap shown in the factory drawing. Lower the valve body slightly and
rotate the strap across the servo hub before fully removing the valve
body; this retains the servo and low-band apply pieces. **Tighten the
low-band screw fully before removing the servo piston in the vehicle.**
The hydraulic modulator body is under regulator-spring load; hold it
while removing its screws.

<a id="p383"></a> **[PDF p.383](../page-images/p0383.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Use factory figure 6E-11 to identify and retain in order the transfer
plate/gasket, hydraulic modulator, front and rear pump check valves,
priming ball, regulator, low-drive valves and springs, detent, throttle
valve and manual valve. Release the low-drive spring retainer under
control; tap the body with a plastic hammer to free the valve. Clean
every part and passage in solvent. Check each valve and bore for burrs
or deformation that could make a valve stick.

<a id="p384"></a> **[PDF p.384](../page-images/p0384.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Reassemble the hydraulic modulator with the **rear-pump check valve's
bleed poppet in the outboard bore**. Lower the transfer plate without
dislodging either check valve. Tighten both sets of transfer-plate
clutch-head screws to **38–50 in-lb**. Refit low-drive, throttle, detent,
regulator and manual valves in their illustrated order and align a new
transfer-plate gasket with petroleum jelly. For low-servo service,
measure piston-ring end gap in its bore: **.002–.012 in.**; ring side
clearance in its groove: **.0005–.005 in.** Assemble piston, cushion
spring and shaft with the hairpin retainer. Install the piston/return
spring with the shaft notch engaged on the band apply strut.

| Low-servo piston ring check | Factory limit |
|---|---|
| End gap in servo bore | .002–.012 in. |
| Side clearance in piston groove | .0005–.005 in. |

<a id="p385"></a> **[PDF p.385](../page-images/p0385.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Raise the valve body onto its case dowels while progressively releasing
the fully tightened band screw, or withdraw the temporary servo strap
when the body is almost seated. Engage the manual-valve lever. Install
the 20 valve-body bolts to **9–11 ft-lb**. The base page shortens the unit
to “lbs. torque”; Chevrolet's [Corvair Powerglide training booklet, Part II,
p. 25](https://chevy.oldcarmanualproject.com/booklets/60top04/6004top25.html)
explicitly says **foot-pounds** for the valve-body attaching bolts. Then install the pickup
pipe and its O-ring. Install a new pan gasket and tighten pan bolts
evenly to **3–4 ft-lb**; refill. Readjust the low band if its screw was
disturbed. Transmission removal and carrier/engine separation follow
Section 6. Most further service requires power-train removal.
<!-- RESOLVED UNIT: Base Section 6E p.6E-11 abbreviates "9–11 lbs. torque"; Chevrolet's Servicing the Corvair Powerglide Transmission Part II (T-O-P-60-S&M-4), p.25, explicitly states 9–11 foot-pounds for valve-body attaching bolts. -->

<a id="p386"></a> **[PDF p.386](../page-images/p0386.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For internal teardown with the transmission on the power train, first
tighten the low-band screw fully to keep case parts from coming out with
the front pump. Remove the pump's 12 case bolts and use the specified
slide hammers/adapters to draw it. Remove pump cover, shaft and body
without damaging shaft splines or pump/turbine bushings, then lift its
gasket. Loosen the band screw to remove band and struts; lift clutch
drum, planet carrier and turbine shaft. With the transmission still
attached to the axle, avoid separating ring gear from reverse-clutch
plates unless those parts require replacement: re-engaging them in the
horizontal position is difficult. Further teardown requires separating
the transmission from the power train.

## Case, reverse clutch and front pump inspection

<a id="p387"></a> **[PDF p.387](../page-images/p0387.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

With the transmission separated from the carrier, lift out ring gear.
Remove the reverse-clutch snap-ring clip and ring, then the thick front
reaction plate and alternating faced/steel plates. This inherited 1961
procedure depicts three faced plates; the dated 1963 GM specification
above lists four faced driving discs and four steel driven plates plus
a cast-iron pressure plate. Retain the installed stack for comparison
and verify replacement-part applicability before assembly.
Unbolt the rear pump/reverse piston from the carrier side, twist and pull
the assembly out, and remove its wear plate. Clean parts and passages;
protect finished case surfaces and inspect mating faces and the manual-
cable bore for cracks or wear. An overtightened cable nut can crack the
case at that bore.

Inspect ring-gear teeth/splines, governor drive gear and rear-pump wear

The factory exploded view shows the original Powerglide stack. Use it

<a id="p390"></a> **[PDF p.390](../page-images/p0390.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-16 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Figure 6E-30 identifies the installation tools for the seven

before pressing a replacement.

<a id="p391"></a> **[PDF p.391](../page-images/p0391.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-17 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check faced reverse-clutch plates for heat damage and steel plates for
damaged tangs; inspect case priming ball/seat, manual and throttle-valve
linkage, pickup screen and its O-ring, and low-band facing. Clean front-
pump parts/passages with solvent and compressed air; **do not dry with
rags**. Inspect pump gears, body and cover for scoring. With clean, dry
gears installed, measure the following clearances and replace worn parts:

| Front-pump gear clearance | Factory limit |
|---|---|
| Driven-gear outside diameter to body | .0025–.005 in. |
| Driven gear to crescent | .003–.009 in. |
| Gear end clearance | .0005–.0015 in. |

Check gear-to-crescent interference, cover gasket and square-cut seal,
hub iron rings and body bushing.

<a id="p392"></a> **[PDF p.392](../page-images/p0392.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-18 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove a worn front-pump-body bushing without marking its bore and press
in the new bushing with **J-8360-5**. For clutch-drum teardown, remove
the low-sun-gear/flange retainer, gear/flange, thrust washers, clutch hub
and alternating faced/steel plates. Compress the piston-return springs
in a press using the factory adapters before removing the spring-
retainer snap ring. Release press load gradually, then remove retainer,
springs and piston. Figure 6E-35 identifies **flat reaction plates** and
**waved faced plates**.

## Clutch drum and rear pump

<a id="p393"></a> **[PDF p.393](../page-images/p0393.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-19 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Clean the drum without rags; inspect band contact, bushing, clutch
flange fit, sun gear and plates. Its relief check ball must move and
rattle but stay captured; replace the drum if it cannot. The factory
identifies the newer service clutch pack by a green stripe: **waved
faced plates with flat steel reaction plates**. Do not pair those waved
faced plates with the earlier waved steel plates. Replace worn drum or
low-sun-gear bushings with the matching J-8360 installer. Refit piston
inner and outer lip seals **toward the front of the transmission**, oil
the seals, seat the piston, and position all 15 return springs and their
retainer under a press before securing its snap ring.

<a id="p394"></a> **[PDF p.394](../page-images/p0394.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-20 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fit the clutch-hub front thrust washer with lip toward the drum. Stack
**three flat steel** reaction plates and **two waved faced** drive
plates alternately, beginning with steel. Fit the rear thrust washer
with flange toward the low sun gear, install gear/flange and retainer,
and check that the hub rotates freely. The base text says replace a
failed planet carrier whole; **the supplement supersedes that rule**
with the pinion and bearing service below. Inspect turbine-shaft teeth,
splines, oil holes and bushings; use the distinct front/rear bushing
installers. Inspect pump shaft splines and bronze drive hub for wear,
looseness or an insecure retaining ring.

<a id="p395"></a> **[PDF p.395](../page-images/p0395.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-21 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For rear-pump/reverse-piston teardown, lift drive and driven gears, then
compress its spring retainer with **J-7782/J-5133** in a press. Remove
the snap ring and release all **17** return springs gradually. Fill the
hub's snap-ring groove temporarily with string or a small rubber band
to slide the pump body through the reverse piston; remove/discard its
old seals. Rear-pump gear fits use the **same clearance limits as the
front pump** on p.391. Inspect pump body, piston movement, springs and
bushing; replace a worn bushing with **J-8360-4**.

<a id="p396"></a> **[PDF p.396](../page-images/p0396.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-22 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fit new reverse-piston inner and outer seals with the **outer lip toward
the pump body**, seat the piston and 17 springs, press the retainer and
install its snap ring. Refit pump gears, using a little petroleum jelly
to hold them. The welded converter has no internal service or routine
drain. Inspect starter teeth and welds and converter seams; repair only
as the factory describes while preserving balance, or replace a rough
converter. Converter-hub bushing installer is **J-8360-1**.

To reassemble a separated transmission, mount the case on **J-7896**,
hold the rear-pump wear plate on guide pins and feed the rear-pump/
reverse-piston assembly into the case without rolling its outer seal.
The factory uses .010–.015 in. shim stock around the seal for seating.
Secure five rear-pump bolts to **9–11 ft-lb**. Stack reverse clutch
steel and faced plates alternately, beginning with steel; align each
steel plate's notched lug in the case's **4 o'clock** groove.

<a id="p397"></a> **[PDF p.397](../page-images/p0397.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-23 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The thick reaction plate's square dimple also occupies the 4 o'clock
groove. Install retaining ring with opening at **12 o'clock** and its
clip between the ring ends. Align faced plate grooves and engage ring
gear by feel. If still on the power train, engage turbine shaft in the
converter now. Twist the planet carrier into the ring gear, ensuring
its two rear-pump drive lugs seat in the pump gear.

<a id="p398"></a> **[PDF p.398](../page-images/p0398.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-24 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fit the input-sun-gear thrust washer **flange forward**, then clutch
drum and low band/struts. The front selective thrust washer procedure
depends on assembly position. **Only while the transmission remains
attached to the differential carrier**, bolt gauge **J-8371** to the
case: use **.076 in.** when its seated plunger is below flush and
**.050 in.** when flush or above. On a separated transmission, retain
the original washer unless replacement is needed, finish assembly and
set final end play at the rear governor gear under Section 6. The base
front-pump gasket illustration is corrected by the supplement below.

## Front pump installation and hydraulic diagnosis

<a id="p399"></a> **[PDF p.399](../page-images/p0399.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-25 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Install a **new gasket between front-pump body and transmission case**
as the supplement corrects, then seat the pump body without breaking
its cast-iron hub rings. Insert pump drive shaft carefully through the
bushings. Install a new square-cut front-cover seal, seal bolt heads
against oil leaks, and tighten the **outer bolts in a criss-cross
sequence first**, then the five inner bolts, all at **18–20 ft-lb**.
For a serviced low band, tighten adjusting screw to **40 ±5 in-lb**,
back off **exactly four full turns**, hold screw and secure locknut.
If the transmission was separated from the carrier, determine final
governor-gear selective-spacer thickness under Section 6 before joining
the units.

<a id="p400"></a> **[PDF p.400](../page-images/p0400.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-26 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The front-pump pressure tap is at **6 o'clock** in the cover; the
throttle-valve tap is at **8 o'clock**. Warm transmission, check level
and linkage, zero the gauges and test with rear wheels supported as
the factory describes. The supplement clarifies that these figures
are **actual front-pump pressure, not mainline pressure**, at idle and
**16 in. Hg** vacuum for the first row.

| Front-pump pressure, psi | Reverse | Neutral | Drive | Low |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Idle, 16 in. Hg | 81–98 | 47–57 | 47–57 | 71–82 |
| Idle, balance-tube vacuum hose disconnected | 140–157 | 71–82 | 71–82 | 71–82 |

At approximately **20 mph** with rear wheels raised in Drive, front-
pump pressure should fall to about **0–5 psi** as rear-pump flow takes
over. If not, inspect rear pump and its check ball. For the TV-pressure
check, detach the TV rod at the carburetor and vacuum hose at balance
tube, depress accelerator fully while engine remains at idle:

| TV pressure, psi | Reverse | Neutral | Drive | Low |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rod and hose disconnected, accelerator at WOT | 0 | 0 | 52–54 | 66–77 |

The factory says loosening the TV jam nut **one full turn raises about
3 psi**, and tightening it lowers about 3 psi; hold the Allen-ended
adjusting screw while turning the nut.

<a id="p401"></a> **[PDF p.401](../page-images/p0401.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6E-27 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For no drive, check level and pickup first, then selector cable,
regulator, pump shaft/front pump, and priming/check valves. For flare on
launch, examine low-band apply circuit, servo, band and converter
stator; for flare on upshift, inspect high-clutch feed, plates, seals,
drum relief ball and modulator hose. For no upshift, check band release,
low-drive valve, governor/rear pump output, TV and manual-valve
adjustments. Harsh changes can result from TV rod, band, modulator or
hydraulic-modulator faults. Reverse-only failure points to linkage,
reverse piston/clutch or blocked feed. Aerated fluid from overfill or
pickup leakage can force fluid out the filler.

The base diagnosis list mentions a **line-pressure limit valve**, but
the same manual's opening description explicitly says that valve was
**deleted from the 1961 design**. The 1962–63 supplement does not add it
back; treat the diagnostic-list entry as retained older wording and do
not search for that valve on a 1963 passenger Powerglide.

## Troubleshooting changes

**Front pump check.** The front-pump gauge reads actual pump pressure, not
mainline pressure. Obtain it with the engine at idle (16″ Hg. as printed in the
supplement). The base pressure table is transcribed above at p.400.

**Low band adjustment.** The supplement says no periodic adjustment is
recommended. The access to the adjustment screw from the parcel compartment
inside the vehicle was eliminated.

## Planet carrier removal and inspection

1. Remove the planet carrier assembly by the 1961 shop-manual procedure.
2. Wash the carrier in cleaning solvent, blow out every oil passage, and air dry.

   > **CAUTION:** Do not use rags to dry parts.

3. Inspect the planet pinions for nicks or other tooth damage.
4. Check planet-gear end clearance: **.005″–.035″** (factory fig. 6E-1).
5. Inspect the input sun gear teeth and its thrust washer for damage.
6. Inspect the carrier splines for nicks or damage, and the pinion shaft ends
   for proper staking.

## Planet carrier repair

If the planet pinions, needle bearings, pinion thrust washers, input sun gear,
or input sun gear thrust washer show excessive wear or damage, the supplement
directs replacement by this procedure (factory fig. 6E-2).

1. Put the carrier in a fixture or vise, splined end downward.
2. Beginning with a short planet pinion, use a soft-steel drift to drive the
   upper pinion-shaft end past the staked positions and press-fit portion of
   the carrier. Feed tool J-9560-1 into the pinion from above, advancing the
   shaft until the tool is centered in the pinion (fig. 6E-3).
3. Remove the short pinion and lower thrust washer; finish removing the shaft.
4. Remove J-9560-1, the needle bearings, and both needle-bearing washers from
   the short pinion.

<a id="p679"></a> **[PDF p.679](../page-images/p0679.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6E-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

> **CAUTION:** Do not lose the needle bearings. The short pinion uses 20 long
> needle bearings; the long pinion uses 40 short bearings, 20 at each end with
> a spacer in the middle.

5. Remove and disassemble the other short pinions.
6. Remove the input sun gear and its thrust washer.
7. Following steps 2–4, remove the long pinions and their upper and lower
   thrust washers.
8. Wash all parts in cleaning solvent and air dry.
9. Recheck the pinion and sun-gear teeth and all thrust washers. Replace worn
   or damaged parts.
10. Inspect needle bearings; replace all of them if wear is excessive.
    Inspect pinion shafts and replace worn shafts.
11. With J-9560-2, assemble the needle-bearing spacer and short needle
    bearings (**20 at each end**) in one long pinion. Petroleum jelly holds
    the bearings during assembly. Put a needle-bearing washer at each end.
12. Reverse the carrier's position in the fixture.
13. Center J-9560-2 in the long pinion and position the pinion and a thrust
    washer at each end in the carrier. Face the washer oil grooves toward the
    gears and align the washers with the carrier holes.

    > **NOTE:** Long pinions go opposite the closed portions of the carrier;
    > short pinions go in the openings.

14. Lubricate the selected pinion shaft and tap it into place, pushing the
    assembling tool ahead of it (fig. 6E-4).
15. With a brass or soft-steel drift, drive the shaft until its lower end
    engages the staked positions on the carrier's lower face.
16. Assemble and install the other long pinions.
17. Install the input sun gear thrust washer and the input sun gear.
18. Following steps 11–15, assemble and install the short pinions. Each uses
    **20 long needle bearings** and a needle-bearing washer at each end.

    > **NOTE:** Paired thrust washers go on the thrust surface toward the
    > carrier's flanged side, between short and long pinions; the opposite
    > thrust surface has a single washer.

19. Check planet-gear end clearance: **.005″–.035″** (fig. 6E-1).
20. Restake each pinion shaft at four places on **both ends** of the carrier,
    using a chisel or center punch (fig. 6E-5).

## Front pump gasket figure correction

The supplement says that the 1961 manual's fig. 6E-29 on printed p. 6E-15
places the front-pump gasket incorrectly. It states that corrected fig. 6E-6
puts the gasket **between the front-pump body and the transmission case**.
The recovered three-page supplement PDF does not include fig. 6E-6 itself.
Chevrolet's [1964 supplement, Section 6E p. 6E-5, fig. 6E-6](https://www.scribd.com/document/364459154/1964-supplement-chevrolet-corvair-shop-manual-section-6e-automatic-transmission-pdf)
also labels the front-pump gasket as item 8 directly behind the front-pump
body. This later figure corroborates the **gasket position only**; its other
1964 transmission details should not be substituted for a missing 1963
exploded view.
