# Brakes — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1962–63 supplement says the brake service procedures generally carry
forward from the 1961 passenger-car manual except where it gives changes.
For 1963, all four service brakes use the supplement's **self-adjusting**
shoe hardware and revised brake-pedal adjustment. The base manual's
manual shoe-adjustment interval and old pedal-clearance procedure therefore
do not define 1963 service. Corvair 95 and Greenbrier commercial procedures
are excluded.

Source files: `5-brakes-61.pdf` (base, physical pp. 1–22, combined pp.
210–231; passenger section on printed pp. 5-1–5-14, general trouble/tool
pages 5-21–5-22) and `5-brakes-62-63.pdf` (supplement, physical pp. 1–9,
combined pp. 649–657; passenger section on printed pp. 5-1–5-8).

The base printed pp. 5-15–5-20 and supplement p. 5-9 concern commercial
Corvair 95/Greenbrier models. Base passenger pp. 5-9–5-10 use earlier pedal
and shoe/adjuster hardware; their affected operations are superseded by the
1963 supplement, while the general wheel-cylinder and hydraulic procedures
continue to apply.

## Brake layout and 1963 self-adjustment

<a id="p211"></a> **[PDF p.211](../page-images/p0211.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The passenger car has four hydraulic, duo-servo, single-anchor drum brakes.
Each wheel cylinder has two opposed pistons. A pedal-operated main cylinder
sends hydraulic pressure through lines to the wheel cylinders. The parking
brake lever and cable operate the rear brakes separately. The 1963 passenger
service-brake specification identifies the design as self-adjusting.

<a id="p649"></a> **[PDF p.649](../page-images/p0649.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The supplement explicitly retains the base brake service methods except for
its listed 1962–63 changes. Its 1963 brake illustrations and instructions
apply to the 500, 700, and 900 passenger series.

See the [1963 self-adjusting brake assembly](../diagrams/p0649-1963-self-adjusting-brake.png).

<a id="p650"></a> **[PDF p.650](../page-images/p0650.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-2 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

In reverse, a firm brake application moves an actuator that turns the star
wheel, lengthening the adjusting-screw assembly until lining-to-drum
clearance is correct. If pedal height is low, make repeated forward and
reverse stops with firm brake applications until satisfactory pedal height
returns. Using an automatic-transmission forward range to stop a car moving
backward can prevent the adjuster from operating.

The 1963 drum web has a lanced knockout for retracting the shoes when a
worn drum cannot otherwise be removed. Its use and the required replacement
hole cover belong with the shoe/drum service procedure later in this chapter.

## Hydraulic fluid and bleeding

<a id="p212"></a> **[PDF p.212](../page-images/p0212.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Keep the hydraulic system and brake linings clean. Foreign matter can block
lines and damage cylinder cups; grease or dirt on linings changes brake
action. The base manual calls for **GM Hydraulic Brake Fluid Super No. 11**.
If improper fluid has entered the system, its procedure drains the system,
flushes it with the specified cleaning fluid, replaces all rubber hydraulic
parts including hoses, and refills with the specified brake fluid.

<a id="p213"></a> **[PDF p.213](../page-images/p0213.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Bleed whenever a hydraulic line is disconnected or air enters. Work from
the longest line: **left rear → right rear → right front → left front**.
The base manual also provides manual pedal bleeding: keep the reservoir
filled, submerge the hose end in brake fluid, open the bleeder, depress the
pedal, close the bleeder before releasing the pedal, and repeat until the
discharge has no air bubbles. Finish with a solid fluid stream and the
reservoir at the correct level.

<a id="p650-bleed"></a> **[PDF p.650](../page-images/p0650.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For pressure bleeding, protect the luggage compartment and front fender
from fluid. Clean the main-cylinder top and remove the cover. Fit main-
cylinder adapter **J-9477** and connect a pressure bleeder able to provide
**30–50 lb. hydraulic pressure**. Attach hose and tool **J-7647** to a wheel
bleeder; keep the free hose end submerged in clean brake fluid in a clear
container. Open the bleeder about **¾ turn**, observe the discharge, then
close it tightly when bubbles stop and a solid fluid stream runs. Continue
until enough fluid has passed to replace the old fluid, then repeat at the
other wheels in the sequence above.

| Pressure-bleeding setting | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Bleeder-equipment hydraulic pressure | 30–50 lb. |
| Bleeder valve opening | About ¾ turn |

<a id="p651"></a> **[PDF p.651](../page-images/p0651.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-3 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After bleeding, disconnect equipment, remove J-9477, and replace the
main-cylinder cover. The adapter sets the proper reservoir fill to **¼ in.
below the rim**; do not overfill.

## Brake-pedal free movement and main cylinder

<a id="p651-pedal"></a> **[PDF p.651](../page-images/p0651.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-3 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

With the pedal in its returned position, measure **upward movement of the
pedal pad before the pedal arm touches the fixed stop**. Set this free
movement to **1⁄16–¼ in.** by loosening the push-rod checknut and turning
the rod. Retighten the checknut against the clevis and recheck. Excess free
movement makes the pedal rattle; too little can load the main-cylinder
piston and close its compensating port. The later-model cylinder's free
movement is adjusted **after** mounting to the dash wall, rather than by
the base manual's earlier cylinder-assembly setting.

| 1963 brake-pedal check | Specification |
|---|---|
| Upward free movement at pedal pad before contact with fixed stop | 1⁄16–¼ in. |

The supplement's main cylinder has revised placement and access but works
like the earlier cylinder. To remove it, protect the luggage compartment
and fender, disconnect the hydraulic outlet line and tee, remove the two
stud nuts/lock washers, and lift out the cylinder, gasket, and boot. The
push rod can be withdrawn then; removing its clevis requires removal of the
pedal assembly. The base cylinder disassembly and inspection remain
applicable, subject to the new pedal-free-movement setting.

See the [1963 main-cylinder exploded view](../diagrams/p0651-brake-main-cylinder.png).

For installation, fit the gasket on the studs and rubber boot to the
cylinder. Lubricate the push rod with rubber lubricant; guide it through
the boot into the piston while mounting the cylinder. Secure retaining
nuts, reconnect the hydraulic outlet line and tee, adjust pedal free
movement, fill the reservoir, and bleed the system.

## Brake pedal removal and installation

<a id="p652"></a> **[PDF p.652](../page-images/p0652.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-4 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove the pedal stop and stop-light switch. Loosen the main-cylinder
push-rod checknut and shorten the rod for maximum clearance to the piston.
Remove the outer hairpin clip and cut off the push-on thrust retainer from
the pivot shaft; discard that retainer. Remove pivot shaft, pedal, push rod,
and clevis from the support and boot. Remove clevis, push rod, pad, and
nylon bushings from the pedal. Clean metal parts with suitable solvent;
**wipe nylon bushings with a clean cloth without using cleaning solvent**.
Inspect pivot pin and bushings and replace worn or damaged parts.

Lightly coat the inside of nylon bushings with Lubriplate and fit them to
the pedal. Attach push rod and clevis to the pedal with clevis pin inserted
**from the right side** and a cotter pin. Rubber-lubricate the push rod and
insert it through the boot while positioning the pedal in its support.
Insert the pivot pin **from the left side**. Hold its head against the
support and install a **new** push-on retainer, flush side snug against the
support, with no pivot-pin end play. Fit the hairpin clip, pedal stop, and
stop-light switch, then set the [pedal free movement](#brake-pedal-free-movement-and-main-cylinder).

## 1963 brake drums, shoes, and self-adjusting hardware

<a id="p652-drum"></a> **[PDF p.652](../page-images/p0652.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-4 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The following steps replace the base manual's shoe hardware and manual
adjustment instructions where affected by the 1963 self-adjuster. If a
severely worn drum will not pass the shoes, knock out the lanced opening in
its web, lift the actuator off the star wheel with one screwdriver, and
turn the star wheel with another screwdriver to retract the shoes. Remove
the loose metal from inside the drum and brake compartment. Fit a **new
hole cover** when the drum is reinstalled.

### Remove and inspect shoes

1. Support the raised car on stands. Loosen the parking-brake cable checknuts
   at its front to remove cable tension, then remove the drum. The 1963
   wheel-cylinder boots sit in cylinder grooves and keep pistons in place
   without clamps, but **do not depress the brake pedal with drums off**.

<a id="p653"></a> **[PDF p.653](../page-images/p0653.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

2. With spring tool **J-8049**, unhook shoe pullback springs from the anchor
   and link. Remove actuator return spring, free the link from anchor and
   secondary shoe, and remove hold-down pins/springs. Take off the actuator
   unit. Keep its actuator, pivot, and override spring assembled unless
   broken. Separate the shoes by removing the adjuster screw and spring.
   At the rear, remove the parking-brake lever from the secondary shoe.
3. Clean and inspect drums for roughness, scoring, and out-of-round;
   recondition or replace as needed. Keep dirt out of front-wheel bearings
   and inspect their seal. Gently lift wheel-cylinder boots to inspect for
   fluid. A slight film is normal, but **excess fluid** calls for wheel-
   cylinder overhaul. At the rear, inspect axle-shaft seals for oil leaks.

<a id="p654"></a> **[PDF p.654](../page-images/p0654.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

4. Check brake backing-plate fasteners and clean rust/dirt from shoe contact
   faces with fine emery cloth. Use the correct replacement shoe and lining
   assembly. Keep lining faces clean and free of oil, grease, burrs, and
   excess bonding material.

### Assemble shoes and adjusters

<a id="p654-assembly"></a> **[PDF p.654](../page-images/p0654.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

1. At rear brakes, lubricate the parking-brake cable and lever fulcrum,
   fasten the lever to the secondary shoe with bolt, spring washer,
   lock washer, and nut, and check free movement. Clean and lubricate the
   adjusting screw; contamination or lack of lubrication can cause loose
   adjustment.
2. Join the shoes with the adjusting-screw spring, then position screw,
   socket, and nut. Use the correct **left- or right-hand** adjuster. The
   star wheel belongs **nearest the secondary shoe**, with the spring
   arranged so it cannot interfere with the star wheel.
3. Mount the **short-lined primary shoe facing forward** with its hold-down
   pin/spring and engage both shoes with wheel-cylinder links. Install the
   actuator, override spring, return spring, secondary shoe, and its
   hold-down pin/spring. Install the guide plate over the anchor.
4. Put the wire link over the anchor-pin stud **before** attaching it to
   the actuator held full down. Do not drag a regular spring hook over
   the stud; that can damage the wheel-cylinder boot seal. At rear brakes,
   connect the parking-brake lever and its strut to the primary shoe.
5. Replace nicked, distorted, or weak shoe-return springs. Hook the primary
   spring from its shoe over the anchor with J-8049, then the secondary
   spring over the wire-link end. Pry shoes away from the backing plate and
   apply only a thin coat of brake lubricant to shoe contact areas. Keep
   lubricant off linings. Work the actuator by hand to verify that the
   star wheel advances freely. Repeat at all four wheels.

See the [shoe assembly and contact-point illustrations](../diagrams/p0654-brake-shoe-assembly.png).

### Initial shoe setting and automatic final adjustment

<a id="p655"></a> **[PDF p.655](../page-images/p0655.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

After relining or when adjuster length changes, set a preliminary clearance
with the drum removed: lift the actuator away from the star wheel and turn
the adjuster. Chevrolet recommends **J-21177 drum-to-shoe clearance gauge**:
set the gauge to drum inside diameter, reverse it over the installed shoes,
and turn the star wheel until the gauge just slides over the linings. Rotate
the gauge around the lining surface to check clearance.

See the [gauge and actuator illustrations](../diagrams/p0655-brake-clearance-gauge.png).

<a id="p656"></a> **[PDF p.656](../page-images/p0656.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-8 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

An alternate initial setting uses the drum as the gauge: turn the star
wheel until the drum slides over the shoes with slight drag, then retract
the shoes by **1¼ star-wheel turns**. Refit drum and wheel. If the knockout
was used, remove all loose metal and fit a new cover. Align the drum's
locating tang with the same hub hole used before removal. Make the final
setting by forward and reverse stops until pedal height is satisfactory;
then adjust the parking brake.

| Initial 1963 shoe setting, drum used as gauge | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Retract after slight drum drag | 1¼ star-wheel turns |

### Metallic-lining option

<a id="p656-metallic"></a> **[PDF p.656](../page-images/p0656.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=5-8 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Optional metallic linings use special heat-resistant springs. They require
drums honed to a **20 micro-inch finish**; Chevrolet does not recommend
putting metallic service linings in standard drums without that finish.
After adjustment, seat the new linings with **six to eight stops from
30 mph** at moderate pedal effort, then **six to eight full stops from the
maximum legal highway speed**, about a mile apart.

## Hydraulic lines and flexible hoses carried forward

<a id="p214"></a> **[PDF p.214](../page-images/p0214.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect flexible hoses for road damage. To replace one, disconnect its
steel-line double-flare connector, remove the U-shaped support retainer,
withdraw the hose, and unscrew it from the wheel cylinder. Fit a **new
copper gasket** at the cylinder end of the new hose, moisten its threads
with brake fluid, and install it at the cylinder. With the suspension at
normal loaded height and front wheels straight ahead, pass the female end
through its support bracket and let the hose find its natural curvature.
Seat its hex in the bracket's 12-point opening at the orientation with the
least twist and secure the U retainer. Unload the wheel and steer front
wheels lock to lock to verify that the hose touches no other part at any
suspension or steering position. Rotate the bracket end one or two points
and recheck if clearance is insufficient. Reconnect the steel line and
bleed the brakes.

Use only high-pressure, corrosion-resistant hydraulic brake tubing, with
**double-lap flares**. The base manual expressly rejects ordinary copper
tubing.

<a id="p215"></a> **[PDF p.215](../page-images/p0215.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For a replacement tube, square and deburr its end, slide the fitting on
**before flaring**, wet the end with brake fluid, and use Chevrolet tool
**J-8051** or an equivalent double-lap flaring tool. Gauge projection with
its upset punch, clamp the tube firmly, form the upset first, then form
the final seat with the finishing punch. Inspect the seat as it forms to
avoid overworking it. The base manual's 1961 **manual service-brake shoe
adjustment** on this page is superseded by the 1963 self-adjuster procedure.

## Parking-brake adjustment and cable service

<a id="p216"></a> **[PDF p.216](../page-images/p0216.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Set the 1963 service brakes first; that shoe position is the baseline for
parking-brake adjustment. Raise both rear wheels, pull the parking-brake
lever **four notches** from fully released, loosen the front equalizer
checknut, and tighten the rear one until both rear wheels have a heavy
rotational drag. Tighten checknuts. Fully release the lever and verify
that the wheels turn without drag. If the brake binds or fails to release,
check cable/pulley lubrication, lever straightness and free motion, and
return-spring condition. Apply the 1963 lubrication schedule rather than
the old mileage note in the base chapter.

<a id="p217"></a> **[PDF p.217](../page-images/p0217.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

### Forward cable

Disconnect the battery positive cable before working under the dash.
Release the parking brake, remove equalizer checknuts and separate the
front cable stud. Remove underbody and toe-pan tunnel covers. Remove the
cable pulley from the upper toe-board bracket; on a manual-transmission car,
disconnect the clutch cable if its tension prevents pulley removal. Free
the cable ball from the hand-lever clevis and withdraw the cable. To
install, route through pulley brackets, guides, and tunnel wall; lubricate
and refit the lever ball and pulley; restore clutch cable if equipped;
connect at the equalizer; and replace covers. **Use original-length
toe-pan-cover screws** because longer screws can puncture the fuel tank.
Reconnect the battery and adjust the parking brake.

### Rear cable

With brake released, remove return spring and equalizer checknut, then
free the front stud from the equalizer. Remove the support U-clips and the
clip at the shock absorber. Remove rear wheel, drum, and axle-bearing
retainer bolts, and move the axle flange outward enough for access. Free
the rear cable tip from the secondary-shoe actuating lever by compressing
its retaining spring. Compress the conduit locking fingers at the backing
plate and withdraw the cable.

Fit the new conduit through the backing plate until all locking fingers
expand, then connect its tip to the actuating lever. Restore bearing
retainer, drum, wheel, shock-absorber clip, support clips, boot, equalizer,
and return spring. Keep the equalizer free to slide on the rear cable,
lubricate it per the 1963 schedule, then adjust the parking brake.

## Main-cylinder overhaul carried forward

<a id="p220"></a> **[PDF p.220](../page-images/p0220.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The supplement retains the base main-cylinder disassembly and internal
inspection, but **its revised 1963 removal, installation, and pedal setting
above take precedence**. With the cylinder removed, pull out push rod and
boot, remove the piston stop ring, secondary cup/piston, primary cup,
spring, valve, and valve seat. Wash parts in clean alcohol with clean hands.
Keep the compensating port and piston bleeder holes open. Check that the
bore is smooth and the cups, valve, and seat have no damage or swelling.
Improper fluid or petroleum solvent swells the rubber. Piston-to-bore
clearance should be **.001–.005 in.** Replace damaged parts. An incorrectly
seated check-valve rubber washer can cause excessive pedal travel and a
need to pump the pedal before braking occurs.

<a id="p221"></a> **[PDF p.221](../page-images/p0221.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Install valve seat, valve, and spring. Wet the primary cup with clean brake
fluid and install with its flat face toward the push-rod end, seated over
the spring. Wet and fit the secondary cup to the piston; install piston
with its bleeder-hole end toward the primary cup. Fit stop ring and boot,
making sure the boot seals the cylinder against dirt and water. Install
the cylinder using the 1963 procedure, then set free movement and bleed.

## Wheel-cylinder service carried forward

<a id="p221-cylinder"></a> **[PDF p.221](../page-images/p0221.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Raise and secure the car. Retract shoes as needed using the **1963
self-adjuster method** and remove the drum. At the front, hub and drum
come off together; rear drums are removable from the axle flange. Disconnect
the hydraulic hose, unhook the shoe-return springs, and remove the anchor
pin and cylinder. Pull boots, pistons, cups, and spring from the cylinder,
then wash with clean alcohol.

<a id="p222"></a> **[PDF p.222](../page-images/p0222.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect the bore for scoring or damage, replace swollen or damaged cups,
and measure piston-to-bore clearance. It should be **.002–.004 in.**;
replace the cylinder if clearance exceeds **.004 in.** Wet bore, pistons,
and cups with clean brake fluid. Fit one boot and piston with the piston's
flat side toward the cylinder opening; install the cup flat side toward
the piston, spring with its expanders, the opposite cup and piston, and
the second boot.

At the front, pass the anchor pin and lock washer through the cylinder,
thread into the spindle support, and tighten to **60–85 ft. lb.**, then
peen the washer tabs. At the rear, pass pin through cylinder and backing
plate; fit washer and nut on the inboard side and tighten to **100–125
ft. lb.** Restore push rods and springs, hydraulic hose, drum and wheel;
adjust the [front bearing](03-suspension.md#front-wheel-bearing-adjustment)
when the front hub was removed. Bleed all brake lines, make the 1963
service-brake adjustment, and test braking.

| Passenger wheel-cylinder service | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Front anchor-pin torque | 60–85 ft. lb. |
| Rear anchor-pin torque | 100–125 ft. lb. |
| Piston-to-bore clearance | .002–.004 in. |

## Drum inspection and reconditioning

<a id="p223"></a> **[PDF p.223](../page-images/p0223.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-14 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect each removed drum for cracks, scoring, grooves, out-of-round,
taper, and wear. Light scoring may be polished with fine emery cloth; a
drum more than **.010 in. out-of-round** needs reconditioning. Remove only
enough material for a true, smooth surface. The base manual limits boring
to **.060 in. above maximum standard diameter** and requires finished
runout no greater than **.005 in. total indicator reading**. It calls for
**.030 in. oversize facings** when a drum must be rebored more than
.010 in. over standard. For the **metallic-lining option**, also meet the
supplement's 20 micro-inch finish requirement.

Remove storage rustproofing oil from a new drum with a volatile, non-toxic,
greaseless solvent, keeping oil off the linings. Clean hub and drum mating
faces, refit drums in their original indexed positions, and use the 1963
shoe setting. If the front hub was removed, adjust its bearings by the
1963 procedure.

## Troubleshooting and special tools

<a id="p230"></a> **[PDF p.230](../page-images/p0230.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-21 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base trouble chart remains a diagnostic starting point. Where it says
to adjust service shoes or push-rod clearance, use the **1963 procedures**
in this chapter.

| Symptom | Factory checks and remedies |
|---|---|
| Spongy pedal | Bleed air from the hydraulic lines. |
| All brakes drag | Check for mineral oil or other improper fluid, insufficient pedal free movement, and a restricted main-cylinder compensating port. Flush/replace damaged rubber, set free movement, or overhaul the cylinder as needed. |
| One brake drags | Inspect wheel bearing, shoe return spring, shoe-to-drum clearance, parking-brake equalizer setting, and cable freedom. |
| Excess pedal travel | Check shoe clearance and main-cylinder fluid level; the 1963 self-adjuster must operate correctly. |
| Pedal gradually falls to floor while braking | Find external leaks at cylinder, lines, and wheel cylinders; inspect the main-cylinder primary cup for internal leakage. |
| Uneven braking | Check for grease on lining, unequal tire pressure, and front-suspension faults. |
| High pedal effort or poor stopping | Check lining contamination or incomplete drum contact and scored drums. |
| Pedal hop or chatter | Check for warped drums. |

<a id="p231"></a> **[PDF p.231](../page-images/p0231.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=5-22 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The illustrated tool list includes **J-7647** bleeder-valve wrench,
**J-8051** double-lap flaring set, and **J-8049** brake-shoe spring tool.
The supplement adds **J-9477** main-cylinder pressure-bleeder adapter and
**J-21177** drum-to-shoe clearance gauge. The base list's **J-8363** manual
shoe-adjuster tool relates to the superseded 1961 adjustment method.

For numeric service limits and optional equipment, use the 1963 column of
the [specifications chapter](12-specifications.md) once its brake tables
are fully reconstructed. Do not substitute a 1962-only value.
