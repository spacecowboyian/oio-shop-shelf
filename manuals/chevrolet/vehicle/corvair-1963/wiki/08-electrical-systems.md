# Electrical Systems — 1963 Passenger Cars

The 1962–63 supplement carries forward base electrical service except
where it changes the battery, distributor, wiper, washer, instruments
and wiring. This chapter is being assembled for the **1963 passenger
car**, with commercial-only diagrams excluded. Original files, hashes
and combined PDF page numbers are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json).

## Base engine-electrical checks and charging circuit

<a id="p418"></a> **[PDF p.418](../page-images/p0418.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base chapter separates engine electrical, wiring diagrams and
chassis electrical. Its passenger engine section covers battery,
generator/regulator, starting and ignition circuits. The supplement
changes the standard battery and distributor and supplies 1963 wiring
drawings below. Base Corvair 95 material near the end is outside this
passenger-car reference.

<a id="p419"></a> **[PDF p.419](../page-images/p0419.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For a battery light-load check, inspect case/electrolyte first; then
crank briefly, turn on low-beam headlamps and read each cell with a
fine-scale voltmeter after one minute. A difference of **.05 V or
more** between the highest and lowest cells when a cell is at least
**1.95 V** indicates a defective battery under the factory test.
Uniform but low readings call for recharge; very low cells must be
boost-charged before diagnosis. During prolonged preparation, use
separate cranking intervals with cooling time so the starter is not
damaged. For 1963 standard battery construction and slow-charge rate,
use the supplement p.687 below.

<a id="p420"></a> **[PDF p.420](../page-images/p0420.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Cranking battery voltage below **9.0 V** at about **80 ±20°F** warrants
further battery testing. For poor charging, check belt, generator,
circuit resistance and regulator; excessive water use may indicate a
high regulator setting. At medium engine speed, generator output
should rise when its field terminal is momentarily grounded; if not,
test the generator. Regulator BAT-to-ground voltage should be higher
at medium speed than at idle. A solenoid that fails to pull in needs
control-circuit, current-draw, pinion-clearance and linkage checks;
one that chatters may have an open hold-in winding. No spark during
cranking or an engine that stops when START is released calls for
ignition-circuit diagnosis.

<a id="p421"></a> **[PDF p.421](../page-images/p0421.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base page's **seven-plate 35 Ah standard / nine-plate 40 Ah
optional** battery capacities describe 1961 and are superseded by
the 1963 **nine-plate 42 Ah standard** battery. Continue to check
electrolyte level and external condition; use only distilled or
demineralized water to the vent-well bottom, never overfill. Keep
battery top/cables clean and hold-down secure without straining case.
The factory charging-circuit drawing identifies battery, generator,
cutout, current and voltage regulator.

<a id="p422"></a> **[PDF p.422](../page-images/p0422.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fully seat clean battery clamps; oil the positive-terminal felt washer
at the specified service interval. Fill generator hinge-cap oilers
with light engine oil at lubrication service, inspect commutator and
brushes, and replace brushes below **half original length**. If the
generator lamp stays lit above idle, stop and check **blower belt**
before charging-system diagnosis. The factory charging-circuit voltage-
drop check at approximately **20 A** permits **V1 + V2 ≤.5 V** and
**V3 ≤.03 V** in its illustrated wiring test. Correct poor grounds,
loose terminals and damaged conductors.

<a id="p423"></a> **[PDF p.423](../page-images/p0423.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The Corvair regulator has a **copper-colored external ground strap**.
Check voltage regulator, cutout relay, current regulator and contact
condition in that order with the cover fitted and unit at operating
temperature. To measure voltage-regulator setting, the source places a
fixed load at regulator BAT, measures BAT-to-ground, runs engine at
**1600 rpm for at least 15 minutes**, cycles generator, and records
voltage with regulator ambient temperature. The factory figure and
Section 12 supply the temperature-dependent limits; do not apply a
single room-temperature voltage as an unconditional specification.

<a id="p424"></a> **[PDF p.424](../page-images/p0424.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For regulator adjustment, raising spring tension raises voltage.
Finish adjustment while **increasing** tension so the screw head stays
against its spring support; replace cover and cycle generator before
reading. Figure 8-9 graphs allowable voltage against regulator ambient
temperature (about **45–165°F**) and refers the published normal range
to **125°F**. Evaluate battery charge and water use over service
before changing a setting that falls within the applicable range.

<a id="p425"></a> **[PDF p.425](../page-images/p0425.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory advises tailoring regulator voltage in small increments
only when service history shows chronic undercharge or water use;
avoid corrected settings over **14.8 V** because lights and other
equipment may be damaged. The cutout relay should close between
**11.8 and 13.5 V** and at least **.5 V below** the regulator setting;
verify it opens as speed falls. If generator armature overheats,
measure current-regulator output under the factory load procedure.

<a id="p426"></a> **[PDF p.426](../page-images/p0426.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

To check current regulation, add a load to lower system voltage to
**12.5–13.0 V**, warm regulator covered at **1600 rpm for at least 15
minutes**, cycle generator and read current. Regulator contacts that
have oxidized can suppress output: with the source's ammeter setup at
about **5 A**, grounding regulator field should increase generator
output by no more than **2 A**; a larger rise calls for contact
cleaning. Inspect and correct charging faults before replacing a
battery. Clean a dirty generator commutator with **No. 00 sandpaper or
a cleaning stone**, not emery cloth.

<a id="p427"></a> **[PDF p.427](../page-images/p0427.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base text lists charging-system defects, heavy loads, short trips,
dirty battery tops, long discharge and internal damage as common causes
of battery failure. Its **8-hour slow charge** and **1.250–1.290**
full-charge hydrometer band are superseded by the supplement's **10-
hour minimum** and **1.230–1.310** band on p.687. A stored wet battery
needs periodic level and charge checks; dry-charged storage guidance
applies only until electrolyte is added.

<a id="p428"></a> **[PDF p.428](../page-images/p0428.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For generator removal, disconnect battery ground and generator field/
armature leads, slacken the blower idler, then remove belt and three
mounting bolts. To replace brushes, remove through-bolts, commutator
end frame and armature/drive frame, then unscrew brush leads and refit
brushes. The pulley retaining nut has a **left-hand thread**; remember
this before generator disassembly. Seat new brushes before relying on
immediate output test readings.

<a id="p429"></a> **[PDF p.429](../page-images/p0429.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory exploded generator view identifies armature, frame, field
coils, commutator-end bushings/brushes and drive-end bearing. Keep
their washers, felt seals and spacers in original order through
disassembly and inspection.

<a id="p430"></a> **[PDF p.430](../page-images/p0430.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Clean generator parts and inspect field insulation, brushes, springs,
commutator solder, shaft, bearing and bushing. A growler and steel
blade test identifies armature shorts; a lamp checks armature-to-core
grounds and bar-to-bar continuity. Test field coils for opens and
grounds with the indicated disconnected leads, replacing faulty coils
or armature after ruling out a loose solder joint.

<a id="p431"></a> **[PDF p.431](../page-images/p0431.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-14 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check positive terminal and insulated brush holder for unintended
ground. Resolder a sound open armature connection only with **resin
flux; never acid flux**. If commutator is rough, turn it only within
the previously machined section, undercut insulation between bars,
clean slots and retest for shorts.

<a id="p432"></a> **[PDF p.432](../page-images/p0432.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-15 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Pack drive-end bearing, assemble felt/seals/spacers, fit brushes and
end frames with their dowels aligned, and oil the porous end bushing.
Support the rear bearing cap while pressing on fan and pulley so races
are not brinelled. Torque the **left-hand pulley nut to 50–60 ft-lb**.
For motoring test, connect battery positive to generator armature and
negative to frame with field grounded; reversed polarity can reverse
residual generator magnetism. Compare current draw with Section 12.

<a id="p433"></a> **[PDF p.433](../page-images/p0433.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-16 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Mount generator without preloading its frame. The base order tightens
drive-end adapter and rear/front bracket bolts to **15–22 ft-lb** and
commutator-end bracket nut to **8–11 ft-lb**. Connect brown lead to
armature, dark-blue to field; a radio bypass condenser belongs on
**armature, not field**. Polarize by briefly jumpering BAT and GEN at
regulator. The base blower **deflection** setting here is superseded
by the 1963 strand-tension setting p.681. Remove regulator for contact
cleaning and mechanical air-gap work; electrical settings are checked
with it installed and warm.

## Regulator contact service and starting motor

<a id="p434"></a> **[PDF p.434](../page-images/p0434.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-17 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove regulator cover and contact support to clean oxidized points.
File the large flat voltage/current contacts with a spoon or riffler
file only enough to expose clean metal; clean the small soft-alloy
contacts and cutout contacts with crocus cloth/fine abrasive, then
flush. **Do not use emery cloth or sandpaper.** Recheck electrical
settings after mechanical adjustment. The factory cutout relay air
gap and point opening are both **.020 in.**; voltage and current
regulator air gaps are **.075 in.**

<a id="p435"></a> **[PDF p.435](../page-images/p0435.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-18 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check current-regulator air gap in the same way as voltage-regulator
gap, then readjust electrical setting. Fit replacement spiral springs
without bending supports. Install regulator with a clean ground and
cushioned mounts, connect BAT black / GEN brown / FIELD dark blue,
and polarize by **momentarily connecting BAT and GEN**. Never attach
a radio bypass condenser to the generator or regulator **field**
terminal; the factory links that error to burned regulator contacts.

<a id="p436"></a> **[PDF p.436](../page-images/p0436.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-19 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The four starter field coils are connected in series. The solenoid's
pull-in and hold-in windings engage the pinion before motor power is
applied. No periodic starter lubrication is prescribed between
overhauls. For slow or failed cranking, measure voltage drops under
load across battery-positive-to-solenoid, battery-negative-to-starter
housing and solenoid-battery-to-motor portions separately.

<a id="p437"></a> **[PDF p.437](../page-images/p0437.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-20 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Any of those starting-circuit drops above **.2 V** indicates excessive
resistance in that segment. Do not crank continuously for more than
**30 seconds**. If solenoid fails to pull in, test control-circuit
voltage drop and terminal voltage; a cool solenoid should pull in at
**7.7 V or more** at its switch terminal. For removal, disconnect
battery ground, then violet S, black battery and yellow R leads,
remove bolts and draw starter forward from the housing.

<a id="p438"></a> **[PDF p.438](../page-images/p0438.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-21 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory exploded starting-motor and solenoid figures establish
orientation of through-bolts, field frame, armature, commutator end,
drive housing, shift lever and overrunning clutch. Retain small
washers, snap rings and brake washer in disassembly order.

<a id="p439"></a> **[PDF p.439](../page-images/p0439.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-22 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Do not use grease-dissolving solvent on the overrunning clutch,
armature or field insulation. The clutch pinion must turn freely in
its overrunning direction and hold in cranking direction. Check
teeth, spring, collar, brush holders and brushes; replace brushes at
**half original length**. Oil drive/end bushings sparingly and inspect
armature commutator and connections. A growler checks armature shorts.

<a id="p440"></a> **[PDF p.440](../page-images/p0440.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-23 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Lamp-test armature for grounds and field coils for open/grounded
windings. Resolder an open sound connection with **resin, not acid,
flux**. If commutator must be turned, stay within previous machined
area, undercut insulation between bars, remove dust, smooth lightly
with No. 00 paper, then retest the armature.

<a id="p441"></a> **[PDF p.441](../page-images/p0441.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-24 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Reassemble starter brush rigging with insulated and grounded holders
and V-springs correctly paired. Lightly oil armature drive splines,
fit overrunning clutch and secure its snap ring inside retainer without
scoring shaft. Install armature/shift lever in drive housing, align
field frame with brushes protected and fit end frame/leather brake
washer. Oil bushings only as the factory specifies. A free-speed
check follows in the source.

## Starter solenoid and ignition circuit

<a id="p442"></a> **[PDF p.442](../page-images/p0442.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-25 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base page describes a free-speed test of a removed starter and
solenoid removal/contact replacement. Its **1961 free-speed range**
must not be used as an unqualified 1963 specification because the
supplement's Section 12 has later starter data. Retain the solenoid
end-cover gasket and the distinction between battery, motor, switch
and ignition-bypass terminals when replacing contacts. The source
tests hold-in and combined winding currents at **10 V**, but verify
the installed starter model against the 1963 table before judging it.

<a id="p443"></a> **[PDF p.443](../page-images/p0443.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-26 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Install solenoid with gasket/seal and reconnect shift linkage. Check
starter pinion clearance after assembly: the base limit is
**.010–.140 in.** There is no clearance adjustment; replace worn
linkage or correct assembly. Distributor and spark plugs require
periodic ignition service; use the **1963 tune-up values in Chapter 7**
and the **redesigned distributor procedure pp.688–692** instead of
base point/distributor instructions. The base ignition-circuit voltage
check begins here and continues on pp.444–445.

<a id="p444"></a> **[PDF p.444](../page-images/p0444.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-27 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

With lamps/accessories off, trace no-spark or start-then-stall faults
through connections, coil secondary, ignition bypass and resistance-
wire circuits using the factory V1–V4 diagram. At the listed test
states, the base table calls for **V1 ≤1 V while cranking**, **V2
approximately battery voltage with points open**, **V2 4½–6½ V with
points closed**, **V3 ≤.2 V** and **V4 ≤.7 V**. It identifies
**1.8 ohms** as the resistance-wire value. Follow the full test
conditions/points-open state on the source page before comparing a
reading; these figures are not interchangeable with charging voltage.

<a id="p445"></a> **[PDF p.445](../page-images/p0445.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-28 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

If the in-car sequence does not isolate the fault, remove and bench-
test distributor, coil and resistance wire. The remainder of this
base page begins the **old distributor** servicing section; use the
supplement's revised assembly instead.

## Spark plugs, ignition switch and wiring-year filter

<a id="p450"></a> **[PDF p.450](../page-images/p0450.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-33 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The distributor-installation procedure on this base page belongs to
the earlier unit and is superseded by the 1963 supplement pp.691–692.
The ignition coil is replaced by disconnecting primary and high-
tension leads, releasing its clamp/support, fitting the replacement
and reconnecting securely. Before spark-plug removal, disconnect wires
and blow debris away from plug wells.

<a id="p451"></a> **[PDF p.451](../page-images/p0451.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-34 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Use a socket that retains the plug so it cannot fall into the engine
shroud; center plugs may require moving a carburetor throttle rod and
using a universal drive. Inspect deposits, glazing, electrode wear and
cracked porcelain. Regap by bending the **ground electrode only**;
never bend the center electrode. The base page's blanket AC-46FF
identification is superseded by Chapter 7's engine-specific 1963 plug
table. The 1963 gap remains **.035 in.**

<a id="p452"></a> **[PDF p.452](../page-images/p0452.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-35 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory diagnosis chart distinguishes flashover at dirty boots,
oil/carbon fouling, glazed deposits, rapid electrode wear and damaged
insulators/shells. Replace damaged plugs and correct the underlying
fuel, ignition or mechanical cause. Clean plug-hole threads carefully,
keep chips out of cylinders and use **new gaskets**. The base page's
**20–25 ft-lb** installation torque is **superseded by the 1963
15–20 ft-lb** table on p.685; do not index the former for 1963.

<a id="p453"></a> **[PDF p.453](../page-images/p0453.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-36 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fully seat spark-plug access covers: loose covers can lose cooling
air pressure and cause overheating. Avoid dirty gasket seats,
corroded threads and overtightening. To remove ignition switch,
disconnect battery ground, release lock cylinder in LOCK with a wire
through its face hole, undo the dash nut and release the theft-
resistant rear connector with the specified clip tool. Refit connector,
switch/nut and cylinder, then reconnect battery.

<a id="p454"></a> **[PDF p.454](../page-images/p0454.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-37 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory fault table maps slow/no cranking to battery condition,
terminals/cables, solenoid, starter internals or engine load. A low
charging rate with a full battery can be normal; otherwise inspect
blower belt, commutator, charging-circuit resistance, regulator
setting/contacts and generator fields. High charging with a full
battery points to regulator setting, contacts/ground or field shorts.

<a id="p455"></a> **[PDF p.455](../page-images/p0455.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-38 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

If the generator lamp stays on with low battery, check belt and open
circuits before regulator or generator. Ignition no-start/hard-start
diagnosis includes battery, wet/broken secondary wiring, cap/rotor,
coil/condenser, point adjustment and plugs. Misfire can also reflect
poor compression. Loose spark-plug covers may cause noise **and engine
overheating**.

## Passenger chassis electrical: lighting and instruments

<a id="p463"></a> **[PDF p.463](../page-images/p0463.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-46 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The passenger chassis section covers headlamps, switches, lamps,
instruments, horn, turn signals, and wipers. For a sealed-beam unit,
remove the four door screws and the retaining spring and ring without
disturbing the aiming screws. Unplug the unit, fit the replacement with
its molded lens number at the top, reinstall the ring and spring, and
replace the door. The **inboard lamp is No. 1**, the outboard **No. 2**.
Aim at curb weight with spare tire, full fuel and oil, recommended tire
pressures, and no passengers; settle the suspension first. Corvair
front-compartment loading lowers the beam.

<a id="p464"></a> **[PDF p.464](../page-images/p0464.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-46 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger duplicate=p463 -->

This physical scan is byte-identical to p.463; its text is represented
once above.

<a id="p465"></a> **[PDF p.465](../page-images/p0465.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-47 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Figures 8-87 through 8-91 show the lamp junction block, retaining
spring, ring screws, sealed beam and connector. Keep the adjusting
screws fixed during lamp replacement.

<a id="p466"></a> **[PDF p.466](../page-images/p0466.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-48 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The T-3 Type B mechanical aimer fits the Corvair's **5¾-inch** sealed
beams. Check its calibration with the factory three-screw fixture; its
horizontal arm must be level. The factory recommends **zero downward
deflection** for Corvair vertical aim, subject to applicable local
headlamp rules.

<a id="p467"></a> **[PDF p.467](../page-images/p0467.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-49 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Center the aimer bubble against the calibrated fixture to verify
vertical calibration; if needed, adjust the aimer's lower screw. The
weighted-string check verifies horizontal calibration. To assess an
aiming floor, mount aimers on a lamp pair, set both sliders to zero,
center the bubbles, reverse the car into the same tire positions, and
check that both bubbles remain within the outer vial lines.

<a id="p468"></a> **[PDF p.468](../page-images/p0468.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-50 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

If the floor is outside aimer tolerance, calibrate both aimers for
that exact floor and direction using the factory half-offset method;
mark Corvair tire positions. For aiming, use an unloaded car at curb
weight, remove doors, mount aimers to one lamp pair, stretch the
elastic string between crossarms, and settle the suspension. Turn
the **horizontal** adjuster to center the string over each crossarm;
finish each adjustment clockwise to remove mechanism play.

<a id="p469"></a> **[PDF p.469](../page-images/p0469.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-51 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For **vertical** aim, set each aimer's DOWN window to **0**. Turn the
lamp adjuster counterclockwise until the bubble reaches the near end,
then clockwise to center it. Recheck string and bubbles; move the
aimers to the other lamp pair and repeat. Refit the lamp doors.

<a id="p470"></a> **[PDF p.470](../page-images/p0470.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-52 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Figure 8-104 provides the alternative **25-foot screen** aiming
layout; use the drawing for its beam geometry. To replace the lighting
switch, disconnect battery ground, pull the knob to ON, depress its
shaft retainer under the dash, remove shaft/knob, ferrule and bezel,
lower the switch, transfer the multi-plug, then reverse assembly and
test. The stoplight switch sits at the brake-pedal bracket; refit its
locknuts and adjust until electrical contact begins after about
**½–¾ inch** of pedal travel from released. For the dimmer switch,
lift the floor mat, unplug the switch, remove its two floor screws,
replace it, and test. The base wiper-switch connector description is
superseded by the 1963 wiper/washer switch and wiring below.

<a id="p471"></a> **[PDF p.471](../page-images/p0471.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-53 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory screen chart shows separate upper- and lower-beam targets
at **25 ft**. Set each beam against its own figure; the lower-beam
adjustment on the No. 2 units also establishes their upper beam. Follow
applicable local aiming requirements if they differ.

<a id="p472"></a> **[PDF p.472](../page-images/p0472.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-54 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger variant=powerglide -->

For the Powerglide neutral safety switch, disconnect the selector pin
retainer and the two switch screws; lower the switch and unplug its
starting and optional back-up circuits. On installation, set the nylon
contact block by the factory scribe-mark method in Figure 8-110,
reconnect the switch, set the selector to **N**, and verify that the
engine cranks only in N. Check back-up lamps if equipped. Figure 8-109
shows the earlier wiper switch; use the 1963 supplement for its
switch and connector arrangement.

<a id="p473"></a> **[PDF p.473](../page-images/p0473.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-55 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For a **three-speed** back-up lamp switch, remove the front tunnel
cover, unplug and remove the switch, install the replacement, then
select reverse and adjust the striker to press the plunger as shown
in Figure 8-111; test before replacing the cover. The glove-box lamp
switch pushes out of its mounting hole after its bulb is released;
transfer terminal and refit bulb. Front parking-lamp bulbs pull out
with their socket from inside the front compartment. For housing
removal, remove headlamp door and parking lens, free housing screws
and underside stud, unplug the headlamp and withdraw the unit.

<a id="p474"></a> **[PDF p.474](../page-images/p0474.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-56 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

<a id="p475"></a> **[PDF p.475](../page-images/p0475.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-57 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For standard-cluster removal, free the light switch and optional
lighter; lower the steering mast or remove the steering wheel as
described by the source, remove **eleven** cluster screws, then draw
cluster and center panel straight out. Refit aligned components and
test every circuit. The generator telltale should light with ignition

alone may be normal. The oil-pressure/head-temperature telltale also
lights before starting. If it remains lit, inspect oil level and
temperature, then verify oil pressure with a gauge and inspect sender
circuit. A dark lamp before starting calls for bulb, wiring and sender
ground checks. The fuel-gauge circuit is sensitive to resistive or
corroded connections.

<a id="p476"></a> **[PDF p.476](../page-images/p0476.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-58 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

all sender terminals before condemning it.

<a id="p477"></a> **[PDF p.477](../page-images/p0477.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-59 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

service, disconnect both ends, withdraw the old core, apply a **thin

do not over-lubricate. Speedometer-head repair requires specialist
equipment. For a horn that will not operate, first verify the battery,

checks further isolate poor grounds and high resistance. The source

<a id="p478"></a> **[PDF p.478](../page-images/p0478.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=8-60 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

## Battery and redesigned distributor

<a id="p687"></a> **[PDF p.687](../page-images/p0687.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory specifies a **nine-plate-per-cell, 42 Ah battery** for
standard production. Battery service otherwise follows the base
manual. For slow charging, correct electrolyte level with distilled
water and charge at **5 A for at least 10 hours**. Three hourly
hydrometer readings with no rise show full charge; charging may take
**24 hours or more**. On a fully charged battery, cell specific-gravity
readings of **1.230–1.310** are considered ready for use under this
supplement's hydrometer test.

| 1963 standard battery | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Plates per cell | Nine |
| Capacity | 42 Ah |

The turbocharged engine uses a **pressure-retard distributor** rather
than the ordinary vacuum-advance unit. Manifold pressure retards spark
and opposes some centrifugal advance at high speed. Identify the
installed variant before applying the distributor curve; the source
curve continues on p.688.

<a id="p688"></a> **[PDF p.688](../page-images/p0688.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-2 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Figure 8-2 is the factory turbocharged distributor spark curve. For
contact-point replacement on the redesigned distributor, release cap,
remove rotor, unplug primary/condenser leads from the point quick-
disconnect and remove the point-set screw. The illustrations identify
the breaker plate, spring-tension check and point-opening adjustment.

<a id="p689"></a> **[PDF p.689](../page-images/p0689.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-3 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Clean breaker plate, wipe protective film from new points and seat the
point pilot in the breaker-plate hole. Reattach primary/condenser leads,
check alignment and spring tension, and set new point gap to **.019 in.**
Rotate the cam lubricator **90°**, reinstall rotor/cap and set timing
under Chapter 7. For condenser replacement, remove cap/rotor, unplug
its lead, remove screw and condenser, clean plate and reverse assembly.

For complete distributor removal, mark rotor position against housing,
disconnect coil primary and vacuum/pressure line, note control-unit
position, release clamp and lift out. **Do not rotate the engine** while
it is out if preserving the original timing. A distributor test machine
requires a protective adapter over the lower shaft to avoid scoring it.

<a id="p690"></a> **[PDF p.690](../page-images/p0690.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-4 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The redesigned distributor disassembles into breaker plate, condenser,
point set, vacuum/pressure control, gear, shaft, weights, springs and
cam as Figure 8-7 shows. Keep solvents off cap, rotor, condenser,
breaker plate, cam lubricator and control unit. The housing bushing is
**not serviced separately**. Check shaft runout in V-blocks: **.002 in.
maximum**. Check weights, pins, cam and shaft for free action/wear;
test condenser with proper equipment. Lubricate shaft, reinstall
weights and springs, cover, thrust washers and gear, then confirm free
shaft rotation.

<a id="p691"></a> **[PDF p.691](../page-images/p0691.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Install driven gear with its hub mark aligned to the rotor segment.
Refit breaker plate, points and condenser with point pilot seated;
attach control unit in the **upper mounting holes**, cam lubricator and
rotor. If the engine was not moved, start the rotor slightly before
its scribed position so gear engagement brings it to the mark; place
the control unit at its recorded attitude. Secure clamp and wires, set
point gap and timing. Route plug/coil wires as the factory figure shows
to prevent cross-firing. If the engine moved, find No. 1 compression
stroke and the crankshaft **zero** mark before installing distributor.

<a id="p692"></a> **[PDF p.692](../page-images/p0692.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

With No. 1 at firing TDC, seat distributor so oil-pump shaft engages,
align rotor to No. 1 cap terminal, turn body until points just open,
secure clamp and connect leads. Set final timing under Chapter 7.

## 1963 passenger wiper and washer

The standard 1962–63 passenger wiper uses a **single-speed electric
motor** and non-depressed park, with blades about **2 in. above the
windshield moulding**. An optional two-speed non-depressed-park motor
uses the new gear train. The dash and cam-operated park switches are
in parallel. The transmission linkage removal is carried over from
the base manual; the supplement changes motor/gearbox construction.

For gearbox disassembly, remove washer pump/cam if equipped, crank arm,
seal cap, retaining ring, washer/shims and riveted cover. Mark ground
strap location. Lift output gear/shaft and intermediate gear, noting
its wave washer.

<a id="p693"></a> **[PDF p.693](../page-images/p0693.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Figure 8-10 identifies wiper gearbox and motor parts. For terminal
board/park-switch removal, unsolder leads and drill its mounting
rivets, retaining the ground-strap position. To open motor, remove
through bolts, lift frame from mounting plate and release the brush
springs; continue on p.694.

<a id="p694"></a> **[PDF p.694](../page-images/p0694.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-8 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Lift armature and end plate, preserving thrust plug and end-play wave-
washer order. Inspect parts, replacing damaged units; motor frame and
field are serviced together. Oil armature-shaft bushings lightly;
lubricate gears with Delco cam and ball-bearing lubricant. Reinstall
ground strap and cover on dowels. Run motor to park, then align crank-
arm marks with cover marks before securing its nut.

<a id="p695"></a> **[PDF p.695](../page-images/p0695.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-9 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The new positive-displacement washer pump uses a four-lobe cam,
bellows, spring, valves and a relay/ratchet programmer. Mark hoses,
unplug harness and remove pump bracket. On installation, place the
harness **battery lead on the terminal without a tang**; reversal
directly grounds the circuit and destroys the wiper-motor fuse. The
factory figure identifies relay, pawl and valve/bellows assembly.

<a id="p696"></a> **[PDF p.696](../page-images/p0696.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-10 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

To remove bellows, manually turn pump clockwise out of lockout, hold
plunger arm, press/twist bellows **90°** and lift it with spring. Reverse
for assembly. The source begins single-speed circuit troubleshooting
here. If a motor stalls, turning off the dash switch may not remove
power until blades are manually parked or motor cools; allow it to
recover before testing.

## Wiper and washer electrical diagnosis

<a id="p697"></a> **[PDF p.697](../page-images/p0697.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-11 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For an inoperative single-speed wiper, turn ignition on, check fuse,
ground strap, dash switch mounting and harness first. The factory test
looks for **12 V at the harness terminal feeding wiper terminal 2**.
Do **not** put the hot lead on terminal 1. Power terminal 2 and ground
terminal 1 with a jumper to isolate the dash switch/wiring; if still
inoperative, disconnect the wiper transmission to isolate mechanical
binding, then bench-test the motor. A detached motor drawing **0 A**
points to an open connection; **2–3 A** to armature/brush/splice faults;
**10–12 A** to a shunt-field or gear fault as the factory table lists.
For failure to shut off, disconnect dash-switch lead, then test the
park-switch circuit independently; a grounded terminal-1 lead can
keep the motor running.

<a id="p698"></a> **[PDF p.698](../page-images/p0698.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-12 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For intermittent single-speed operation, inspect ground, switch,
splices and brushes. If blades fail to park, check ground and park-
switch contacts. Slow running at **7–9 A** may indicate gearbox bind,
shorted armature or dry armature bearings. If motor stops before its
crank-arm index reaches park, inspect park contacts. For the optional
two-speed unit use an ammeter rated **at least 30 A** and check both
speeds, fuse, ground, switch, harness and terminal-2 power before
isolating motor from wiper linkage.

<a id="p699"></a> **[PDF p.699](../page-images/p0699.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-13 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Two-speed parking uses **low speed** even when high was selected.
If it will not shut off, determine which speeds still operate, then
bench-test independently of the dash switch using factory figure
8-22. A normal bench result points to switch/vehicle wiring; a failed
bench result points to park contacts, terminal leads or shunt field.
High-speed-only operation can indicate an open terminal-3 lead or
shunt field. Low-speed-only with excessively fast high speed suggests
checking the **24-ohm** terminal-board resistor. For no return to
park, inspect ground strap and contacts.

<a id="p700"></a> **[PDF p.700](../page-images/p0700.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-14 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For an inoperative washer, check fluid, hoses, jar screen, nozzles,
connections and dash switch. Run wiper and push washer button; listen
for relay click. If absent, check **12 V at washer terminal 2**. If
present, ground terminal 1 as factory figure 8-25 shows to isolate
switch wiring from relay coil. Relay click without pump action points
to ratchet/cam/valve mechanism. The detached-pump test checks pawl
engagement, holding contacts and lockout after a full ratchet cycle.
This diagnostic page twice calls its rotating test cam **three-lobe**.
The assembly description (printed p. 8-9, [PDF p.695](../page-images/p0695.png))
and labeled fig. 8-18 ([PDF p.696](../page-images/p0696.png)) identify
the washer drive as **four-lobe**. Chevrolet's October 1962 *Service News*,
[p. 14](https://corvanatics.org/files/maintenance-repair/repair-manuals-bulletins/chevy-service-news/CSN-34-9.pdf#page=14),
independently specifies a four-lobe direct drive for **all 1963 wiper motors**.
Treat “three-lobe” on this diagnostic page as the inconsistent factory wording;
identify the installed pump before applying the procedure.
<!-- RESOLVED SOURCE CONFLICT: Supplement Section 8 p.8-14 twice says three-lobe, but p.8-9 and fig.8-18 on p.8-10 identify the washer drive as four-lobe. October 1962 Chevrolet Service News p.14 explicitly specifies four-lobe direct drive for all 1963 wiper motors. -->

<a id="p701"></a> **[PDF p.701](../page-images/p0701.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-15 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The valve assembly should pass air **into** its large intake pipe but
not back out, and pass air **out of** each small discharge pipe but not
back in. Flow both ways through any valve means replace the assembly.
For continuous washer operation, inspect contact opening at the
ratchet ramp, relay coil, pawl spring and ratchet brake. The factory
two-speed bench diagram distinguishes low, high and off jumper
connections; use it with the actual terminal numbers rather than
substituting the single-speed hookup.

## Monza Spyder warning instruments

<a id="p702"></a> **[PDF p.702](../page-images/p0702.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=8-16 years=1963 scope=passenger variant=spyder -->

The optional Monza Spyder cluster adds a cylinder-head-temperature
gauge and overheat buzzer to standard warning lamps, plus tachometer
and manifold-pressure gauge. Remove the cluster before servicing an
individual instrument. With ignition on, temperature gauge indicates
head temperature. **Low oil pressure lights TEMP-PRESS only**;
**excess temperature lights TEMP-PRESS and sounds the buzzer**. A
silicon rectifier in the harness allows temperature-switch current to
light the lamp while preventing oil-pressure-switch current from
completing the buzzer circuit. Tachometer connects at the coil.

**thermistor/gauge sender in the left cylinder head** and the warning
temperature switch in the **right head**; buzzer and diode are under
the dash, with the diode taped into the harness. Keep the oil-pressure
and temperature-switch circuits distinct during diagnosis.
