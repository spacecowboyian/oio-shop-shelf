# Engine Tune-Up — 1963 Passenger Cars

Use the 1961 factory sequence for tests and inspection, then the
**1962–63 supplement** for distributor, blower-belt, carburetor, automatic
choke and engine-specific tune-up settings. The supplement explicitly
replaces those procedures and its table supersedes the 1961 tune-up
table. Select values for the installed engine and transmission. The
turbocharged engine's separate carburetor service is in Section 9.
Original files, hashes and combined PDF page numbers are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json).

## Mechanical and electrical preparation

<a id="p405"></a> **[PDF p.405](../page-images/p0405.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-1 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory tune-up order begins with mechanical and visual checks,
then uses instruments after the engine has warmed. Investigate defects
found during preparation before trying to correct idle and ignition by
adjustment alone. The 1961 index separates passenger pages 7-1–7-12
from Corvair 95/Greenbrier commercial material on 7-13.

<a id="p406"></a> **[PDF p.406](../page-images/p0406.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For compression testing, clear dirt around plugs, loosen them, briefly
run the engine to expel loose carbon, then remove plugs. Hold throttle
and choke wide open and crank each cylinder through **at least four
compression strokes**. The supplement's 1963 table below requires
**130 psi** with **no more than 20 psi variation** between cylinders.
Because the cylinders are horizontal, adding oil can pool along the
lower wall and mislead a ring-versus-valve retest; use vacuum and other
diagnostics as well. Clean and inspect plugs, replace damaged ones,
and fit new sealing washers. Plug type, gap and torque come from the
1963 table, not the base 1961 chart.

<a id="p407"></a> **[PDF p.407](../page-images/p0407.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect ignition wires, cap, rotor, centrifugal advance return and
vacuum-breaker-plate motion. Clean slightly dirty points with a clean
fine-cut contact file; avoid abrasive cloth and replace badly burned,
pitted or misaligned used points. The supplement's redesigned distributor
changes the point/condenser removal and cap orientation; follow p.681
below for those steps and 1963 gap/dwell settings.

<a id="p408"></a> **[PDF p.408](../page-images/p0408.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check battery case, hold-down, electrolyte and cables for leaks,
corrosion, looseness and damage. Clean and reconnect the terminals
securely. Inspect generator commutator, brushes and wiring; replace
brushes below **half original length**. Lubricate distributor cam,
breaker pivot and generator at their specified points. The base page's
blower-belt **deflection** method is superseded by the 1963 strand-
tension gauge setting below. Avoid generator preload during belt work.

<a id="p409"></a> **[PDF p.409](../page-images/p0409.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Inspect fuel lines/filter and service air cleaners **before** idle or
mixture adjustment. The 1961 manual-choke carburetor setup on this page
is superseded by the 1962–63 automatic-choke sequence on pp.682–685.
Float-level and drop work remains in Section 9.

<a id="p410"></a> **[PDF p.410](../page-images/p0410.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base linkage drawing identifies carburetor cross-shaft, rods and
swivels. Use that layout to find the parts, but perform the **supplement
synchronization** below, including its extra left-rod turn and newer
vacuum test points.

<a id="p411"></a> **[PDF p.411](../page-images/p0411.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base page explains equal adjustment of the paired idle screws and
final mixture adjustment with air cleaners fitted. Its manual-choke
cable and spark-port vacuum test are superseded; do not use them to set
the 1963 automatic chokes or balance the carburetors.

<a id="p412"></a> **[PDF p.412](../page-images/p0412.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The remainder of the **manual choke cable** adjustment is 1961-only
for this reference. With engine warm, inspect cooling-air damper doors
for free travel and bellows operation; the measurement continues on
the next page.

<a id="p413"></a> **[PDF p.413](../page-images/p0413.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The warm-engine exhaust/cooling-air damper opening is given in the
1963 table below. Ensure engine shrouds fit, blower/air inlets are
clear and crankcase oil level/leaks are checked. Warm the engine in
Neutral before instrument checks. **Do not retorque cylinder heads**
as a routine tune-up step. The base dwell-check method is retained,
but use the supplement's **31°–34°** target. Dwell changing more than
**3°** as speed rises to 1500 rpm points to distributor shaft, bushing
or breaker-plate wear.

<a id="p414"></a> **[PDF p.414](../page-images/p0414.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check timing with light on No. 1 plug at idle; loosen distributor clamp
only to adjust, then secure it. Use the **engine/transmission-specific
1963 timing table below** with vacuum advance disconnected, as the
supplement specifies. Compare advance against Section 12 after timing;
if total advance is wrong, isolate centrifugal from vacuum operation.
For cylinder balance, test firing-order pairs **1–2, 4–3, 5–6** at
1500 rpm with the other plugs grounded. More than **1 in. Hg** or
**40 rpm** difference between pairs suggests an unbalanced cylinder
pair requiring further diagnosis.

<a id="p415"></a> **[PDF p.415](../page-images/p0415.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=7-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For poor high-speed fuel delivery, direct one carburetor fuel line into
a measured container and plug the other; run briefly on bowl fuel.
The supplement's 1963 target is **one pint in 30–45 seconds**. If flow
is poor, inspect tank strainer and clear both lines before condemning
the pump. With a pressure gauge attached, the factory target is
**4–5 lb pressure** from idle to 1000 rpm. Reconnect and leak-check
both lines. Starting, generating and ignition circuit tests are in
Section 8.

The **1961-only tune-up table** is superseded by p.685 below. Its
manual-choke, spark-plug torque, belt-deflection and air-cleaner
interval values are not indexed as 1963 specifications.

## 1963 distributor, belt and carburetor setup

<a id="p681"></a> **[PDF p.681](../page-images/p0681.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

On the redesigned distributor, remove cap, lift primary/condenser leads
from their clip, remove condenser bracket and points. Install leads
**between the insulator and contact-point spring arm**. Set points
**.019 in. new / .016 in. used**, check spring tension, turn cam-
lubricator wick **½ turn**, and reinstall cap with its **notch aligned
to the vacuum-advance lever opening**. Belt removal is unchanged; set
idler using a **strand tension gauge to 75 ±5 lb**.

<a id="p682"></a> **[PDF p.682](../page-images/p0682.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-2 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For paired Rochester H carburetors, engine off, disconnect both
throttle rods and choke control rods. At each carburetor, clear the
fast-idle cam, back off idle screw, insert a **.003 in.** feeler between
idle screw and lever, bring screw just to the gauge and turn it **1½
more turns**. Seat each mixture screw **lightly** and back out **1½
turns**. Attach right throttle rod. Rotate cross-shaft to positively
close right throttle, lengthen/shorten left rod until it enters its
swivel freely, then **shorten one additional turn** and clip it.
Keep subsequent idle adjustments equal at both carburetors. Warm
engine, check timing, connect tachometer and a vacuum gauge to the
balance-tube adapter; set preliminary idle to the applicable value in
the 1963 table and adjust both mixture screws for peak steady vacuum.

<a id="p683"></a> **[PDF p.683](../page-images/p0683.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-3 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

For balance, remove each choke-diaphragm hose at its carburetor-base
adapter and measure each bank's idle vacuum with equally calibrated
gauges. **Do not turn the tubing in the carburetor body.** A difference
of **1 in. Hg or less** is synchronized. If greater, change **only the
left throttle rod** one swivel turn at a time; up increases its vacuum,
down decreases. The factory prefers the right side slightly higher.
Move the cross-shaft by its **accelerator rod**, not other linkage.
Reconnect diaphragm hoses and fit air cleaners before final mixture.
The [factory linkage figure](../diagrams/p0683-carburetor-linkage.png)
identifies the rods, cross-shaft and adapters.

<a id="p684"></a> **[PDF p.684](../page-images/p0684.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-4 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

With air cleaners fitted, adjust both idle screws equally for the
highest steady balance-tube vacuum, **14–18 in. Hg**, at specified idle
speed. After curb idle is set, stop engine, put throttle lever on the
fast-idle cam's **second-highest step** and bend its tang for **.078 in.**
between idle-speed screw and throttle lever. Restart and recheck idle.

<a id="p685"></a> **[PDF p.685](../page-images/p0685.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Hold choke vacuum diaphragm arm squarely to its housing; measure
**.160–.175 in.** from choke-valve lower edge to air-horn wall, bending
its link if needed. At that setting the fast-idle tang must rest on
the second-highest cam step. For choke control rod adjustment, engine
off, hold choke closed and the rod at its thermostat bracket stop;
turn the **vertical** rod portion until it freely enters the choke-
shaft lever. After warmup the choke valve must open and fast-idle cam
clear the throttle. For unloader, hold throttle wide open and bend
throttle-lever tang for **.250 in.** lower-edge choke clearance.

throttle wide open. Timing is measured **with vacuum advance
disconnected**.

The GM [1963 vehicle information kit, Power Trains p. 11](https://www.gm.com/content/dam/company/no_search/heritage-archive-docs/vehicle-information-kits/chevrolet/1963-Chevrolet-Corvair.pdf#page=48),

confirm this gap for the 1963 passenger engines. The supplement's Section 12
electrical chart prints a different range; see the
[source comparison](12-specifications.md#1963-spark-plug-gap).

The [1963 lubrication schedule](02-lubrication.md#p637-cooling) sets
air-cleaner service at **6,000 miles** for regular and turbocharged
engines. It supersedes the Spyder section's earlier interval.

The paired standard carburetors are Rochester Model H; the turbocharged
engine uses a Carter YH side-draft carburetor. The crankcase-vent valve
is specified as **AC-CV584** for applications shown by the source;
verify the installed equipment before replacement.

<a id="p686"></a> **[PDF p.686](../page-images/p0686.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=7-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

All 1963 Chevrolet engines have positive or closed positive crankcase
ventilation. Closed systems take clean air from the carburetor air
cleaner and use a non-vented oil-filler cap. **Do not interchange
ventilation valves between engines**: flow calibration differs. Clean
the air cleaner and test the valve at its maintenance interval. With
tachometer and vacuum gauge attached and parking brake set, note idle
rpm, disconnect ventilation hose on the air-cleaner side of the valve,
block the valve opening and note rpm change. **Less than 50 rpm change**
means its metering hole is plugged; replace the valve and readjust
idle speed/mixture.
The [factory ventilation figure](../diagrams/p0686-crankcase-ventilation.png)
shows the closed-system routing.
