# Engine Mechanical — 1963 Passenger Cars

<!-- INCOMPLETE: The 1962–63 supplement's engine changes are being integrated here. Base 1961 Section 6A procedures across 61 physical pages remain to be reconstructed and filtered for 1963 passenger variants. Do not treat this chapter as complete. -->

The supplement says the 1963 Corvair engine keeps the 1961 basic design.
Its 1963 changes include automatic choke controls, revised shrouding,
late-style Monza combustion chambers where equipped, and an updated
distributor. Base mechanical service continues where these changes do not
supersede it. The turbocharged Monza Spyder is a **1963 passenger-car
variant** and is included with explicit applicability. Commercial-only
Corvair 95 operations are excluded.

The original source filenames and hashes are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json). The base engine section
occupies combined PDF pp. 246–306; the supplement occupies pp. 659–667.

## 1963 engine changes and carburetor access

<a id="p659"></a> **[PDF p.659](../page-images/p0659.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The automatic choke controls fuel mixture during warmup; its detailed
service appears in the factory Fuel and Exhaust section. Engine
shrouding provides access to the choke thermostat at the lower side of the
head. The Monza **9:1 compression** engine uses the revised combustion
chamber introduced during 1961, with filled-in roof surfaces near valves
and spark-plug holes. The 1963 distributor service changes belong to the
electrical chapter.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Monza 9:1 engine | 9:1 |

When removing a carburetor for access to another part on the regular
two-carburetor engine, remove **both carburetors and the cross-shaft as an
assembly** to preserve synchronization. Remove air cleaners and cross-tube;
disconnect accelerator rod and right-carburetor distributor spark hose;
disconnect choke rods and fuel lines; remove two hold-down nuts per
carburetor; and lift off the assembly. Reverse the operation and **check
carburetor synchronization** after installation. The Spyder's single
side-draft carburetor has different service arrangements.

## Revised engine sheet metal

<a id="p660"></a> **[PDF p.660](../page-images/p0660.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the upper shroud, remove air-cleaner assemblies/supports, fuel lines,
spark-advance hose, accelerator and choke rods, and both carburetors with
cross-shaft. Remove blower belt, spark-plug wires, and vacuum balance tube.
Detach generator outboard bracket from the shroud and its front pulley-end
bolt; swing generator rearward. Disconnect heater hose at the compartment
wall **if equipped**. Remove remaining shroud screws; lift the front edge
first and rotate clockwise to clear oil filter and generator adapter.
During installation turn the blower as screws are tightened to verify
blower-to-shroud clearance. Tighten upper-shroud screws to **30–40 in. lb.**,
restore removed parts, and check carburetor synchronization.

<a id="p661"></a> **[PDF p.661](../page-images/p0661.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-3 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The exploded view identifies separate upper, front, lower, rear, and side
shrouds; cylinder air baffles; exhaust ducts and thermostatic dampers;
oil cooler; seals; and direct-air-heater connections where equipped.
See the [engine shrouding exploded view](../diagrams/p0661-engine-shrouding.png).
For front-shield removal, disconnect battery ground, air-cleaner supports,
vacuum tube and heater hose (if fitted), front seal retainer, and accelerator
rod. Move the front shield rearward to reach starter-solenoid and fuel-line
connections. Disconnect tank fuel line at pump, rear hose connection, battery
cable at solenoid and quick-disconnect wires. Pass lines and wires through
their grommet openings and remove the shield over accelerator rod and boot.
Reverse the procedure to install, restoring grommets and shields.

<a id="p662"></a> **[PDF p.662](../page-images/p0662.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-4 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The lower shroud separates from exhaust duct/damper, permitting access to
oil-pressure and temperature switches and the 1963 choke-stove unit without
removing the duct. On either side, loosen thermostat screws and remove
sheet-metal screws, then eight shroud-to-head/crankcase-cap screws. Lower
and shift duct rearward enough to release thermostat tension and disconnect
its rod from the damper door. Reverse removal, guiding the road-draft tube
through the right-side opening and checking damper adjustment by the base
procedure.

<a id="p663"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1963 lower shroud has thermostat bracket and heat shield **riveted**
to the shroud. To replace either, remove the lower-shroud assembly and
thermostat, drill out the rivets, then attach the replacement part with
specified **¼-20 bolt and self-locking nut**. For thermostat replacement,
remove lower shroud and rod, remove stop bolt, lift thermostat out, then
reverse installation.

For exhaust-duct service, remove lower shroud and rear seal/retainer.
On the left remove oil-cooler and rear-center-shield screws; on the right,
coil and muffler bracket. Remove remaining duct screws and duct, reverse
to install, and check damper setting. The page's Greenbrier, Lakewood
station-wagon, and van removal note does **not** describe the 1963 passenger
sedan/coupe reference.

To remove the front shroud, first remove lower shroud and exhaust manifold,
then its front-shield screws. On the right remove muffler-shield bolts;
detach heater hoses if direct-air heat is fitted. Remove remaining bolts,
including the left bolt inside heater-duct opening, and take out the
shroud. Reverse to install and check the exhaust-duct damper.

## Exhaust valves, guides, push rods, and hydraulic lifters

<a id="p663-valve"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

All 1963 Corvair engines use exhaust-valve locks with **four-bead grooves**.
This includes the turbocharged Spyder; the supplement says that engine
received the design in 1962. Install the new locks by the same method as
the earlier single-groove type, but match locks to their valve stems.

See the [valve-lock comparison](../diagrams/p0663-exhaust-valve-locks.png).

<a id="p664"></a> **[PDF p.664](../page-images/p0664.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Straight-stem exhaust valves introduced during 1961 carry into 1963.
Service oversizes are **.003, .010, and .020 in.**; Chevrolet identifies
**J-5830-5** as the .020-in. reamer. Measure stem diameter one inch from
tip and guide bore at the port end; subtract stem diameter from guide
diameter. Alternatively, measure side play with an indicator at the valve
stem just above the guide and valve head approximately 1⁄16 in. off its seat.
If clearance exceeds the worn limit, select the next oversize valve and
ream the guide to correct new clearance.

| Valve stem-to-guide clearance | New | Worn limit |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | .001–.0027 in. | .004 in. |
| Exhaust | .0014–.0029 in. | .006 in. |

Install each push rod with the **side oil hole and paint-marked end toward
the rocker arm**. For a new or rebuilt hydraulic lifter, submerge it in
engine oil and work its plunger several times before installation. The
separate lower shroud permits lifter access; use the base lifter-removal
method after removing that shroud.

### Running valve-lash adjustment

With the engine at normal operating temperature, replace each valve cover
temporarily with a cut-out cover and gasket to catch oil. At idle, back
off one rocker nut until it clatters, then tighten slowly until clatter
just stops: **zero lash**. Tighten **¼ turn**, pause **10 seconds** for the
lifter to settle, and repeat in quarter turns with pauses until the nut
has moved **one full turn from zero lash**. Perform this slowly: the source
warns that faster preload can bring an intake valve into contact with a
piston or bend a push rod. Replace noisy lifters, adjust remaining valves,
and restore the valve covers with new gaskets.

### Blower-bearing hub height

The base replacement procedure carries forward, but the **1963 location
dimension** from hub to lower edge of crankcase cover is **4.475 ± .015
in.** The base dimension is superseded.

| 1963 blower bearing installation dimension | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Hub to lower edge of crankcase cover | 4.475 ± .015 in. |

## Oil filter, cylinder heads, and senders

<a id="p665"></a> **[PDF p.665](../page-images/p0665.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

On an **air-conditioned** car, support the filter and adapter elbow while
removing the bolt through generator/filter adapter plate. Lift the filter
and elbow together to limit oil spillage, then remove the filter mounting
bolt and separate them. Discard the filter, clean the elbow, replace all
gaskets and fit a new filter; reinstall in reverse order.

The automatic-choke control rod passes through the 1963 cylinder head and
must be removed before head removal. Other head service uses the applicable
base procedure with year-specific dimensions from Section 12.

The **late-1962 and 1963 oil-pressure switch** sits at the **top of the
oil-filter adapter, left of the fuel pump**. Disconnect its lead, replace
the switch with a six-point deep socket, and reconnect the lead. The
separate retrofit wiring/plug instructions on the following page concern
**1961 and early-1962 cars only** and are excluded from 1963 service.

<a id="p666"></a> **[PDF p.666](../page-images/p0666.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-8 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1962–63 rear main bearing uses a thrust-flanged **left bearing half**
and **unflanged right half** in the crankcase to prevent mismatched thrust
flanges. Match the halves to their sides as shown in the source's
Figure 6A-11.

See the [rear-main-bearing illustration](../diagrams/p0666-rear-main-bearing.png).

## Turbocharged Monza Spyder variant

<a id="p667"></a> **[PDF p.667](../page-images/p0667.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-9 years=1963 scope=passenger variant=spyder -->

The optional Spyder engine is a matched turbocharged assembly. Chevrolet
explicitly warns **not to move its turbocharger onto another Corvair
engine**. The Spyder uses heavy-duty main and rod bearings, stronger
connecting rods, variant-specific rings, crankshaft and cylinder heads,
and **8:1 compression**. Its left head carries the temperature-gauge
thermistor; its right head has a turbo oil drain. External differences
include a single side-draft Carter YH carburetor, variant fuel lines,
distributor and timing tab, exhaust plumbing, heat shields, wiring for
temperature warning, and turbo oil-feed components.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Turbocharged Monza Spyder | 8:1 |

See the [factory Spyder engine illustration](../diagrams/p0667-monza-spyder-engine.png).

For Spyder upper-shroud removal, first remove turbo assembly with its
carburetor/air cleaner, inlet and exhaust piping, oil lines, and diffuser,
then use the regular-engine shroud procedure. The lower shrouds use the
regular procedure. The left side shield requires fuel-filter removal and
manifold-pressure-line disconnection; the right needs exhaust-insulator
clearance. For front shield/seal retainer removal, remove turbo and fuel
filter, disconnect accelerator linkage and tank line, then use the base
procedure. Its right seal is heat-resistant insulation: seat it along its
full length when reinstalling. Spyder air-recirculation plates are removed
in winter and installed in summer. Turbocharger and fuel/air service
continues in Section 9 of the factory supplement.
