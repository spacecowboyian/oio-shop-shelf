# Engine Mechanical — 1963 Passenger Cars

The supplement says the 1963 Corvair engine keeps the 1961 basic design.
Its 1963 changes include automatic choke controls, revised shrouding,
late-style Monza combustion chambers where equipped, and an updated
distributor. Base mechanical service continues where these changes do not
supersede it. The turbocharged Monza Spyder is a **1963 passenger-car
variant** and is included with explicit applicability. Commercial-only
Corvair 95 operations are excluded.

The original source filenames and hashes are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json). The base engine section
occupies combined PDF pp. 246–306; the supplement occupies pp. 659–667.

## Engine design and service orientation carried forward

<a id="p247"></a> **[PDF p.247](../page-images/p0247.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The Turbo-Air is an air-cooled, horizontally opposed **six-cylinder** engine
with a vertically split aluminum crankcase and individual cast-iron
cylinders. Long studs pass through each cylinder and head into the
crankcase. Opposed aluminum heads carry integral intake passages. The
camshaft sits below the crankshaft and operates the valves through hydraulic
lifters, push rods, and rocker arms. For 1963, use the supplement and
year-specific specification table for compression ratio and internal
dimensions of each engine variant; the base introduction predates the
1963 engine lineup.

<a id="p248"></a> **[PDF p.248](../page-images/p0248.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Steel push-rod tubes protect the rods between crankcase and heads and drain
oil from the heads back to the sump. Hydraulic lifters follow cam lobes
within the crankcase; the base cross-section shows the opposed-cylinder,
head, and shroud layout.

<a id="p249"></a> **[PDF p.249](../page-images/p0249.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The camshaft journals run in machined crankcase bores and its thrust is
controlled at the gear end. The forged crankshaft has paired throws and
four main bearings supported by the crankcase halves. Timing gears connect
the crankshaft and camshaft. Each aluminum piston has two compression rings
and an oil-control assembly. Refer to the original cross-sections for
component orientation before teardown.

<a id="p250"></a> **[PDF p.250](../page-images/p0250.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The powertrain uses two front transmission mounts at the rear crossmember
and one centered engine rear mount. In installed orientation, the flywheel
is at the **front** of the engine and cylinders are numbered from the rear:
**right bank 1-3-5, left bank 2-4-6**. Firing order is **1-4-5-2-3-6**.

The rear engine housing contains the gear-driven oil pump, regulator,
crankshaft seal, and main oil passages. Oil flows from sump pickup through
pump, full-flow filter, air-cooled cooler, crankcase galleries, bearings,
and hydraulic lifters. The 1963 oil-pressure switch is at the location
given in [the supplement](#oil-filter-cylinder-heads-and-senders).

<a id="p251"></a> **[PDF p.251](../page-images/p0251.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory lubrication diagram traces the distinct passages through the
rear housing, filter, cooler, and crankcase. Its filler location marked
for the coupe and sedan is the passenger-car arrangement in this manual.
The station-wagon and Corvair 95 filler routing in the diagram does not
apply to this reference.

<a id="p252"></a> **[PDF p.252](../page-images/p0252.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Right and left main galleries feed crankshaft and camshaft bearings and
hydraulic lifters. Oil passes through the crankshaft to connecting-rod
bearings and through hollow push rods to rocker arms, valve springs, and
stems. Keep the push-rod **side oil hole toward the rocker arm**, as the
supplement emphasizes. A louvered crankcase-vent plate and separation
chamber fit between gaskets beneath the crankcase cover. Cooling air from
the centrifugal blower moves through the engine shrouding over cylinder
and head fins.

<a id="p253"></a> **[PDF p.253](../page-images/p0253.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Keep engine oil between dipstick **ADD** and **FULL** marks; do not overfill.
The base engine-oil, belt, ignition-timing, compression-check, and tune-up
instructions refer to their other factory sections. For running valve lash,
the supplement's [one-turn-from-zero procedure](#running-valve-lash-adjustment)
supersedes the different base preload turns. The base diagnostic method
for locating a noisy hydraulic lifter uses a listening tube or a finger
on the valve-spring retainer to distinguish its shock from other noises.

<a id="p254"></a> **[PDF p.254](../page-images/p0254.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Hard lifter rapping can indicate a varnished or galled plunger; moderate
rapping can indicate excessive leakdown, check-valve leakage, or wrong lash.
General valve-train noise can indicate low oil supply or lash error.
Intermittent clicking may come from check-ball contamination. Keep lifter
plungers with their matched bodies; replace a damaged pair as a complete
unit. Check that each clean plunger falls freely by its own weight and that
the check valve seals.

Keep oil off engine shrouding so fumes are not carried into the passenger
compartment. Investigate push-rod-tube, oil-cooler, filter-adapter, valve-
cover, crankcase-cover, distributor, housing, and exhaust-sleeve seals for
leaks. Clean cylinder/head fins and clear the oil-cooler air passages of
leaves, chaff, and other debris. Use the 1963 lubrication schedule for
maintenance intervals.

## Oil-cooler cleaning and exhaust-damper setting

<a id="p255"></a> **[PDF p.255](../page-images/p0255.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory service-reference matrix identifies parts to remove before
servicing a selected engine component. Its **1961 sheet-metal access paths
must be read with the supplement's revised 1963 shrouds**; the original
[matrix page](../page-images/p0255.png) is preserved for that purpose.

<a id="p256"></a> **[PDF p.256](../page-images/p0256.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The service-reference matrix continues across this landscape page. It
distinguishes parts removed for easy access from the component actually
being serviced. The 1963 lower-shroud and choke changes override its older
shroud-removal cells.

<a id="p257"></a> **[PDF p.257](../page-images/p0257.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

To clear debris from the oil cooler, remove its upper-shroud access cover,
brush off loose material, and blow air **upward through the cooler fins**
from below while the exhaust damper door is held open with a wood block.
Reinstall the access cover. The thermostat bellows is designed so that a
failure leaves its exhaust damper **open** for maximum cooling airflow.

<a id="p258"></a> **[PDF p.258](../page-images/p0258.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After replacing a damper thermostat, adjust the damper with its exhaust
duct **installed on the engine**. With door fully open, pull the actuating
rod to maximum travel so the thermostat bears against its bracket stop.
The rod threads into the bellows; use its wrench flats when tightening it.

<a id="p259"></a> **[PDF p.259](../page-images/p0259.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-14 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Adjust the swivel to enter the damper door and secure its clip. With the
thermostat at the stop, measure from the flat damper-door edge to the upper
exhaust-duct edge. Set **both doors** to approximately **2 11⁄32 in.**
opening. The base page's upper-shroud removal steps use the older choke
arrangement; use the 1963 supplement's [revised procedure](#revised-engine-sheet-metal)
instead. The base instruction to protect sheet metal from distortion and
use anti-seize compound on fasteners threaded into aluminum remains
applicable.

| Exhaust-damper setting, both sides | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Open-door gap, thermostat at bracket stop | Approximately 2 11⁄32 in. |

## In-vehicle shields and engine mounts carried forward

<a id="p260"></a> **[PDF p.260](../page-images/p0260.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-15 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The upper- and front-shield removal operations on this base page use the
old choke linkage and are replaced by the supplement's 1963 instructions.
The **left side shield** still slides out from under the upper shroud.
Remove left air-cleaner support, loosen upper-shroud-to-head screws, and
remove the screw beneath the left carburetor. Remove side-shield screws at
the lower shroud and duct, oil cooler, and rear-shield seal; disconnect
radio ground and remove the side seal retainer. Slide shield outward.
Refit under the upper shroud, restore oil-cooler, lower-shroud, rear-seal,
radio-ground and air-cleaner attachments, then tighten all screws. Work
around the revised 1963 lower-shroud arrangement described above.

<a id="p261"></a> **[PDF p.261](../page-images/p0261.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-16 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the **right side shield**, remove spare tire, coil/bracket, muffler
support/shield, side seal retainer, and right air-cleaner support. Remove
the beneath-carburetor screw, loosen upper-shroud-to-head fasteners,
disconnect radio ground, remove attachments at lower shroud/duct and rear
shield, then slide the side shield from under the upper shroud. Reverse
the operation and restore the coil and muffler supports. The base page's
one-piece lower-shroud/exhaust-duct procedure is **superseded** by the
separate 1963 shroud and duct procedures.

<a id="p262"></a> **[PDF p.262](../page-images/p0262.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-17 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base **rear left shroud** removal starts by freeing oil-cooler
attachments and seals, then lower-shroud/duct and head/shroud screws.
Refit the shield on crankcase lips, replace both oil-cooler seals, restore
cooler and shroud fasteners, and check for cooler leaks after starting the
engine. The oil-cooler attaching bolt is **8–12 ft. lb.** The **rear right
shroud** requires removal of muffler support/shield, spare tire, coil
bracket, lower shroud/duct, and temperature/oil-pressure sender leads.
Restore leads and all removed parts during installation, using the **1963
oil-pressure switch location** given above.

The front and rear powertrain mounts are not adjustable. Replace broken or
deteriorated mounts before they strain adjacent mounts and driveline parts.

<a id="p263"></a> **[PDF p.263](../page-images/p0263.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-18 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For front-mount replacement, support the powertrain with the factory
jack adapter. Disconnect the clutch cross-shaft ball stud and shift
coupling on a manual-transmission car, or control cable on an automatic
car. Disconnect the parking-brake return spring, remove mount stud nuts,
lower powertrain enough to free the bracket, and remove bracket and
mounts. **Preserve and return the bracket shims to their original sides
and thicknesses.** Fit replacement mounts, bracket, and coupling, raise
powertrain into place, and restore cable/linkage settings and cotter pins.

The rear mount is accessed after removing the engine rear-center shield.
Support the engine, remove the mount's castellated nut and washers, lower
engine enough to unload the mount, then remove its two mounting bolts.
Install the new mount, raise engine into position, install nut and cotter
pin, and restore center shield.

<a id="p264"></a> **[PDF p.264](../page-images/p0264.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-19 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The **oil-filter and generator adapter** is removed after taking off blower
belt and generator, disconnecting battery ground and generator harness, and
disconnecting fuel lines at pump. Remove adapter bolts, including those
around filler tube, and lift adapter with pump, filter and idler as a unit.
Discard its gasket. Inspect rear housing and adapter surfaces, fit a new
gasket and pump push rod/return spring, attach adapter, and tighten its
bolts to **7–13 ft. lb.** Restore generator, fuel lines, and belt and check
for oil leaks after starting.

This base page begins the blower-bearing service. Use its removal and
press method with the **supplement's 4.475 ± .015-in. hub-height dimension**;
the base page's earlier height is superseded.

## Blower, oil pan, cooler, and crankcase vent in the vehicle

<a id="p265"></a> **[PDF p.265](../page-images/p0265.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-20 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

To replace the blower bearing, support the crankcase cover while pressing
the bearing shaft out. Press against the **bearing outer race** when
separating bearing and hub; do not load the inner race. Lubricate the
shaft for pressing, then install the replacement at the **1963 hub-height
dimension above**, not the old dimension in this base page. Fit new cover
gaskets above and below the crankcase vent plate, install cover and blower,
and turn the blower during upper-shroud installation to confirm it clears
the shroud. The blower pulley bolts are **20–25 ft. lb.**

For oil-pan service, drain oil, remove pan bolts, discard gasket, and clean
both rails without gouging them. Hold a new gasket with a little petrolatum,
fit the pan, and tighten its bolts to **40–60 in. lb.** Refill according to
the [1963 lubrication specification](02-lubrication.md). For crankcase
vent-tube service, remove upper shroud and blower pulley, detach its clamp,
withdraw the tube, and replace its cork gasket before refitting.

For oil-cooler removal, undo shroud/cylinder-head screws and long cooler
bolt, lift out the cooler, discard seals, and drain it inverted. Clean its
fins with air and suitable solvent. Fit **new cooler seals**, reinstall
cooler, tighten the long retaining bolt to **8–12 ft. lb.**, restore shroud
fasteners, and check for leaks with the engine running.

## Cylinder head, valve gear, and lifters in the vehicle

<a id="p266"></a> **[PDF p.266](../page-images/p0266.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-21 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For a left-head removal, protect the vehicle, drain engine oil, disconnect
battery and generator ground, and remove air cleaner/support and accelerator
linkage. On a **1963 regular engine**, first disconnect the automatic-choke
control rod that passes through the head. Remove the two-carburetor and
cross-shaft assembly by the supplement's procedure, along with fuel line,
vacuum balance tube, spark-plug wires/plugs, generator brace, side shield,
and oil cooler. Support the powertrain with the specified jack adapter.
Remove side/rear seals, center shield, **separate 1963 lower shroud and
exhaust duct**, exhaust pipe/manifold, and rear-mount nut. Remove valve
cover, rockers/balls/nuts, push rods, studs and guides, and upper push-
rod-tube O-rings. This sequence gives access to the head retaining nuts.

<a id="p267"></a> **[PDF p.267](../page-images/p0267.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-22 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove the lower push-rod-tube O-rings and head nuts/flat washers. Lower
the supported engine just enough to clear the carburetor flange, slide the
head from its crankcase studs, and discard the old head gaskets. For
installation, fit **new head gaskets** and lubricated O-rings, slide the
head over its studs, and fit push-rod drain tubes **before** push-rod
guides. Install each tube's crankcase-end O-ring **after passing the tube
through the head**, avoiding shear at the bore. Refit guides chamfered
side up, rocker studs with anti-seize, head washers/nuts, and tighten in
the factory head-nut sequence. Insert push rods with **blue-band/side
oil-hole end toward the rocker**, seated correctly in lifters. Install
rockers and new valve-cover gasket; set lash by the 1963 procedure.

<a id="p268"></a> **[PDF p.268](../page-images/p0268.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-23 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Restore oil cooler with new seals, side and center shields/seals, exhaust
manifold with new gaskets, revised lower shroud/duct, rear mount, spark
plugs, wiring, two-carburetor/cross-shaft assembly, generator bracket,
vacuum balance tube and air cleaner. Check carburetor synchronization and
leaks. The right-head operation follows the same mechanical sequence, plus
disconnection/reconnection of coil and sending-unit leads. On 1963 cars,
use the **relocated oil-pressure switch** position.

For rocker-arm replacement, remove valve cover, nuts, balls and arms; fit
replacements, adjust lash, and refit the cover. For a valve spring, remove
spark plug and use compressed air to hold valves closed, compress the spring
with factory tool **J-5892-1**, remove locks/cap/spring, and refit the
replacement. Match the **1963 four-bead exhaust-valve locks** to the valve.

For a push rod, remove valve cover and rocker parts for that cylinder,
replace the rod **oil-hole/paint-marked end up toward the rocker**, then
restore lash and cover. For a lifter, remove lower shroud using the 1963
procedure, then rocker gear, rod, guide, and tube. Pull the lifter with a
magnet or grip its snap-ring groove. Clean varnish before forcing out a
stuck lifter. Oil a replacement lifter as the supplement specifies,
restore tube/O-rings and valve gear, set lash, and refit shroud.

## Oil pump, rod bearings, rear seal, and piston rings

<a id="p269"></a> **[PDF p.269](../page-images/p0269.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-24 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The oil-pump cover and gears can be reached by draining oil, supporting
the powertrain, removing rear center shield and revised left lower
shroud/duct, lowering the engine slightly, and removing rear bracket and
skid plate. Remove cover/gasket and inspect the pump by the base overhaul
procedure. Reassemble gears, new gasket and cover; tighten cover bolts to
**60–80 in. lb.** Refit bracket/shields and refill oil. The regulator is
accessed after removing the left lower shroud: remove plug, nylon gasket,
spring and valve, inspect by the base procedure, reinstall, and check
for leaks and proper regulated oil pressure.

For an in-vehicle **connecting-rod bearing**, remove upper shroud by the
1963 procedure, crankcase cover and vent, and position the rod for access.
Remove cap nuts, cap and bearing halves. Fit protective plastic hose over
rod studs to keep them from scoring the crankshaft. The base bearing-
clearance procedure applies to selection and inspection. The **Spyder**
uses heavy-duty bearings specified for its engine; select inserts for
that variant rather than assuming a regular-engine part.

<a id="p270"></a> **[PDF p.270](../page-images/p0270.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-25 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fit selected bearing inserts and cap, and tighten connecting-rod nuts to
**20–26 ft. lb.** Clean crankcase-cover and vent-gasket surfaces without
scraping aluminum; fit new gaskets and cover, then restore the 1963 upper
shroud, carburetors, and linkages. Start and inspect for oil leaks.

The rear-housing crankshaft seal can be replaced in the vehicle. Drain oil,
disconnect battery ground, remove rear seals/shields and rear body grille,
support the engine, and unload/remove rear mount bracket. Lower engine
enough to access crankshaft pulley. Use the factory pulley puller with
its bolts inserted **only to the specified shallow depth** so they do not
damage the rear housing seal; pry the old seal out by its outer edge.

<a id="p271"></a> **[PDF p.271](../page-images/p0271.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-26 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Lubricate the new seal lips as specified on the base page, tap it into
place with a wood drift, draw the pulley on using its retaining bolt,
then back the bolt off before final tightening to **60–80 ft. lb.** Restore
filter, rear mount, shields, front mount and clutch linkage as applicable;
refill and check leaks. Engine-mount fastener torques remain under review
as noted above.

For ring replacement on a cylinder in one bank, drain oil and remove its
head and cylinder. Replace rings with the **oil-control-ring gap at the
engine top** and compression-ring gaps **45° away** from it. Install
compression rings with bevel away from piston top and face markings
toward the top. Oil the rings, compress gently with **J-8356**, put a
**new cylinder-base gasket** on its pilot, slide the cylinder over piston
and rings, and release the compressor without scoring the assembly. For
complete piston/cylinder overhaul, use the base off-vehicle procedure.

## Camshaft-lobe inspection and off-vehicle teardown

<a id="p272"></a> **[PDF p.272](../page-images/p0272.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-27 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For an in-vehicle camshaft-lobe check, drain oil and remove valve covers,
rockers, balls, and nuts. Attach dial-indicator fixture **J-8520** to the
rocker stud **opposite** the lobe being checked. Seat the push rod in its
lifter and fixture socket. Use a remote starter switch to put lifter on
the cam's base circle, zero the indicator, then advance to full lift and
record travel. Continue around until it returns to zero as a check.
Repeat for every lobe. Replace camshaft and lifters if any measured lobe
falls below its **applicable 1963 engine-variant limit**; the base page's
1961 Turbo-Air/Super Turbo-Air comparison table is not treated as a
universal 1963 limit. Refit rocker parts and set lash by the supplement.

For engine-off service, remove the powertrain, separate transmission and
differential by their respective factory procedures, and remove clutch
if equipped. Use the illustrated balanced lifting adapter to support the
offset rear mounting point. The turbocharged Spyder must first have its
variant-specific turbo plumbing and equipment disconnected.

<a id="p273"></a> **[PDF p.273](../page-images/p0273.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-28 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

With engine lifted onto the factory stand, drain oil. Remove air cleaners,
carburetors and linkage using the **1963 automatic-choke arrangement**,
blower belt, fuel pump and rod spring, generator, vacuum tube, front
shield, flywheel or drive plate, distributor, coil, upper/side shrouds,
spark plugs, and engine filter/generator adapter. Preserve identifying
marks and small hardware for reassembly.

<a id="p274"></a> **[PDF p.274](../page-images/p0274.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-29 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Take off blower/pulley, crankcase vent tube, cover/bearing, vent plate,
and both cover gaskets. Remove rear and **separate 1963 lower** shrouds
and exhaust ducts, muffler bracket/shield, and oil pan. Bend back exhaust-
manifold lock tabs, remove clamps and manifolds, and discard manifold
gaskets. Remove front shrouds. If a pressed exhaust sleeve must come out,
the base manual calls for the next oversize replacement sleeve.

<a id="p275"></a> **[PDF p.275](../page-images/p0275.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-30 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Remove rear mounting bracket/skid plate, then valve covers and gear.
**Identify each push rod** for return to its original location; its side
oil hole belongs toward the rocker on assembly. Remove rocker studs and
guides, replace their O-rings, then remove head nuts, lower tube O-rings,
tubes and both heads. Fit temporary cylinder-holding tubes on crankcase
studs before turning the crankshaft so cylinders cannot lift and damage
their seals. Remove lifters with magnet/hook and identify each location.

<a id="p276"></a> **[PDF p.276](../page-images/p0276.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-31 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Mark every cylinder, piston, and rod with its cylinder number before
separation. Remove the holding tubes **one cylinder at a time**, detach
air baffles, and withdraw each cylinder/piston/rod assembly. Push piston
from its cylinder with a wooden handle and remove upper-bore ridge or
deposits without scoring the bore. Pull crankshaft pulley and rear
housing, discard housing gasket, and remove flywheel housing.

<a id="p277"></a> **[PDF p.277](../page-images/p0277.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-32 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Loosen the crankcase through-bolts. Prop the case at a slight angle so
crankshaft and camshaft remain supported when the left half is lifted.
Turn and lift the camshaft without scraping its journals, then lift the
crankshaft straight up to protect the rear bearing. Use the indicated
pullers/press tools for cam, distributor-drive, and crankshaft gears;
do not pry them off. Identify each main-bearing insert by location before
removal.

## Cylinder-head repair and valve assembly

<a id="p278"></a> **[PDF p.278](../page-images/p0278.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-33 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Handle the aluminum head without damaging cooling fins. On a bench,
compress each valve spring with **J-8062**, remove locks, cap, spring,
shim, and valve; keep components identified by position. Clean combustion
chambers/ports with a fine wire brush without damaging the cylinder
sealing face, then clean valve-guide bores with **J-8101**.

<a id="p279"></a> **[PDF p.279](../page-images/p0279.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-34 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Clean valve stems and faces, wash and dry parts, then inspect head,
exhaust sleeves, valves, inserts, and guides for cracks, burning, scuffing,
and wear. Replace a head whose seat insert or guide is beyond repair.
Check stem-to-guide clearance with micrometer/telescoping gauge or dial
indicator. **Use the supplement's 1963 intake and exhaust limits above**;
the older exhaust clearance numbers printed on this base page are
superseded.

<a id="p280"></a> **[PDF p.280](../page-images/p0280.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-35 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For an indicator check, measure stem side movement above the guide with
the valve head slightly off its seat. A new valve tried in a worn guide
helps identify whether valve replacement or guide reaming for an oversize
stem is needed. The supplement adds the later **.020-in. service oversize**
to the base page's older available sizes. Check valve-spring pressure
with the factory spring tester, choosing the **1963 engine variant's
spring specification**; the old regular/high-output table must not be
applied to the Spyder. Clean and inspect the guide before centering any
valve-seat grinder pilot in it.

<a id="p281"></a> **[PDF p.281](../page-images/p0281.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-36 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After machining, seat contact must be concentric and wide enough to carry
heat away from the valve head. Reface a pitted valve only if its edge
will retain the specified thickness; replace a badly worn, warped, or
burned valve instead of leaving a knife-edge face. If a valve guide or
seat insert cannot be restored, replace the head as an assembly.

Do not remove an exhaust-manifold sleeve unless necessary. For removal,
warm the head uniformly and turn the sleeve out with a suitable wrench;
never pry or hammer it from the head. Inspect its bore. Chill the new
oversize press-fit sleeve and warm the head without an open flame, then
start it square and install with a soft tool, with its flat side parallel
to the exhaust push-rod tube opening.

<a id="p282"></a> **[PDF p.282](../page-images/p0282.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-37 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

When assembling a head, orient its vacuum balance-tube fitting for the
correct bank and refit needed carburetor/exhaust studs with anti-seize.
Oil each valve and return it to its original or newly fitted guide. Fit
a hardened spring-seat shim, spring, and cap; compress with J-8062 and
install the locks. For **1963 exhaust valves, use the four-bead locks**.
Check that locks seat in their grooves before releasing the compressor.
The high-output engine has valve-spring dampers; select springs and
installed-height limits by engine variant.

<a id="p283"></a> **[PDF p.283](../page-images/p0283.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-38 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Valve-seat refacing changes the spring's installed height. Measure it
after assembly and use a suitable hardened shim only if needed and within
the applicable 1963 spring-height limit. Fit new head gaskets with a light
petrolatum film at the cylinder counterbore to hold them in place.

For piston/rod disassembly, expand and remove rings with **J-8014**;
support the piston and press the pin out of the rod with the specified
arbor-press adapters. A lightly oiled pin at room temperature should
support its own weight in either piston pin boss. Pistons, pins, and
cylinders are serviced as matched assemblies.

<a id="p284"></a> **[PDF p.284](../page-images/p0284.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-39 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Support each cylinder without bruising its sealing edges, remove the
upper-bore ridge, and measure taper and out-of-round at several bore
depths/directions. The base manual replaces the **matched cylinder and
piston together** when taper or wear exceeds **.005 in.** Square a new
ring in the cylinder with a piston, then measure its end gap; the base
service range is **.010–.020 in.** Clean ring grooves and test each ring
around the full groove to detect binding. Keep the ring-face markings
up and bevel away from the piston top. The Spyder has variant-specific
rings; check its 1963 ring dimensions before selecting replacements.

<a id="p285"></a> **[PDF p.285](../page-images/p0285.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-40 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Check ring side clearance and freedom in the grooves. Install oil-ring
expander with gap aligned to the piston-pin hole and the oil ring with
gap toward engine top. For piston/rod assembly, oil pin bores, align rod
with its matched piston, and press the pin in with the factory support,
pilot, and installer; verify free movement in the piston bores afterward.
The oil pump in the rear housing has distributor-driven gears and a
spring-loaded regulator. Remove its cover/gasket and gears for inspection,
then remove plug, nylon gasket, spring, and regulator valve as needed.

## Oil pump, rear housing, and crankcase inspection

<a id="p286"></a> **[PDF p.286](../page-images/p0286.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-41 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Wash the oil-pump parts and inspect the gears and housing. Install the idler
gear so its shaft sits **.010–.020 in. below** the gasket surface; install
the drive gear and check gear projection **.0025–.0045 in.**, gear-to-housing
clearance **.002–.005 in.**, and backlash **.002–.008 in.** Chevrolet offered
gears .001 in. thicker to restore the end clearance needed for pump priming.

| Oil-pump fit, carried procedure | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Idler shaft below gasket surface | .010–.020 in. |
| Gear projection above gasket surface | .0025–.0045 in. |
| Gear-to-housing clearance | .002–.005 in. |
| Gear backlash | .002–.008 in. |

Oil the gears, install cover and gasket, then confirm the pump turns freely
through the distributor opening before fitting the regulator valve, spring,
nylon gasket, and plug. For a rear-housing seal, remove it with a wood or
fiber drift, inspect the bore, lubricate the seal's outside bead, and install
it squarely; retain the specified high-melting grease between its lips.

<a id="p287"></a> **[PDF p.287](../page-images/p0287.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-42 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Fit the oil-cooler bypass valve with its **spring down** and the oil-filter
bypass valve with its **spring up**; discard each removed valve. To clean the
crankcase, remove both gallery plugs at the flywheel end and flush the
passages. Inspect cylinder pilot bores, bearing seats, and gasket surfaces
for damage. Dissolve old gasket material with solvent instead of scraping
the aluminum. Use the factory stud-length drawing when replacing studs and
anti-seize compound on threads entering aluminum.

Cylinder studs adjacent to main-bearing webs enter blind holes; the others
do not. Replace a damaged oil-level-gauge tube by withdrawing it with pliers,
applying anti-seize to the new tube's leading surface, and driving it with a
wood block until its rolled ring seats. Whenever the case is split, inspect
both halves of each main bearing and its crankshaft journal. Replace bearing

## Bearings, hydraulic lifters, and crankcase assembly

<a id="p289"></a> **[PDF p.289](../page-images/p0289.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-44 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

To check main-bearing clearance with Plastigage, clean and dry the journal
and insert, lay a strip along the journal for its full width, assemble the
other case half, and torque the case fasteners in the printed sequence.
**Do not rotate the crankshaft** while the strip is compressed. Separate the
halves and compare its widest flattened part with the package scale. A
change of more than .001 in. across the strip calls for micrometer checks
of the journal and bearing. Keep the distinct No. 4 bearing in its assigned
location; its shell geometry differs from Nos. 2 and 3.

<a id="p290"></a> **[PDF p.290](../page-images/p0290.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-45 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After each main-bearing fit, turn the crankshaft to feel for drag and check
end play with a dial indicator. For rod-bearing clearance, protect the rod
bolts with hose, clean the journal and insert, lay Plastigage lengthwise,
fit and torque the cap, then remove it without turning the crankshaft.
Compare the compressed strip to the scale and check taper with a micrometer
when width varies more than .001 in. Do not file a rod or cap to adjust an
insert bearing.

<a id="p291"></a> **[PDF p.291](../page-images/p0291.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-46 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After rod-bearing work, rotate the crankshaft and check rod side clearance
at each crankpin. The factory hydraulic-lifter drawing shows the body,
plunger/spring, ball/check spring, retainer, and push-rod seat. Disassemble
with the plunger depressed; clean and inspect every part, replacing a
damaged lifter as an assembly. Refit the check ball and spring beneath the
retainer, plunger spring and plunger, then push-rod seat and top retainer.
Prime the assembled lifter with oil as the factory directs. Replace the
flywheel-housing seal using a wood or fiber drift and square installation;
retain the specified high-melting grease between sealing lips.

distributor drive gear, and oil slinger in the order shown in Figure 6A-90/91.
Clean both case bearing bores and inserts; fit the **1962–63 left flanged
and right unflanged rear main bearing halves** as specified on supplement
page 6A-8. Oil the inserts and lower the crankshaft into the supported left
case half without striking the bearing edges. Inspect camshaft journals for

See the [camshaft end-play and gear-backlash illustrations](../diagrams/p0294-camshaft-timing.png).

<a id="p303"></a> **[PDF p.303](../page-images/p0303.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-58 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

## 1963 engine changes and carburetor access

<a id="p659"></a> **[PDF p.659](../page-images/p0659.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The automatic choke controls fuel mixture during warmup; its detailed
service appears in the factory Fuel and Exhaust section. Engine
shrouding provides access to the choke thermostat at the lower side of the
head. The Monza **9:1 compression** engine uses the revised combustion
chamber introduced during 1961, with filled-in roof surfaces near valves
and spark-plug holes. The 1963 distributor service changes belong to the
electrical chapter.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Monza 9:1 engine | 9:1 |

When removing a carburetor for access to another part on the regular
two-carburetor engine, remove **both carburetors and the cross-shaft as an
assembly** to preserve synchronization. Remove air cleaners and cross-tube;
disconnect accelerator rod and right-carburetor distributor spark hose;
disconnect choke rods and fuel lines; remove two hold-down nuts per
carburetor; and lift off the assembly. Reverse the operation and **check
carburetor synchronization** after installation. The Spyder's single
side-draft carburetor has different service arrangements.

## Revised engine sheet metal

<a id="p660"></a> **[PDF p.660](../page-images/p0660.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the upper shroud, remove air-cleaner assemblies/supports, fuel lines,
spark-advance hose, accelerator and choke rods, and both carburetors with
cross-shaft. Remove blower belt, spark-plug wires, and vacuum balance tube.
Detach generator outboard bracket from the shroud and its front pulley-end
bolt; swing generator rearward. Disconnect heater hose at the compartment
wall **if equipped**. Remove remaining shroud screws; lift the front edge
first and rotate clockwise to clear oil filter and generator adapter.
During installation turn the blower as screws are tightened to verify
blower-to-shroud clearance. Tighten upper-shroud screws to **30–40 in. lb.**,
restore removed parts, and check carburetor synchronization.

<a id="p661"></a> **[PDF p.661](../page-images/p0661.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-3 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The exploded view identifies separate upper, front, lower, rear, and side
shrouds; cylinder air baffles; exhaust ducts and thermostatic dampers;
oil cooler; seals; and direct-air-heater connections where equipped.
See the [engine shrouding exploded view](../diagrams/p0661-engine-shrouding.png).
For front-shield removal, disconnect battery ground, air-cleaner supports,
vacuum tube and heater hose (if fitted), front seal retainer, and accelerator
rod. Move the front shield rearward to reach starter-solenoid and fuel-line
connections. Disconnect tank fuel line at pump, rear hose connection, battery
cable at solenoid and quick-disconnect wires. Pass lines and wires through
their grommet openings and remove the shield over accelerator rod and boot.
Reverse the procedure to install, restoring grommets and shields.

<a id="p662"></a> **[PDF p.662](../page-images/p0662.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-4 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The lower shroud separates from exhaust duct/damper, permitting access to
oil-pressure and temperature switches and the 1963 choke-stove unit without
removing the duct. On either side, loosen thermostat screws and remove
sheet-metal screws, then eight shroud-to-head/crankcase-cap screws. Lower
and shift duct rearward enough to release thermostat tension and disconnect
its rod from the damper door. Reverse removal, guiding the road-draft tube
through the right-side opening and checking damper adjustment by the base
procedure.

<a id="p663"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1963 lower shroud has thermostat bracket and heat shield **riveted**
to the shroud. To replace either, remove the lower-shroud assembly and
thermostat, drill out the rivets, then attach the replacement part with
specified **¼-20 bolt and self-locking nut**. For thermostat replacement,
remove lower shroud and rod, remove stop bolt, lift thermostat out, then
reverse installation.

For exhaust-duct service, remove lower shroud and rear seal/retainer.
On the left remove oil-cooler and rear-center-shield screws; on the right,
coil and muffler bracket. Remove remaining duct screws and duct, reverse
to install, and check damper setting. The page's Greenbrier, Lakewood
station-wagon, and van removal note does **not** describe the 1963 passenger
sedan/coupe reference.

To remove the front shroud, first remove lower shroud and exhaust manifold,
then its front-shield screws. On the right remove muffler-shield bolts;
detach heater hoses if direct-air heat is fitted. Remove remaining bolts,
including the left bolt inside heater-duct opening, and take out the
shroud. Reverse to install and check the exhaust-duct damper.

## Exhaust valves, guides, push rods, and hydraulic lifters

<a id="p663-valve"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

All 1963 Corvair engines use exhaust-valve locks with **four-bead grooves**.
This includes the turbocharged Spyder; the supplement says that engine
received the design in 1962. Install the new locks by the same method as
the earlier single-groove type, but match locks to their valve stems.

See the [valve-lock comparison](../diagrams/p0663-exhaust-valve-locks.png).

<a id="p664"></a> **[PDF p.664](../page-images/p0664.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Straight-stem exhaust valves introduced during 1961 carry into 1963.
Service oversizes are **.003, .010, and .020 in.**; Chevrolet identifies
**J-5830-5** as the .020-in. reamer. Measure stem diameter one inch from
tip and guide bore at the port end; subtract stem diameter from guide
diameter. Alternatively, measure side play with an indicator at the valve
stem just above the guide and valve head approximately 1⁄16 in. off its seat.
If clearance exceeds the worn limit, select the next oversize valve and
ream the guide to correct new clearance.

| Valve stem-to-guide clearance | New | Worn limit |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | .001–.0027 in. | .004 in. |
| Exhaust | .0014–.0029 in. | .006 in. |

Install each push rod with the **side oil hole and paint-marked end toward
the rocker arm**. For a new or rebuilt hydraulic lifter, submerge it in
engine oil and work its plunger several times before installation. The
separate lower shroud permits lifter access; use the base lifter-removal
method after removing that shroud.

### Running valve-lash adjustment

With the engine at normal operating temperature, replace each valve cover
temporarily with a cut-out cover and gasket to catch oil. At idle, back
off one rocker nut until it clatters, then tighten slowly until clatter
just stops: **zero lash**. Tighten **¼ turn**, pause **10 seconds** for the
lifter to settle, and repeat in quarter turns with pauses until the nut
has moved **one full turn from zero lash**. Perform this slowly: the source
warns that faster preload can bring an intake valve into contact with a
piston or bend a push rod. Replace noisy lifters, adjust remaining valves,
and restore the valve covers with new gaskets.

### Blower-bearing hub height

The base replacement procedure carries forward, but the **1963 location
dimension** from hub to lower edge of crankcase cover is **4.475 ± .015
in.** The base dimension is superseded.

| 1963 blower bearing installation dimension | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Hub to lower edge of crankcase cover | 4.475 ± .015 in. |

## Turbocharged Monza Spyder variant

<a id="p667"></a> **[PDF p.667](../page-images/p0667.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-9 years=1963 scope=passenger variant=spyder -->

The optional Spyder engine is a matched turbocharged assembly. Chevrolet
explicitly warns **not to move its turbocharger onto another Corvair
engine**. The Spyder uses heavy-duty main and rod bearings, stronger
connecting rods, variant-specific rings, crankshaft and cylinder heads,
and **8:1 compression**. Its left head carries the temperature-gauge
thermistor; its right head has a turbo oil drain. External differences
include a single side-draft Carter YH carburetor, variant fuel lines,
distributor and timing tab, exhaust plumbing, heat shields, wiring for
temperature warning, and turbo oil-feed components.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Turbocharged Monza Spyder | 8:1 |

See the [factory Spyder engine illustration](../diagrams/p0667-monza-spyder-engine.png).

For Spyder upper-shroud removal, first remove turbo assembly with its
carburetor/air cleaner, inlet and exhaust piping, oil lines, and diffuser,
then use the regular-engine shroud procedure. The lower shrouds use the
regular procedure. The left side shield requires fuel-filter removal and
manifold-pressure-line disconnection; the right needs exhaust-insulator
clearance. For front shield/seal retainer removal, remove turbo and fuel
filter, disconnect accelerator linkage and tank line, then use the base
procedure. Its right seal is heat-resistant insulation: seat it along its
full length when reinstalling. Spyder air-recirculation plates are removed
in winter and installed in summer. Turbocharger and fuel/air service
continues in Section 9 of the factory supplement.
