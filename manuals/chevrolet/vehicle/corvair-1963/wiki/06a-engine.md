# Engine Mechanical — 1963 Passenger Cars

<!-- INCOMPLETE: The 1962–63 supplement's engine changes are being integrated here. Base 1961 Section 6A procedures across 61 physical pages remain to be reconstructed and filtered for 1963 passenger variants. Do not treat this chapter as complete. -->

The supplement says the 1963 Corvair engine keeps the 1961 basic design.
Its 1963 changes include automatic choke controls, revised shrouding,
late-style Monza combustion chambers where equipped, and an updated
distributor. Base mechanical service continues where these changes do not
supersede it. The turbocharged Monza Spyder is a **1963 passenger-car
variant** and is included with explicit applicability. Commercial-only
Corvair 95 operations are excluded.

The original source filenames and hashes are in the
[source inventory](../data/source-inventory.json). The base engine section
occupies combined PDF pp. 246–306; the supplement occupies pp. 659–667.

## Engine design and service orientation carried forward

<a id="p247"></a> **[PDF p.247](../page-images/p0247.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-2 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The Turbo-Air is an air-cooled, horizontally opposed **six-cylinder** engine
with a vertically split aluminum crankcase and individual cast-iron
cylinders. Long studs pass through each cylinder and head into the
crankcase. Opposed aluminum heads carry integral intake passages. The
camshaft sits below the crankshaft and operates the valves through hydraulic
lifters, push rods, and rocker arms. For 1963, use the supplement and
year-specific specification table for compression ratio and internal
dimensions of each engine variant; the base introduction predates the
1963 engine lineup.

<a id="p248"></a> **[PDF p.248](../page-images/p0248.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-3 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Steel push-rod tubes protect the rods between crankcase and heads and drain
oil from the heads back to the sump. Hydraulic lifters follow cam lobes
within the crankcase; the base cross-section shows the opposed-cylinder,
head, and shroud layout.

<a id="p249"></a> **[PDF p.249](../page-images/p0249.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-4 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The camshaft journals run in machined crankcase bores and its thrust is
controlled at the gear end. The forged crankshaft has paired throws and
four main bearings supported by the crankcase halves. Timing gears connect
the crankshaft and camshaft. Each aluminum piston has two compression rings
and an oil-control assembly. Refer to the original cross-sections for
component orientation before teardown.

<a id="p250"></a> **[PDF p.250](../page-images/p0250.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-5 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The powertrain uses two front transmission mounts at the rear crossmember
and one centered engine rear mount. In installed orientation, the flywheel
is at the **front** of the engine and cylinders are numbered from the rear:
**right bank 1-3-5, left bank 2-4-6**. Firing order is **1-4-5-2-3-6**.

The rear engine housing contains the gear-driven oil pump, regulator,
crankshaft seal, and main oil passages. Oil flows from sump pickup through
pump, full-flow filter, air-cooled cooler, crankcase galleries, bearings,
and hydraulic lifters. The 1963 oil-pressure switch is at the location
given in [the supplement](#oil-filter-cylinder-heads-and-senders).

<a id="p251"></a> **[PDF p.251](../page-images/p0251.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-6 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory lubrication diagram traces the distinct passages through the
rear housing, filter, cooler, and crankcase. Its filler location marked
for the coupe and sedan is the passenger-car arrangement in this manual.
The station-wagon and Corvair 95 filler routing in the diagram does not
apply to this reference.

<a id="p252"></a> **[PDF p.252](../page-images/p0252.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-7 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Right and left main galleries feed crankshaft and camshaft bearings and
hydraulic lifters. Oil passes through the crankshaft to connecting-rod
bearings and through hollow push rods to rocker arms, valve springs, and
stems. Keep the push-rod **side oil hole toward the rocker arm**, as the
supplement emphasizes. A louvered crankcase-vent plate and separation
chamber fit between gaskets beneath the crankcase cover. Cooling air from
the centrifugal blower moves through the engine shrouding over cylinder
and head fins.

<a id="p253"></a> **[PDF p.253](../page-images/p0253.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-8 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Keep engine oil between dipstick **ADD** and **FULL** marks; do not overfill.
The base engine-oil, belt, ignition-timing, compression-check, and tune-up
instructions refer to their other factory sections. For running valve lash,
the supplement's [one-turn-from-zero procedure](#running-valve-lash-adjustment)
supersedes the different base preload turns. The base diagnostic method
for locating a noisy hydraulic lifter uses a listening tube or a finger
on the valve-spring retainer to distinguish its shock from other noises.

<a id="p254"></a> **[PDF p.254](../page-images/p0254.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-9 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Hard lifter rapping can indicate a varnished or galled plunger; moderate
rapping can indicate excessive leakdown, check-valve leakage, or wrong lash.
General valve-train noise can indicate low oil supply or lash error.
Intermittent clicking may come from check-ball contamination. Keep lifter
plungers with their matched bodies; replace a damaged pair as a complete
unit. Check that each clean plunger falls freely by its own weight and that
the check valve seals.

Keep oil off engine shrouding so fumes are not carried into the passenger
compartment. Investigate push-rod-tube, oil-cooler, filter-adapter, valve-
cover, crankcase-cover, distributor, housing, and exhaust-sleeve seals for
leaks. Clean cylinder/head fins and clear the oil-cooler air passages of
leaves, chaff, and other debris. Use the 1963 lubrication schedule for
maintenance intervals.

## Oil-cooler cleaning and exhaust-damper setting

<a id="p255"></a> **[PDF p.255](../page-images/p0255.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-10 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The factory service-reference matrix identifies parts to remove before
servicing a selected engine component. Its **1961 sheet-metal access paths
must be read with the supplement's revised 1963 shrouds**; the original
[matrix page](../page-images/p0255.png) is preserved for that purpose.

<a id="p256"></a> **[PDF p.256](../page-images/p0256.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-11 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The service-reference matrix continues across this landscape page. It
distinguishes parts removed for easy access from the component actually
being serviced. The 1963 lower-shroud and choke changes override its older
shroud-removal cells.

<a id="p257"></a> **[PDF p.257](../page-images/p0257.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-12 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

To clear debris from the oil cooler, remove its upper-shroud access cover,
brush off loose material, and blow air **upward through the cooler fins**
from below while the exhaust damper door is held open with a wood block.
Reinstall the access cover. The thermostat bellows is designed so that a
failure leaves its exhaust damper **open** for maximum cooling airflow.

<a id="p258"></a> **[PDF p.258](../page-images/p0258.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-13 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

After replacing a damper thermostat, adjust the damper with its exhaust
duct **installed on the engine**. With door fully open, pull the actuating
rod to maximum travel so the thermostat bears against its bracket stop.
The rod threads into the bellows; use its wrench flats when tightening it.

<a id="p259"></a> **[PDF p.259](../page-images/p0259.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-14 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

Adjust the swivel to enter the damper door and secure its clip. With the
thermostat at the stop, measure from the flat damper-door edge to the upper
exhaust-duct edge. Set **both doors** to approximately **2 11⁄32 in.**
opening. The base page's upper-shroud removal steps use the older choke
arrangement; use the 1963 supplement's [revised procedure](#revised-engine-sheet-metal)
instead. The base instruction to protect sheet metal from distortion and
use anti-seize compound on fasteners threaded into aluminum remains
applicable.

| Exhaust-damper setting, both sides | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Open-door gap, thermostat at bracket stop | Approximately 2 11⁄32 in. |

## In-vehicle shields and engine mounts carried forward

<a id="p260"></a> **[PDF p.260](../page-images/p0260.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-15 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The upper- and front-shield removal operations on this base page use the
old choke linkage and are replaced by the supplement's 1963 instructions.
The **left side shield** still slides out from under the upper shroud.
Remove left air-cleaner support, loosen upper-shroud-to-head screws, and
remove the screw beneath the left carburetor. Remove side-shield screws at
the lower shroud and duct, oil cooler, and rear-shield seal; disconnect
radio ground and remove the side seal retainer. Slide shield outward.
Refit under the upper shroud, restore oil-cooler, lower-shroud, rear-seal,
radio-ground and air-cleaner attachments, then tighten all screws. Work
around the revised 1963 lower-shroud arrangement described above.

<a id="p261"></a> **[PDF p.261](../page-images/p0261.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-16 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the **right side shield**, remove spare tire, coil/bracket, muffler
support/shield, side seal retainer, and right air-cleaner support. Remove
the beneath-carburetor screw, loosen upper-shroud-to-head fasteners,
disconnect radio ground, remove attachments at lower shroud/duct and rear
shield, then slide the side shield from under the upper shroud. Reverse
the operation and restore the coil and muffler supports. The base page's
one-piece lower-shroud/exhaust-duct procedure is **superseded** by the
separate 1963 shroud and duct procedures.

<a id="p262"></a> **[PDF p.262](../page-images/p0262.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-17 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The base **rear left shroud** removal starts by freeing oil-cooler
attachments and seals, then lower-shroud/duct and head/shroud screws.
Refit the shield on crankcase lips, replace both oil-cooler seals, restore
cooler and shroud fasteners, and check for cooler leaks after starting the
engine. The oil-cooler attaching bolt is **8–12 ft. lb.** The **rear right
shroud** requires removal of muffler support/shield, spare tire, coil
bracket, lower shroud/duct, and temperature/oil-pressure sender leads.
Restore leads and all removed parts during installation, using the **1963
oil-pressure switch location** given above.

The front and rear powertrain mounts are not adjustable. Replace broken or
deteriorated mounts before they strain adjacent mounts and driveline parts.

<a id="p263"></a> **[PDF p.263](../page-images/p0263.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-18 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For front-mount replacement, support the powertrain with the factory
jack adapter. Disconnect the clutch cross-shaft ball stud and shift
coupling on a manual-transmission car, or control cable on an automatic
car. Disconnect the parking-brake return spring, remove mount stud nuts,
lower powertrain enough to free the bracket, and remove bracket and
mounts. **Preserve and return the bracket shims to their original sides
and thicknesses.** Fit replacement mounts, bracket, and coupling, raise
powertrain into place, and restore cable/linkage settings and cotter pins.

The rear mount is accessed after removing the engine rear-center shield.
Support the engine, remove the mount's castellated nut and washers, lower
engine enough to unload the mount, then remove its two mounting bolts.
Install the new mount, raise engine into position, install nut and cotter
pin, and restore center shield.

<a id="p264"></a> **[PDF p.264](../page-images/p0264.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=base-1961 page=6A-19 years=1961,1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The **oil-filter and generator adapter** is removed after taking off blower
belt and generator, disconnecting battery ground and generator harness, and
disconnecting fuel lines at pump. Remove adapter bolts, including those
around filler tube, and lift adapter with pump, filter and idler as a unit.
Discard its gasket. Inspect rear housing and adapter surfaces, fit a new
gasket and pump push rod/return spring, attach adapter, and tighten its
bolts to **7–13 ft. lb.** Restore generator, fuel lines, and belt and check
for oil leaks after starting.

This base page begins the blower-bearing service. Use its removal and
press method with the **supplement's 4.475 ± .015-in. hub-height dimension**;
the base page's earlier height is superseded.

## 1963 engine changes and carburetor access

<a id="p659"></a> **[PDF p.659](../page-images/p0659.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-1 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The automatic choke controls fuel mixture during warmup; its detailed
service appears in the factory Fuel and Exhaust section. Engine
shrouding provides access to the choke thermostat at the lower side of the
head. The Monza **9:1 compression** engine uses the revised combustion
chamber introduced during 1961, with filled-in roof surfaces near valves
and spark-plug holes. The 1963 distributor service changes belong to the
electrical chapter.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Monza 9:1 engine | 9:1 |

When removing a carburetor for access to another part on the regular
two-carburetor engine, remove **both carburetors and the cross-shaft as an
assembly** to preserve synchronization. Remove air cleaners and cross-tube;
disconnect accelerator rod and right-carburetor distributor spark hose;
disconnect choke rods and fuel lines; remove two hold-down nuts per
carburetor; and lift off the assembly. Reverse the operation and **check
carburetor synchronization** after installation. The Spyder's single
side-draft carburetor has different service arrangements.

## Revised engine sheet metal

<a id="p660"></a> **[PDF p.660](../page-images/p0660.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-2 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

For the upper shroud, remove air-cleaner assemblies/supports, fuel lines,
spark-advance hose, accelerator and choke rods, and both carburetors with
cross-shaft. Remove blower belt, spark-plug wires, and vacuum balance tube.
Detach generator outboard bracket from the shroud and its front pulley-end
bolt; swing generator rearward. Disconnect heater hose at the compartment
wall **if equipped**. Remove remaining shroud screws; lift the front edge
first and rotate clockwise to clear oil filter and generator adapter.
During installation turn the blower as screws are tightened to verify
blower-to-shroud clearance. Tighten upper-shroud screws to **30–40 in. lb.**,
restore removed parts, and check carburetor synchronization.

<a id="p661"></a> **[PDF p.661](../page-images/p0661.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-3 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The exploded view identifies separate upper, front, lower, rear, and side
shrouds; cylinder air baffles; exhaust ducts and thermostatic dampers;
oil cooler; seals; and direct-air-heater connections where equipped.
See the [engine shrouding exploded view](../diagrams/p0661-engine-shrouding.png).
For front-shield removal, disconnect battery ground, air-cleaner supports,
vacuum tube and heater hose (if fitted), front seal retainer, and accelerator
rod. Move the front shield rearward to reach starter-solenoid and fuel-line
connections. Disconnect tank fuel line at pump, rear hose connection, battery
cable at solenoid and quick-disconnect wires. Pass lines and wires through
their grommet openings and remove the shield over accelerator rod and boot.
Reverse the procedure to install, restoring grommets and shields.

<a id="p662"></a> **[PDF p.662](../page-images/p0662.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-4 years=1962,1963 scope=passenger -->

The lower shroud separates from exhaust duct/damper, permitting access to
oil-pressure and temperature switches and the 1963 choke-stove unit without
removing the duct. On either side, loosen thermostat screws and remove
sheet-metal screws, then eight shroud-to-head/crankcase-cap screws. Lower
and shift duct rearward enough to release thermostat tension and disconnect
its rod from the damper door. Reverse removal, guiding the road-draft tube
through the right-side opening and checking damper adjustment by the base
procedure.

<a id="p663"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1963 lower shroud has thermostat bracket and heat shield **riveted**
to the shroud. To replace either, remove the lower-shroud assembly and
thermostat, drill out the rivets, then attach the replacement part with
specified **¼-20 bolt and self-locking nut**. For thermostat replacement,
remove lower shroud and rod, remove stop bolt, lift thermostat out, then
reverse installation.

For exhaust-duct service, remove lower shroud and rear seal/retainer.
On the left remove oil-cooler and rear-center-shield screws; on the right,
coil and muffler bracket. Remove remaining duct screws and duct, reverse
to install, and check damper setting. The page's Greenbrier, Lakewood
station-wagon, and van removal note does **not** describe the 1963 passenger
sedan/coupe reference.

To remove the front shroud, first remove lower shroud and exhaust manifold,
then its front-shield screws. On the right remove muffler-shield bolts;
detach heater hoses if direct-air heat is fitted. Remove remaining bolts,
including the left bolt inside heater-duct opening, and take out the
shroud. Reverse to install and check the exhaust-duct damper.

## Exhaust valves, guides, push rods, and hydraulic lifters

<a id="p663-valve"></a> **[PDF p.663](../page-images/p0663.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-5 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

All 1963 Corvair engines use exhaust-valve locks with **four-bead grooves**.
This includes the turbocharged Spyder; the supplement says that engine
received the design in 1962. Install the new locks by the same method as
the earlier single-groove type, but match locks to their valve stems.

See the [valve-lock comparison](../diagrams/p0663-exhaust-valve-locks.png).

<a id="p664"></a> **[PDF p.664](../page-images/p0664.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-6 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

Straight-stem exhaust valves introduced during 1961 carry into 1963.
Service oversizes are **.003, .010, and .020 in.**; Chevrolet identifies
**J-5830-5** as the .020-in. reamer. Measure stem diameter one inch from
tip and guide bore at the port end; subtract stem diameter from guide
diameter. Alternatively, measure side play with an indicator at the valve
stem just above the guide and valve head approximately 1⁄16 in. off its seat.
If clearance exceeds the worn limit, select the next oversize valve and
ream the guide to correct new clearance.

| Valve stem-to-guide clearance | New | Worn limit |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | .001–.0027 in. | .004 in. |
| Exhaust | .0014–.0029 in. | .006 in. |

Install each push rod with the **side oil hole and paint-marked end toward
the rocker arm**. For a new or rebuilt hydraulic lifter, submerge it in
engine oil and work its plunger several times before installation. The
separate lower shroud permits lifter access; use the base lifter-removal
method after removing that shroud.

### Running valve-lash adjustment

With the engine at normal operating temperature, replace each valve cover
temporarily with a cut-out cover and gasket to catch oil. At idle, back
off one rocker nut until it clatters, then tighten slowly until clatter
just stops: **zero lash**. Tighten **¼ turn**, pause **10 seconds** for the
lifter to settle, and repeat in quarter turns with pauses until the nut
has moved **one full turn from zero lash**. Perform this slowly: the source
warns that faster preload can bring an intake valve into contact with a
piston or bend a push rod. Replace noisy lifters, adjust remaining valves,
and restore the valve covers with new gaskets.

### Blower-bearing hub height

The base replacement procedure carries forward, but the **1963 location
dimension** from hub to lower edge of crankcase cover is **4.475 ± .015
in.** The base dimension is superseded.

| 1963 blower bearing installation dimension | Factory value |
|---|---|
| Hub to lower edge of crankcase cover | 4.475 ± .015 in. |

## Oil filter, cylinder heads, and senders

<a id="p665"></a> **[PDF p.665](../page-images/p0665.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-7 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

On an **air-conditioned** car, support the filter and adapter elbow while
removing the bolt through generator/filter adapter plate. Lift the filter
and elbow together to limit oil spillage, then remove the filter mounting
bolt and separate them. Discard the filter, clean the elbow, replace all
gaskets and fit a new filter; reinstall in reverse order.

The automatic-choke control rod passes through the 1963 cylinder head and
must be removed before head removal. Other head service uses the applicable
base procedure with year-specific dimensions from Section 12.

The **late-1962 and 1963 oil-pressure switch** sits at the **top of the
oil-filter adapter, left of the fuel pump**. Disconnect its lead, replace
the switch with a six-point deep socket, and reconnect the lead. The
separate retrofit wiring/plug instructions on the following page concern
**1961 and early-1962 cars only** and are excluded from 1963 service.

<a id="p666"></a> **[PDF p.666](../page-images/p0666.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-8 years=1963 scope=passenger -->

The 1962–63 rear main bearing uses a thrust-flanged **left bearing half**
and **unflanged right half** in the crankcase to prevent mismatched thrust
flanges. Match the halves to their sides as shown in the source's
Figure 6A-11.

See the [rear-main-bearing illustration](../diagrams/p0666-rear-main-bearing.png).

## Turbocharged Monza Spyder variant

<a id="p667"></a> **[PDF p.667](../page-images/p0667.png)** <!-- SOURCE id=supplement-1962-63 page=6A-9 years=1963 scope=passenger variant=spyder -->

The optional Spyder engine is a matched turbocharged assembly. Chevrolet
explicitly warns **not to move its turbocharger onto another Corvair
engine**. The Spyder uses heavy-duty main and rod bearings, stronger
connecting rods, variant-specific rings, crankshaft and cylinder heads,
and **8:1 compression**. Its left head carries the temperature-gauge
thermistor; its right head has a turbo oil drain. External differences
include a single side-draft Carter YH carburetor, variant fuel lines,
distributor and timing tab, exhaust plumbing, heat shields, wiring for
temperature warning, and turbo oil-feed components.

| Applicable 1963 passenger engine | Compression ratio |
|---|---|
| Turbocharged Monza Spyder | 8:1 |

See the [factory Spyder engine illustration](../diagrams/p0667-monza-spyder-engine.png).

For Spyder upper-shroud removal, first remove turbo assembly with its
carburetor/air cleaner, inlet and exhaust piping, oil lines, and diffuser,
then use the regular-engine shroud procedure. The lower shrouds use the
regular procedure. The left side shield requires fuel-filter removal and
manifold-pressure-line disconnection; the right needs exhaust-insulator
clearance. For front shield/seal retainer removal, remove turbo and fuel
filter, disconnect accelerator linkage and tank line, then use the base
procedure. Its right seal is heat-resistant insulation: seat it along its
full length when reinstalling. Spyder air-recirculation plates are removed
in winter and installed in summer. Turbocharger and fuel/air service
continues in Section 9 of the factory supplement.
