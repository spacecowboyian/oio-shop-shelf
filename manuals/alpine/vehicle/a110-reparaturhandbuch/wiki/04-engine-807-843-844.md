## Dismantling the cylinder head

1. Remove the engine.
2. Remove the valve cover.
3. Remove the rubber and dished washers from the spark plug tubes.
4. Slacken the valve adjusting screws and remove the pushrods (lay them out in order).
5. Remove the cylinder head bolts and the rocker shafts.
6. Separate the cylinder head from the cylinder block.

> **CAUTION:** if the cylinder head gasket is stuck to the block and the liners, the head must
> **not** be lifted straight off — the liners can come away from their seats, letting foreign
> matter in and damaging the seat seals (leakage). First turn the head about the dowel sleeve
> on the distributor side, or about a cylinder head bolt, to free it.

7. Lift off the cylinder head.
8. Remove the tappet chamber gasket.
9. Fit the liner retainer **Mot. 521**.

<a id="p28"></a>
**[PDF p.28]**

## Cleaning the cylinder block

> **CAUTION:** deposits on the joint face of the cylinder block must **never** be removed with
> a scraper.

Dissolve gasket residue exclusively with the cleaning product **Magnus Magstrib**. Apply the
product to the surface to be cleaned, leave it to act for about **10 min**, then remove the
gasket residue with a wooden spatula.

<a id="p29"></a>
**[PDF p.29]**

## Checking the joint face

Check the joint face for distortion with a straight edge and a set of feeler gauges.

**Maximum distortion (X) = 0.05 mm.** Reface if necessary.

| Cylinder head height | Engines 807-843-844 | Engine 810 |
|---|---|---|
| Normal (mm) | 93.50 | 72 (71.80) |
| Minimum height (mm) | 93 | 71.50 |

Below this dimension the cylinder head must be replaced.

**Maximum skim: 0.5 mm (0.3 mm).**

## Removing, refitting and grinding in a valve

### Removal

1. Compress the valve spring with the compressor **MOT.382**, or **FACOM U43 L** fitted with
   the adaptor **U43 P**.
2. Remove the collets, the upper spring caps, the springs and the seating washers.
3. Lay the valves out in order, or number them.

> **NOTE:** the valve collets are of different shapes for the intake and the exhaust valve.

### Grinding in a valve

Grind the valve in with grinding paste until the seat shows a matt surface all the way round.
Then clean it, apply marking paste, insert the valve and move it back and forth a few times.
The imprint of the valve on its seat shows the quality of the seal and the valve seat width.

> **Clean the entire cylinder head very carefully.**

### Refitting

Carry out the operations in reverse order. Adjust the valves — spanner **Mot. …**

## Replacing a valve guide

This work is carried out with the cylinder head removed, using the holder **Mot.355** with base
**Mot.355-01** and the tool **Mot.356 (1)** with guide bush **Mot.356-02 (2)**.

The base Mot.355-01 has three adjusting screws, by means of which the difference in inclination
between the intake and exhaust valve guides is compensated.

**Inclination of the valve guides:**

| Valve | Inclination |
|---|---|
| Intake | 23° |
| Exhaust | 26° |

**Alignment of the valve guide with the seat:**

| | 807-10 / 807-11 | 807-12 / 843 / 844 |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | C = 32 mm | 40 mm |
| Exhaust | B = 31 mm | 31 mm |

1. Press the valve guide out with the mandrel **(1)** of tool Mot.356.
2. Establish whether it is still the original guide or one that has already been replaced.

<a id="p34"></a>
**[PDF p.34]**

3. Replace the valve guide with one of the next size up.
4. Turn the cylinder head over on the holding plate and secure it again.
5. Ream the valve guide seat with the reamer **Mot.357** corresponding to the diameter of the
   new guide.
6. Fit the bush **(2)** of Mot.356-02 onto the end of the mandrel **(1)**.

**Use of the guide bush:**

- dimension **(A)** for the exhaust valve guide (**31 mm**)
- dimension **(B)** for the intake valve guide (**40 mm**)
- dimension **(C)** for the intake valve guide (**32 mm**)

7. Slide the valve guide onto the end of the mandrel, with the chamfer of the guide facing away
   from the mandrel.
8. Oil the valve guide and press it in until it is correctly seated.

## Recutting a valve seat

1. Fit the cutter and apply light pressure while turning.
2. Once the correct bearing face of the valve seat is reached, its width must be reduced with
   the cutter head **212**. Cut first at **(2)** and then at **(3)**, to achieve a normal seat
   width.
3. Grind in the valve and refit it.

> **NOTES:**
> - Sooted seats must first be cleaned with a wire brush.
> - If recutting leaves light scoring on the seat face, it is enough to offset one of the
>   blades slightly and recut briefly.
> - If the pilot jams in the valve guide during dismantling, pull it out using the mandrel.
> - When the blades are replaced, make sure their acute (cutting) angle is aligned in the
>   direction of rotation.

## Fitting the cylinder head

> **IMPORTANT:** it is of the greatest importance to fit the cylinder head correctly aligned,
> because the alignment of the distributor shaft to its drive pinion depends on it.

1. Remove the liner retainer.
2. Make sure the dowel sleeve for the cylinder head is present in the cylinder block.
3. Lay the cylinder head gasket on — **it must not be lifted off again**.

## Tightening the cylinder head bolts

> This is an important operation to be carried out very carefully, since incorrectly tightened
> cylinder head bolts can cause leaks.

Use the torque wrench **Mot. 50**. Observe:

- the **sequence**;
- the **torque** (per the table).

Tighten evenly, not in jerks; otherwise slacken and retighten several times. Then remove the
centring piece.

### Tightening sequence

The bolts are numbered on the photograph on this page. The arrow under the photo points to the
**clutch (flywheel) end** (*Kupplung*). Seen as in the photo, with the clutch end on the right:

| Row | Bolt order, left to right (clutch end on the right) |
|---|---|
| Far row | 10 — 6 — 1 — 3 — 7 |
| Near row | 8 — 4 — 2 — 5 — 9 |

In words: start at the centre (1 far, 2 near), then work outwards in a spiral — 3 far and
4 near, 5 near and 6 far, 7 far and 8 near, and finish with 9 near and 10 far at the two
ends.

### Tightening procedure

| Operation | Cold engine, first fitting | Run the engine 10 minutes | Slacken ¼ turn | Warm engine, 50 min after stopping | Cold engine, at least 2 h 30 min after stopping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fitting the cylinder head | 7.5 to 8.25 mkp | X | X | 8.5 to 9 mkp | — |
| 500 km after fitting the cylinder head | — | — | X | 8.5 to 9 mkp | **or** 7.75 to 8.25 mkp |

Read across each row in order. When fitting the head: tighten cold to **7.5 to 8.25 mkp**, run
the engine for 10 minutes, slacken each bolt ¼ turn, and retighten warm — 50 minutes after
stopping the engine — to **8.5 to 9 mkp**. After **500 km**: slacken each bolt ¼ turn and
retighten either warm (50 minutes after stopping) to **8.5 to 9 mkp**, or cold (at least
2 hours 30 minutes after stopping) to **7.75 to 8.25 mkp**. Follow the sequence above at
every stage.

## Dismantling the cylinder block

1. Remove the cylinder head.
2. Remove the tappet chamber gasket.
3. Fit the liner retainer **Mot.521**.
4. Remove:
   - the distributor drive pinion;
   - the rear camshaft bearing;
   - the left-hand bearing pedestal;
   - the oil sump;
   - the oil pump and the two gears.
5. Remove the gaskets.
6. Unlock and slacken the bolt of the chain tensioner cylinder.
7. Insert a **3 mm** hexagon key into the tensioner plunger cylinder and turn it clockwise
   until the tension of the plunger on the chain is released.
8. Remove the chain tensioner complete with plunger and strainer.
9. Remove the timing chain guide rails.
10. Remove the two securing bolts of the camshaft flange.
11. Remove the key of the crankshaft pulley.
12. Remove the crankshaft sprocket and the timing chain.

<a id="p40"></a>
**[PDF p.40]**

13. Unlock and slacken the flywheel bolts; remove the flywheel.
14. Mark the connecting rods: **no. 1 towards the clutch** and towards the camshaft.
15. Unscrew the con-rod nuts; remove the con-rod caps and the bearing shells.
16. Mark the main bearing caps against cap and cylinder block.
17. Slacken the cap bolts.
18. Remove the caps (except cap no. 1) and the bearing shells.
19. Press cap no. 1 upwards, tapping lightly on its two lower ends with a plastic mallet.
20. Remove the gasket and the two side seals of the bearing cap.
21. Remove the crankshaft, the bearing shells and the thrust washers.
22. Remove the liner retainer.
23. Remove the liners together with the pistons and connecting rods.

## Replacing the pistons (press fit)

Take the connecting rod together with the piston out of the liner. The gudgeon pin is a press
fit in the con-rod and floats in the piston.

Use tool **Mot. 574** for removing and refitting the gudgeon pin. It comprises:

- a V-block **(A)**;
- a removal mandrel **(B)**;
- four piston support bushes **(C)** for gudgeon pins ⌀ 21 mm, 20 mm, 18 mm, 16 mm;
- four fitting mandrels with centring sleeves for gudgeon pins ⌀ 21 mm, 20 mm, 18 mm and 16 mm.

### Removing the gudgeon pin

Lay the piston on the V-block; the gudgeon pin must line up with the bore (two marks show the
centre of the bore).

<a id="p42"></a>
**[PDF p.42]**

## Fitting the gudgeon pin (press fit)

### Preparing the connecting rod

1. Check the con-rod with an alignment gauge and straighten it if necessary.
2. With the con-rod back in correct condition, heat the con-rod small end to a temperature of
   **250 °C**. Use a **1500 W** hot plate.
3. Lay the con-rod small ends on the hot plate, making sure the whole face is in contact.

<a id="p43"></a>
**[PDF p.43]**

4. Fit the support bush **(C)** of the diameter corresponding to the gudgeon pin on the
   V-block, and secure the piston to the V-block with the retaining clamp. The machined flat on
   the piston boss must rest on the bush.

> **The following operations are to be carried out as quickly as possible**, so that the heated
> con-rod cools as little as possible.

<a id="p44"></a>
**[PDF p.44]**

## Removing and refitting the piston and gudgeon pin — floating type

### Removing the gudgeon pin

1. Remove one circlip from the gudgeon pin.
2. Press the gudgeon pin out and separate the piston from the con-rod.

### Preparing the connecting rod

The gudgeon pin floats in both the con-rod and the piston. The con-rod small end is fitted with
a bush.

If the new gudgeon pin has too much play, press in a new bush, making sure its oil feed hole
lines up with the one in the con-rod. Ream the bush until the gudgeon pin slides in it without
play. Check that the con-rod is correctly aligned.

### Fitting the gudgeon pin

1. Fit one circlip into the piston.
2. Slide the gudgeon pin into the con-rod and the piston.
3. Observe the fitted orientation of the two parts to each other:
   - **arrow on the piston crown pointing downwards**;
   - **number on the con-rod foot pointing to the right** (the piston crown then faces the
     fitter).
4. Fit the second circlip and check that all parts move freely.
5. Fit to the piston:
   - the oil scraper ring;
   - the compression ring (marking towards the combustion chamber);
   - the "Top" ring.

> **The ring gaps must on no account be reworked.**

Oil the piston rings and stagger them; set the gap of the scraper ring between two oil return
holes.

> **NOTE:** the pistons are only drawn into the liners after the protrusion has been checked.

<a id="p45"></a>
**[PDF p.45]**

## Fitting the crankshaft

1. Fit the lower main bearing shells; **these are the ones with the oil holes**.
2. Oil the bearing shells.
3. Oil the main journals and fit the crankshaft.
4. Fit the thrust washers, **white metal towards the crankshaft**.

<a id="p46"></a>
**[PDF p.46]**

5. Fit the upper bearing shells (**without** oil holes) into the four bearing caps 2-3-4-5.
6. Oil the bearing shells.
7. Fit the bearing caps so that the marks made earlier line up.
8. Run the bolts down but do not tighten them.
9. Fit the two side seals to bearing cap no. 1:
   - groove of the seal facing outwards;
   - protrusion of the seals at the lower bearing face **d = 2/10 mm**.
10. Fit the bearing shell; oil the shell and the side seals.
11. In place of the front bearing bolts, fit two centring studs of **10 mm** ⌀.

<a id="p47"></a>
**[PDF p.47]**

12. Tighten the bearing cap bolts to **6.5 mkp** using the torque wrench **Mot. 50**.
13. Check that the crankshaft turns freely.
14. Set a dial gauge against the end face of the crankshaft and check the end float. It must be
    **0.05 to 0.23 mm**.

If the end float is outside this tolerance, change the thrust washers; they are supplied in
various thicknesses.

## Fitting the crankshaft oil seal

Fit the oil seal of bearing no. 1 (flywheel side) with tool **Mot. 259-01**.

> **CAUTION:** the sealing lip of this seal is very delicate; fitting must therefore be carried
> out with the utmost care.

1. Lay the seal on the tool and oil its outer circumference.
2. Offer the tool with the seal up and seat it with light taps on the tool knob.

> Tool Mot. 259-01 must always be kept in its container so that no damage occurs.

## Fitting the liners and pistons

> Before fitting liners, pistons and con-rods into the cylinder block, do not forget to fit the
> O-ring seal onto the liners; make sure they are not twisted.

Liners and pistons supplied as a set by the spare parts stores are matched to each other. Keep
matching parts together by their common number.

<a id="p51"></a>
**[PDF p.51]**

1. Fit the connecting rods onto the oiled crankpins.
2. Fit the caps with their bearing shells, observing which cap belongs to which con-rod.
3. Screw on the cap nuts.
4. Tighten the nuts to **4.5 mkp** with the torque wrench **Mot. 50**.
5. Check that the crank assembly turns freely.
6. Fit the outer and inner gears of the oil pump.

> Checking the oil pump: see [Removing, refitting and overhauling the oil pump](#removing-refitting-and-overhauling-the-oil-pump).

7. Offer up and secure the oil pump cover.

**[PDF p.52]**

## Fitting the timing chain and camshaft

1. Oil the camshaft journals and fit the shaft, but do not push it fully home.
2. Lay the timing chain onto the camshaft sprocket; align the mark on this sprocket so that it
   lies on the line of centres of the camshaft and crankshaft (see the figure on the page).
3. Fit the locating key to the crankshaft.
4. Turn the crankshaft so that the locating key points upwards.
5. Lay the crankshaft sprocket — **mark facing outwards** — into the chain, then align it so
   that it corresponds to the mark on the camshaft sprocket and the line of centres.

<a id="p53"></a>
**[PDF p.53]**

## Fitting the chain tensioner

1. Press the crankshaft sprocket on progressively with tool **Mot.525**, at the same time
   pushing the camshaft inwards.
2. Fit and tighten the two bolts of the camshaft flange.
3. Fit the chain tensioner complete with plunger and strainer.
4. Tighten the two securing bolts.
5. Tension the chain tensioner.

### Chain tensioner with manual release

The tensioner is locked and released through the bore exposed by removing the blanking plug
**(b)**, using a **3 mm** hexagon key.

<a id="p54"></a>
**[PDF p.54]**

### Chain tensioner with automatic release

Parts: plunger **(1)**, tensioning spring **(2)**, tensioning sleeve **(3)**, tensioner body
**(4)**.

The tensioning sleeve **(3)** is locked in the plunger when the pin **(e)** of the plunger
cylinder sits in the detent **(c)** of the sleeve's cut-out.

If the plunger **(1)** is fitted into the tensioner body with the tensioning sleeve locked, a
gap **"a"** of **2 to 3 mm** results between plunger and tensioner body.

The tensioning sleeve **(3)** is released when the plunger is moved towards the tensioner body
**(4)**.

## Fitting the timing cover and sump

1. Fit the timing cover with gasket and engine mounting plate.
2. Fit the bolts and align the end face of the timing cover so that it is flush with the joint
   face of the cylinder block.
3. Tighten the bolts.
4. Trim the two protruding sealing strips of the rear main bearing and the timing cover seals
   flush with the joint face.
5. Fit the oil sump — **the two shorter bolts screw into the bearing on the flywheel side**.
6. Fit to the rear camshaft bearing:
   - the gasket;
   - the O-ring of the oil gallery.

<a id="p56"></a>
**[PDF p.56]**

7. Secure the special tool **Mot.258** to the camshaft end with the pulley bolt. This tool
   serves to spread the seal lip apart.
8. Check that the small O-ring of the oil gallery is correctly seated.
9. Offer up the camshaft bearing housing, fitted with a new paper gasket, and secure it.
10. Remove the tool.
11. Insert the two roll pins of the pulley; press the pins in only lightly, as they are pushed
    fully home when the pulley is tightened. Note the alignment of the roll pin slots.

## Fitting the drive pinion for the oil pump and distributor

1. Turn the engine over until the first cylinder is at top dead centre (ignition point), with
   the cams of no. 4 cylinder on the overlap.
2. Insert the drive pinion for the oil pump and distributor.
3. Observe its alignment:
   - smaller segment towards the camshaft;
   - angle **(A)**, formed by the slot of the pinion and the axis running at right angles to
     the camshaft axis, equal to **53°**.
4. Fill the recesses in the camshaft housing with engine oil.
5. Remove the liner retainer.
6. Fit the cylinder head.

### Removal

## Removing, refitting and overhauling the oil pump

### Removal

1. Put the vehicle on a lift.
2. Drain the engine oil.
3. Remove the oil sump.
4. Slacken the securing bolts of the oil pump and remove the housing.
5. Remove the outer pump gear.

> The inner gear with the drive shaft cannot be taken out completely — the engine would have to
> be removed for that.

### Refitting

Carry out the removal operations in reverse order.

<a id="p60"></a>
**[PDF p.60]**

### Overhaul

1. Unscrew the pick-up pipe.
2. Remove the retaining split pin of the pressure relief valve and take out:
   - the spring cap;
   - the spring;
   - the spring guide;
   - the piston.
3. Clean and check all parts.
4. Check the clearance of the oil pump gears in the two positions illustrated, with the gears
   fitted in the housing:

| Position | Dimension | Min | Max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | 0.04 mm | 0.29 mm |
| 2 | B | 0.02 mm | 0.14 mm |

If the clearance is outside these tolerances, replace both gears (the inner gear is supplied
with the drive shaft).

## Removing and refitting the water pump

### Removal

1. Disconnect the battery.
2. Drain the cooling system.
3. From inside the vehicle, disconnect:
   - the heater hose at the water pump;
   - the water hoses at the pump.
4. Slacken and remove the V-belts of the water pump and alternator.
5. Slacken the securing bolt of the alternator adjusting strut at the pump.
6. Remove:
   - the water pump pulley;
   - the camshaft pulley.
7. Remove the securing bolts of the water pump.
8. Free the pump with a plastic mallet and remove it.
9. Remove the spacer plate and clean the joint face.

> **The water pump cannot be overhauled.**

## Removing and refitting the expansion tank

> If coolant escapes at the valve of the expansion tank, the valve must be replaced.

### Removal

1. Clamp the hose to the radiator shut with the clamp **Mot. 453**.
2. Unscrew the cap of the expansion tank and remove the valve.
