## Characteristics

The body is a plastic (polyester) body consisting of two main elements:

- the body floor (*Karosserieboden*)
- the body superstructure (*Karosserieaufbau*)

These two elements are fixed to a central tube frame (the chassis) with polyester resin and
glass-fibre cloth, and together form a load-bearing unit (approx. 100 kg for the body and 50 kg for
the chassis).

Removable body parts:

- doors
- bonnets/lids (front and rear)

These parts must be specially fitted to each vehicle. As spare parts they are supplied with maximum
dimensions to allow fitting to the body.

> **Note:** on request, Alpine supplies an even lighter body for racing. The weight saving here can
> be 30 to 40 kg.

## Materials used and mixing ratios

1. **Cloth**
   - Light glass-fibre cloth of 270 g/m² (ROVING light)
   - Heavy glass-fibre cloth of 520 g/m² (ROVING heavy)
   - Felt (mat) of 300 g/m²
2. **Resin** — standard polyester resin, already containing some hardening accelerator. Example:
   STERPON 1069 AC with a hardening acceleration of 33 %. The resin is used to impregnate the
   glass-fibre cloth.
3. **Hardening accelerator** — cobalt naphthenate at 6 %. Example: NL 51, added to the resin to
   speed up its "taking up" (*"Anziehen"*).
4. **Catalyst** — methyl ethyl ketone peroxide. Example: BUTUNOX or TRIGONOX X 40 ("faster"). The
   catalyst causes the resin to "take up" when used.

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: the OCR read the catalyst brand as "SUTUNOX"; the page image shows "BUTUNOX" — corrected from image. Also OCR read "189 C" in the temperature below; image shows 18° C. -->

## Application

When processing, **1.5 %** of the weight must be added as catalyst (between 1 and 2 % depending on
temperature). At temperatures above 20 °C reduce the amount of hardener; in cold weather increase it.

**Polymerisation:** after correct mixing as specified, a chemical curing reaction with heat
development occurs within 15 to 20 min (depending on the ratio), which hardens the resin. If the
repair is done in an insufficiently heated room (below 18 °C), in a draught, in high humidity or in
the open, the curing time is lengthened. It is therefore advisable to warm the surroundings with
infrared lamps or to increase the proportion of catalyst.

## Repairing the laminated-polyester body — repair kit

The Alpine spare-parts store supplies, under order no. 6000001354, a repair bag with 4 kg of resin and
its catalyst, plus 2 kg of light glass-fibre cloth and felt.

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: order number is printed "6000001354" (no spaces, unlike the "60 00 001 ..." style used elsewhere in this chapter); copied exactly. -->

Repairing a laminated-polyester body poses no particular problems. The repair methods are relatively
simple and can easily be carried out by non-specialist staff. Practically the same means and methods
are used as in manufacturing the body.

Broadly, two cases of damage are to be distinguished: either the original body shape is retained, or
the damaged part no longer bears any resemblance to its original shape.

### 1. Repairing minor damage (without replacing parts)

This mostly concerns small cracks or "frayed" surfaces (*"zerfledderte" Oberflächen*), that is,
places where the plastic has come away from the glass-fibre cloth.

<a id="p357"></a>
**[PDF p.357]**

First, roughen the inner surface (not the pressed one) carefully to remove any trace of paint or
grease and to achieve a surface condition that guarantees a good bond. This work is often done with
a grinding wheel; in hard-to-reach places a wooden file or a simple scraper also works well. The
surface prepared in this way must always extend **5 cm** beyond the edges of the damaged piece.

The actual repair then begins: the inner layers of cloth are laid first, in such a way that the two
edges of a crack are joined to each other or, in the case of a "scratched" zone, the original
mechanical strength is restored.

Light cloths (approx. 250/300 g/m²) should preferably be used, as they are easier to lay. By using
several layers the same thickness as the part being repaired can be achieved.

Cut the pieces of cloth so that each one slightly exceeds the one beneath in size, to avoid edges
lying on top of one another and thus too sharp differences in thickness. It is advisable to
impregnate the cloth with resin on a flat sheet, then lay it carefully and work it in with the brush.

After the repair and polymerisation of the resin (if necessary with infrared lamps), the surface is
to be treated. Use a file or grinding wheel to remove the paint and primer layers at the damaged
place. Starting from the cloth uncovered in this way, the surface shape is restored by filling with
polyester filler. This is practically the same work as painters do when applying filler to sheet-metal
body parts (see the paragraph on applying polyurethane lacquer).

For cracks the edges are to be bevelled, **C = 40 to 50 mm**.

> Drawing (PDF p.357): a section through a crack with both edges bevelled, marked "c" on each side
> of the crack centreline. The only dimension is C = 40 to 50 mm, stated in the text.

Use a grinding wheel (No. 24) or a file for this. Cover the zone exposed in this way with a mixture of
resin and cloth, then apply the polyester filler.

### 2. Repair by replacing a part of the body

If the shape of the body was completely lost in the collision, it goes without saying that the only
valid remedy is replacement of the part concerned. First establish which part of the body must be
replaced. The standard replacement parts supplied by Alpine are found on plates 82-05 to 82-08 of the
spare-parts catalogue PR 871.

The part to be replaced is cut out with a saw; then the replacement part is fitted. The latter is
held by small sheet-metal plates (size approx. 50 x 15 mm) fixed with two cross-head screws. The
connecting cloth is then laid as described above. After curing, the small plates can be removed for
the surface treatment, i.e.:

1. bevel the edges over a width of approx. 5 cm on both sides of the joint line;
2. lay and impregnate small strips of cloth (1 and 2) over the joint;
3. level the surface with polyester filler;
4. prime, sand and paint.

A properly carried-out repair is absolutely invisible and in no way impairs the value of the vehicle
treated, since the durability of the body is not reduced (continued on the next page).

<a id="p358"></a>
**[PDF p.358]**

Furthermore, these jobs can be carried out by any body shop, provided one or two workers have
attended a course of a few days; the latter is, however, only really worthwhile if larger repairs
become necessary.

The replacement parts are made by the manufacturer; experience shows, however, that no problems arise
from this, since the part concerned can be ordered under the number given in the Alpine catalogue
PR 871. In addition, no special tools outside the ordinary are needed, only those that every workshop
has anyway.

> **Note:** brushes and containers are cleaned with acetone.

#### 3. Preparing the surfaces

- Roughen with abrasive paper 220 - 320, 400 (wet).

  lead to the lacquer lifting.
- Sand.
- Apply the polyester primer. Prepare directly before processing. Pot life: **20 min**.
  - primer K 89007 = 100 parts
  - catalyst 817 = 10 parts

### Windscreen — drain-hole drilling and refitting (continued)

1. Using a 9.5 mm cutter, long version (usable length 150 mm), drill a hole as shown in the figure:
   hold the drill at an angle that ensures the hole emerges at the stated point in the door frame.
2. Insert a PVC tube of 8/10.
3. If the vehicle is fitted with a heated windscreen, use the guide tube for the power cables; route the
   latter towards the windscreen, and for this drill out the door pillar to 8 mm Ø as drawn.
4. Reduce the thickness of the trim on the windscreen pillars over the width of the frame.
5. Fit the windscreen in the usual way with the new seal No. 60 00 001 281; seal the joint between
   seal and body with Caoustat 20, Ref. 306, or with Filgum from BOYRIVEN, and make the joint between
   glass and seal with Caoustat or Electroplastic type PB.

> **Note:** the new seal can be recognised by a fine longitudinal groove on the inner sealing lip.

### Rear window

1. Remove the rear window and clean the frame.
2. As described above, drill two holes of 8 mm diameter according to Fig. D.
3. Lift the parcel-shelf (*Ablagebrett*) trim beforehand to check that the holes do not emerge into
   the interior but into the engine compartment.
4. Thin the shelf trim at the frame.
5. Fit the rear window with a new seal No. 60 00 001 282; use the same sealing compounds as for the
   windscreen.

> **Note:** the new seal can be recognised by two fine longitudinal grooves on the inner sealing lip.

### Sealing the roof gutters

Symptom: water collects on the dashboard at the ends of the windscreen-pillar trims, especially when
the vehicle has been parked for a long time.

This water entry, which seems to be caused by a leaking windscreen, is due to inadequate sealing
between the aluminium gutter No. 60 00 000 163 and 164 (plate 82-19 of PR 871) and the roof edge.
The remedy is given on the previous page (PDF p.365).

## Checking the chassis (section A)

The following tools are required:

- a) the gauge **Car.27** (*Messlehre*)
- b) the body jig (*Karosserie-Richtbank*)
- c) certain tools of the workshop's own manufacture

### 1. Various uses of the gauge (see Alpine drawing 05-11-04)

First check the position of the plumb disc (*Lotscheibe*), which is welded under the central beam.
This disc must be at a distance of **570 mm ± 2** from the pedal-assembly axis. Correct its position if
necessary, or alternatively drill a hole of **10.5 mm** diameter in the beam at the place concerned
and close it with sealing compound.

The drawing is reproduced in [19-chassis.md](19-chassis.md) (PDF p.372).

#### a) Front axle check — alignment of the lower wishbones

- Check the caster and the difference between right and left; maximum deviation **1°** (an adjustment
  of 1° corresponds to an offset of the lower ball joint of 3 mm).
- Determine and compare the dimensions **E** and **E1** with the gauge fitted with the **short** probe
  tip (the measuring points on the front axle are the bulges of the bearing cups of the lower ball
  joints).

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| E | 1005 ± 2 |
| E1 | 1008 ± 2 |

Since it is preferable for the caster on the right to be somewhat greater than on the left, the
dimension E1 may be 3 mm greater than the dimension E (see above).

#### b) Front axle check — position of the upper wishbones and the track-rod ball joints

- Fit the gauge with the **angled** probe tip and compare the distances right and left.

#### c) Front axle crossmember check — with the front axle fitted or removed

The measuring points to be checked are the axes, or the inner fixing bolts, of the rear lower
trailing arms, right and left.

- Compare: **A = A1 = 794 mm ± 2**

#### d) Rear axle check — position of the axle tubes

- Taking into account the restrictions given on page N-13 of MR 131: use the centre of the lower
  fixing bolt of the bearing cage as the measuring point; fit the gauge with the **short** probe tip
  and compare:

| Dimension | Value |
|---|---|
| D = D1 | 1386 mm ± 2 for all models except 1600 S |
| D = D1 | 1380 mm ± 2 for 1600 S |

The engine axis of the Alpine **1600 S** is nearly horizontal, whereas on the Alpine **1300** it is
inclined by approx. **3°** (spacer wedges of 12 instead of 6 mm). This difference shows up as an
increase of dimensions D and D1 by **6 mm**. (The inclination of the engine gives the wheels toe-in
