## Cooling system

Sealed system with expansion tank.

**Capacity:**

| Version | Coolant capacity |
|---|---|
| All versions with front radiator | 13 litres |
| All versions with rear radiator | 6.5 to 7 litres |

On vehicles with an **aluminium front radiator**, only a mixture of **50 % antifreeze and 50 %
demineralized water**, or ready-mixed coolant (part no. **806 834** — 6 litre can, or
**806 835** — 8 litre can) may be used.

### Alpine 1300 G — 1300 S — 1600 S

Front-mounted radiator with two fans, one of them switched on by hand. On vehicles in racing
specification a water box replaces the expansion tank. **No thermostat is fitted on the 1600 GS.**

### Alpine 1300

Rear radiator — version **R. 1135** — cooled by a fan.

The figure shows the rear radiator with its expansion tank, arrows marking its mountings and
connections, and the fan.

### Thermostat

Depending on outside temperature and use, **1 to 4 additional overflow holes of 4.5 diameter**
were drilled ("winter" or "summer" thermostat). "Winter" thermostat for temperatures **below
15 °C**.

> **NOTE:** to prevent overheating ("boiling"), especially at high outside temperatures, the
> following measures can be taken:
>
> - Careful inspection, checking and cleaning of the whole cooling system, including the V-belts,
>   the expansion tank valve, the bleeding, etc. (The system works at **0.1 - 0.2 bar**
>   overpressure, which naturally puts all hose connections, seals etc. under particularly high
>   load. This overpressure is very important, since the temperature at which water boils rises
>   when the pressure is increased.)
> - Removing the thermostat. Recommended only in summer, since the engine then reaches its
>   operating temperature very slowly. Do not put the engine under full load until the water and
>   oil have reached an adequate temperature!
> - Fitting an additional water radiator: it is connected in place of the heater matrix, which is
>   taken out of service and its hoses short-circuited with a suitable piece of pipe. A normal
>   heater matrix is suitable as the additional radiator, mounted for example in the air duct of a
>   wing. The effect obtained is that of a heater permanently switched on, i.e. an enlarged
>   heating surface.

## Draining, filling and bleeding the cooling system

### Front radiator — draining

1. Open the cap of the expansion tank.
2. Set the heater to "Warm".
3. Open the drain plugs on the radiator (if present) and on the water pump.
4. Open the heater bleed screw (hose between tank and "bulkhead").
5. If necessary, open a hose connection on the water pipe that runs inside the central tube
   (backbone), to drain the system completely.
6. Flush carefully.

### Rear radiator — draining

1. Open the cap of the expansion tank.
2. Remove the drain plug on the radiator.
3. Open the filler opening on the radiator.
4. Set the heater to "Warm".
5. Open the heater bleed screw (between tank and "bulkhead").
6. Open the drain screw on the engine.
7. Flush carefully.

### Front radiator — filling and bleeding

1. If necessary, raise the rear of the car.
2. Screw in all the drain plugs (fit new seals, if present).
3. Fill the expansion tank to the "Maxi" mark and close it.
4. Fill the system with coolant through the filler opening.
5. Open the bleed screws on the water pump and on the heater until the coolant runs free of
   bubbles (keep topping up steadily at the same time).
6. Start the engine and wait until the thermostat opens (the radiator becomes warm).
7. Check that bubble-free coolant comes out immediately at the water pump and heater.
8. Close the filler opening.
9. After about **100 km**, check that bubble-free coolant comes out at the water pump and heater.

### Rear radiator — filling and bleeding

1. If necessary, raise the rear of the car.
2. Screw in all the drain plugs (fit new seals, if present).
3. Fill the expansion tank to the "Maxi" mark and close it.
4. Fill the system through the radiator filler opening.
5. Open the bleed screw on the water pump until coolant comes out free of bubbles.
6. Start the engine.
7. Open the bleed screw on the heater until coolant comes out free of bubbles.
8. Wait until the thermostat opens (bubbling in the radiator filler opening).
9. Check that bubble-free coolant comes out immediately at the heater bleed.
10. Close the radiator filler opening.
