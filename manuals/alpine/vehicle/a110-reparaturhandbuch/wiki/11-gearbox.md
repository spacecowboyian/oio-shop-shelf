## Gearbox type 353 / 364 — 5-speed

### Technical data

- Two-piece light-alloy casing (pressure die-cast).
- Five synchronised forward gears:
  - 1st and 2nd: Renault synchromesh;
  - 3rd and 4th: Borg-Warner synchromesh;
  - one reverse gear, Borg-Warner synchromesh.

**Primary shaft:** cast in one piece with five gears. One driven gear running on a needle
bearing. One synchro hub.

**Secondary shaft:** four driven running gears. One gear seated on splines. Two synchro hubs.
The 3rd/4th sliding gear also forms the reverse gear.

**Reverse shaft:** one driven gear.

**Differential:** the differential comprises two sun (side) gears and four planet (differential)
gears.

| Gear | Teeth |
|---|---|
| Crown wheel | 33 |
| Pinion | 8 |

(See also the table on PDF pp.172–173 for other final drives.)

The speedometer is driven by a square drive, since there is no transmission angle as on the
R8G. Exclusively for racing purposes, a **"HEWLAND"** limited-slip differential is available.

> **NOTE:** if gearbox oil escapes at **C** or at the wheel hub, the oil seal is defective (see
> the differential cross-section, PDF p.176). Remove the corresponding universal joint including
> the differential bearing carrier, and press out the defective seal. Press the new seal in
> carefully using the bearing outer race, and carry out the removal operations in reverse
> order. **Do not forget the spacer shims!**

### Lubrication

The gears are splash-lubricated. The oil is filled through the filler plug **(A)** on the
side of the casing. Plug **(A)** also serves as the oil level check. The oil is drained
through plug **(B)**.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Oil capacity | **2 litres** |
| Oil grade | **EP 80** (hypoid oil) |

**Checking the oil level:** unscrew plug **(A)**. The oil must reach the lower thread edge of
the opening (spanner **B.Vi. 380**).

## Removing and refitting the gearbox

**Alpine 1300 - 1300 G - 1300 S:** on these vehicles the gearbox can be removed on its own.

**Alpine 1600:** it is recommended to remove the complete power unit.

### Removal

1. Disconnect the battery.
2. Release the throttle cable.
3. Remove the starter cover plate.
4. Disconnect and remove the starter.
5. Remove the silencer.
6. Remove the left-hand engine panel.
7. Slacken the rear wheel nuts.
8. Raise the vehicle with the jack **Cha.23** onto the axle stands **Cha.21** and **Cha.22**.
9. Remove the wheels.
10. Drain the gearbox oil.
11. Release the handbrake cable.
12. Open the locknut of the handbrake adjustment as far as possible and slacken the handbrake
    adjusting screw a few turns.
13. Unhook the end of the guide plate from the guide pin.
14. Remove the brake caliper (**do not disconnect the brake hose**).
15. Unlock and remove the nuts of the rear axle radius arms.
16. Remove the three securing bolts of the rear axle radius arms at the floor panel.
17. Take out the rear axle radius arms.

## Removing and refitting the gearbox with the power unit removed

### Removal

1. Disconnect the brake pipes from the brake hoses.
2. Pull off the retaining clips.
3. Compress the rear axle springs with the compressor **Sus.21**.
4. Release the upper shock absorber mountings.
5. Unhook the check straps on one side.
6. Take out the springs and the rubber mountings.
7. Unscrew the securing bolts of the side rubber mountings on the cross-member.
8. Remove the bolt of the upper rubber mounting.
9. Take off the cross-member.
10. Mark the position of the joint shells relative to the differential bearing carriers and the
    casing.
11. Slacken the securing nuts and pull off the joint shells.
12. Remove the axle trumpets and secure the differential bearing carriers again with two nuts.

<a id="p184"></a>
**[PDF p.184]**

13. Remove the starter.
14. Remove the clutch housing cover plate and the stiffener.
15. Slacken the connecting bolts between the clutch housing and the engine (arrowed on drawing
    61 532).
16. Separate the gearbox from the engine.

### Refitting

Carry out all removal operations in reverse order, noting the following:

- Lightly grease the end of the clutch shaft.
- Coat the seating faces of the joint shells with sealing compound **"Perfect-Seal"** (part no.
  **805 463**) and observe the marks made on removal.
- Fit flat washers at the mounting points of the side rubber mountings and of the cable sleeves.
- The gearbox oil is filled only after installation in the vehicle.

## Replacing the gearshift linkage or the gear lever

Remove the gaiter and the cover housing of the gear lever. Disconnect the linkage at the gearbox
and take it out, together with the gear lever, rearwards and downwards. Separate linkage and
lever. If the linkage is difficult to remove, slacken the rear engine mounting so that the
gearbox can be raised.

### Dismantling

1. Secure the gearbox on the mounting support **B.Vi.240**. Remove the mounting bracket.
2. Remove the clutch release fork.
3. Remove the securing bolts of the clutch housing and take the housing off (bolts arrowed on

5. Drive out the roll pin of the selector shaft with the drift **B.Vi.31 A**.

8. Remove the securing bolts of the front cover.
9. Pull the front cover off the casing and remove it.

## Reassembly

### Secondary shaft

#### Matching of crown wheel and pinion

The crown wheel and pinion are lapped together in manufacture. They must therefore not be
separated. Damage to one part necessarily entails replacing both parts.

The same markings are found on the crown wheel and on the pinion: **for example 27-200**
(drawing 65 348).

> **THE OTHER MARKINGS MADE ON THE CROWN WHEEL MUST, AS A RULE, BE DISREGARDED.**

#### Pairing of secondary shaft and synchro hubs

The synchro hubs and the secondary shaft are matched to each other.

**1 — Checking the parts**

- *The pinion (the secondary shaft), the crown wheel or the roller bearing is damaged.*

  Replacing the crown wheel and pinion: the secondary shaft, which forms one piece with the
  pinion, is supplied with the roller bearing shrunk on. The synchro hubs can be re-used, but
  the old secondary shaft must be measured before the new parts are ordered, so that the match
  with the synchro hubs is preserved.

- *The synchro hubs are damaged.*

  Replace the synchro hubs. The secondary shaft can be retained; however, it must be measured
  so that the matching synchro hubs can be ordered and the matching of these parts thereby
  ensured.

**[PDF p.195]**

## A — Setting the pinion cone distance

### Position of the pinion

The position of the pinion is correct when the distance between its end face and the centre
line of the crown wheel axis is **A = 50.50 mm**.

The setting is made by fitting a spacer shim of the appropriate thickness between the taper
roller bearing and the spline shoulder of the secondary shaft (drawing 65 083).

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: A = 50.50 mm here is the nominal for the type 353 gearbox with 10 mm bolts.

<a id="p202"></a>
**[PDF p.202]**

In exceptional cases the dimension A may not correspond to the theoretical distance. A number
**X** then appears on the end face of the pinion — next to the pairing number — representing the
difference between A and the real distance (drawing 60 553.1).

This number is given in **1/100 mm**, for example **20**. The distance must then be A plus the
stated number. In our example:

**50.50 + 0.20 = 50.70 mm**

### Checking the cone distance

1. Secure the left-hand casing half on the support **B.Vi.240**.
2. Insert the secondary shaft and secure it with a few bolts (do not tighten fully).
3. Secure the spacer plate with its two bolts.
4. Tighten the connecting bolts of the two casing halves.
5. The cone distance is checked with the gauge **T.Ar.64**. The base plate with the mark **"0"**
   must bear with its back face against the right-hand casing half. The gauge must touch the end
   face of the pinion.
6. **The dimension read on the gauge (against the "0" mark) must agree with the correct cone
   distance.**
   - **If the distance is too small**, the spacer shim is replaced **by a thinner one**.
   - **If the opposite is the case**, a **thicker** spacer shim must be fitted.
7. As soon as the cone distance is correctly set, the gauge **T.Ar.64** can be removed. Remove
   the right-hand casing half. Remove the secondary shaft.

| Cone-distance spacer shims | Value |
|---|---|
| Thickness range supplied | **3.50 to 4.10 mm** |
| Increment | **5/100 mm** (0.05 mm) |

## B — Setting the crown-wheel backlash and the differential bearings

The special tool **T.Ar.63** is used for this; it consists of two each of:

- measuring bells **(1)**;
- threaded beams **(2)** with spindle **(3)**.

### 1 — Backlash

1. Into each differential bearing carrier fit:
   - the bell **(1)** in place of the lip seal;
   - the bearing outer race (press it fully home with the press).
2. Fit the differential bearing carriers to the corresponding casing halves (observe the marks

3. Screw the spindle **(3)** on the beam **(2)** fully out and offer the beam up to the
   differential bearing carrier.
4. Secure the differential bearing carrier with at least **4 nuts**, but do not tighten them
   fully.
5. Insert the differential with its bearings.
6. Insert the secondary shaft.
7. Fit the right-hand casing half and secure it with a few bolts (do not tighten fully).
8. Fit the front spacer plate with its two bolts and tighten the connecting bolts of the two
   casing halves.
9. Tighten the securing bolts of the differential bearing carriers to **5 mkp**.

**[PDF p.204]**

    outer races (**do not force**). Make sure the pads turn freely up to the point of contact.
11. On both sides, measure the clearance **J** between beam **(2)** and thrust pad **(1)**
    (section 58 095.2, view 58 096.2).

    Example:

    | Side | J |
    |---|---|
    | Right | 80/100 mm |
    | Left | 95/100 mm |

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Crown wheel / pinion backlash | **0.12 to 0.25 mm** |

### 2 — Differential bearings

The setting is made by placing spacer shims **C** under the outer races in the differential
bearing carriers (section 58 192).

1. Measure the clearance **K** between the beam **(2)** and the thrust pad **(1)** at the same
   point as when determining clearance **J**.

   Example:

   | Side | K |
   |---|---|
   | Right | 230/100 mm |
   | Left | 215/100 mm |

2. The thickness of the shims to be fitted on each side is therefore: **C = K − J**.

   Example:

   | Side | Calculation (as printed) |
   |---|---|
   | Right | 230/100 − 80/100 = **150/100 mm** |
   | Left | 215/100 − 95/100 = **120/100 mm** |

   This setting gives a fitting of the bearings **without play**.

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: the left-hand result is printed "120/100 mm" but a scan blemish makes the

Two cases are possible:

**a) The old bearings are refitted.** The differential must turn without play. The measured
setting is therefore final.

1. Remove:
   - the right-hand casing half;
   - the secondary shaft;
   - the differential;
   - the beams **(2)** and the differential bearing carriers.
2. Press out the bearing outer races, supporting on the thrust pads.
3. Into each differential bearing carrier fit:
   - the seal;
   - the spacer shims **C** of the previously determined thickness. Use as few shims as
     possible. Shims are available in the following thicknesses: **0.1 - 0.2 - 0.5 and 1 mm**;
   - the bearing outer races (press them in).

<a id="p206"></a>
**[PDF p.206]**

**b) New bearings.** New bearings must be fitted **with preload**.

The rotating resistance achieved with this preload must be **between 0.080 and 0.180 mkp**. To
achieve it, a spacer shim of **15/100 mm** thickness is fitted on both sides in addition to the

1. Remove:
   - the right-hand casing half;
   - the secondary shaft;
   - the differential;
   - the beams **(2)** and the differential bearing carriers.
2. Press out the bearing outer races, supporting on the thrust pads.
3. Into the differential bearing carriers fit:
   - the seal;
   - the spacer shims **C + 15/100 mm**. Use as few shims as possible; they are supplied in the

|---|---|

1. Fit the corresponding differential bearing carrier into the left-hand casing half. Fit the

   the differential turning.
6. If the setting is not correct, the shims at both bearings must be reduced or increased by the

7. After setting, separate the casing halves and remove the differential and the carriers.

#### Settings before assembly (type 330)

Before assembling the gearbox, the following settings must be made:

- cone distance (see also p. 240!);
- backlash, and fitting of the differential bearings without play (when the old bearings are
  re-used) or with preload (when new bearings are used);
- play of the primary shaft bearings.

#### A — Setting the cone distance (type 330)

**Position of the pinion:** the position of the pinion is correct when there is a distance of
**A = 50.50 mm** between its end face and the centre of the crown wheel axis (section 58 090). To
achieve this setting, a washer of specific thickness is fitted between the double roller bearing
and the shoulder of the secondary shaft.

**Exceptional cases:** in exceptional cases the distance A may not correspond to the setting
dimension. A number in 1/100 mm is then stamped on the end face of the pinion — next to the
pairing number — e.g. **20**. The cone distance is then dimension A plus the stated number. In
our example:

**50.50 + 0.20 = 50.70 mm**

#### B — Setting the backlash and the differential bearings (type 330)

This setting is carried out with the special tool **T.Ar.63**, which consists of:

- two measuring bells **(1)**;
- and two holding bridges **(2)** with adjusting spindles **(3)**.

**1 — Backlash**

1. Into each differential bearing carrier fit:
   - a measuring bell **(1)** in place of the seal;
   - the bearing outer race (press it fully home with the press).
2. Fit the respective differential bearing carrier with its gasket to each casing half (observe
   the marks made on dismantling).
3. Screw the spindles **(3)** of the holding bridges **(2)** fully out and secure the tools on the
   differential bearing carrier.
4. Bolt the differential bearing carriers on with at least four nuts (do not tighten the nuts

5. Insert the differential with its bearings.
6. Fit the secondary shaft.
7. Bolt the right-hand casing half to the left (do not tighten fully).
8. Fit the shift cover temporarily and then tighten the connecting bolts of the two casing

10. By screwing in the spindles **(3)**, bring the measuring bells **(1)** lightly into contact

11. Now measure dimension **J** on each side, between the lower edge of the holding bridges
    **(2)** and the upper edge of the measuring bells **(1)** (section 58 095.1). Example:

    | Side | J |
    |---|---|
    | Right | 80/100 mm |
    | Left | 95/100 mm |

**2 — Setting the differential bearings (type 330)**

   upper edge of the measuring bells **(1)**. Example: right **230/100 mm**; left
   **215/100 mm**.
2. Dimension C is now determined as follows: **C = K − J**. Example:

   | Side | Calculation (as printed) |
   |---|---|
   | Right | 230/100 − 80/100 = **150/100 mm** |
   | Left | 215/100 − 95/100 = **120/100 mm** |

   These dimensions give a fitting of the bearings without play.

**a — The old bearings are re-used.** The differential must then turn without play. The setting

## Tooth-contact pattern check

Only in the rarest cases will the special measuring instruments needed to set the crown wheel and
pinion correctly (cone distance, backlash) be available. A very accurate check of the setting can,
however, also be made by means of a tooth-contact pattern check.

For this, the drive pinion is coated with marking paint (engineer's blue). The crown wheel
(differential housing) must be braked so that the secondary shaft with the pinion can be turned
**under load**. The contact pattern then appears on both flanks of the pinion.

| Contact pattern on the pinion | Diagnosis / correction |
|---|---|
| Evenly distributed over the tooth centre of drive and coast flanks | **Setting is correct.** |
| Drive flank at the small diameter, coast flank at the large diameter | Pinion away from the crown wheel axis: choose a **thinner** spacer shim A (see printed p. 206 or p. 230, arrow A). At the same time **reduce** the backlash, i.e. crown wheel towards the pinion axis (by means of spacer shims C, see printed p. 210 or p. 233, "C"). |
| Drive flank at the large diameter, coast flank at the small diameter | Pinion towards the crown wheel axis, A **thicker**. Then crown wheel away from the pinion axis (with C). |
| Both flanks at the small diameter | **Reduce** the backlash, i.e. crown wheel towards the pinion (by means of C). |
| Both flanks at the large diameter | **Increase** the backlash, i.e. crown wheel away from the pinion (by means of C). |

![Tooth-contact patterns on the pinion and their corrections, PDF p.235](https://github.com/spacecowboyian/oio-shop-shelf/releases/download/manuals-a110-reparaturhandbuch/p0235-tooth-contact-patterns.png)

printed p. 206 = [A — Setting the pinion cone distance](#a-setting-the-pinion-cone-distance)
