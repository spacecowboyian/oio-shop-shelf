## Dismantling the cylinder head

1. Remove:
   - the distributor;
   - the alternator with its V-belt;
   - the valve cover;
   - the fan housing.
   <!-- NEEDS REVIEW: printed "Ventilatorgehäuse"; rendered "fan housing" (the cooling fan cowl). -->
2. Disconnect:
   - the exhaust pipe at the manifold and at the side reinforcement;
   - if fitted, the hot-air hose.
3. Slacken the valve adjusting screws and remove the pushrods (lay them out in order).
4. Remove the cylinder head bolts — **except the bolt at the dowel sleeve on the distributor
   side: this bolt is only slackened and stays in contact with the cylinder head.**

> **CAUTION:** because the cylinder head gasket sticks to the head as well as to the block and
> the liners, **the cylinder head must not be lifted straight off** — the liners would come
> away from their seats, so that foreign matter could get in and the seat seals could be
> damaged.

5. To free the head, first turn it about the dowel sleeve on the distributor side (the bolt
   left in the cylinder head).

<a id="p70"></a>
**[PDF p.70]**

6. Free the cylinder head by turning it in the horizontal plane; if necessary help it with
   light blows of a plastic mallet on the corners of the head.
7. Remove the securing bolt.
8. Lift off the cylinder head.
9. Fit the liner retainer **Mot.521**.

## Cleaning the cylinder block and checking the joint face

See [Engine types 807 / 843 / 844 — overhaul](04-engine-807-843-844.md) (the page says
"see engine 807").

> **The cleaning work must be carried out with particular care**, to avoid foreign matter
> getting into the lubrication passages of the rocker shafts (passages in the cylinder block
> and in the cylinder head). Otherwise there is a risk that the oil outlet holes will block,
> which would quickly damage the cams, the rocker pedestals and the rocker arms.

Draw off the oil present in the cylinder head bolt holes with a syringe.

> **This is necessary to ensure correct tightening of the cylinder head bolts.**

Check the liner protrusion (see
[Fitting the liners and pistons](#fitting-the-liners-and-pistons)).

## Replacing a valve guide

This work is carried out with the cylinder head removed, using the plate **Mot.121** and the
tool **Mot.148**, which comprises:

- a mandrel **(1)**;
- a guide bush **(2)**.

1. If necessary, remove the valve cover retaining studs.
2. Press the valve guide out with a press. Lay the cylinder head on the plate **Mot.121** and
   use the mandrel **(1)** of tool **Mot.148** for the pressing.
3. Check whether the guide pressed out is still the original guide or a repair-size guide.
4. Replace the worn valve guide with one of the next size up in diameter.

**Valve guide position:**

| Valve | Dimension |
|---|---|
| Intake | A = 26.5 mm |
| Exhaust | B = 26.2 mm |

5. Insert the mandrel **(1)** into the guide bush **(2)**; note the different positions for the

## Recutting a valve seat

Tool: **MS 518**.

1. Insert the pilot **150-7** into the valve guide.
2. If necessary, set the blades of the cutter head **204** to the seat diameter.
3. Fit the cutter head onto the pilot, taking care that it does not drop onto the valve seat.

<a id="p74"></a>
**[PDF p.74]**

4. Fit the handle and apply light pressure while turning.
5. Once the correct bearing face of the valve seat is reached, its width must be reduced with
   the cutter head **212**. Cut first at **(2)** and then at **(3)**, to achieve a normal seat
   width.
6. Grind in the valve and refit it.

> **NOTES:**
> - Sooted seats must first be cleaned with a wire brush.
> - If recutting leaves light scoring on the seat face, it is enough to offset one of the
>   blades slightly and recut briefly.
> - If the pilot jams in the valve guide during dismantling, pull it out using the mandrel.
> - When the blades are replaced, make sure their acute (cutting) angle is aligned in the
>   direction of rotation.

4. Fit the cylinder head.
5. Run the cylinder head bolts down, then tighten them to **5.5 m.daN** with the torque wrench

## Dismantling the cylinder block

1. Remove the cylinder head.
2. Fit the liner retainer **Mot.521**.
3. Take out the tappets (lay them out in order).
4. Remove the distributor drive pinion using a bolt **12 x 1.75**.
5. Remove:
   - the pulley;
   - the oil sump;
   - the timing cover;
   - the oil pump.
6. Remove the gaskets.
7. Remove the hydraulic chain tensioner or — in the case of a mechanical chain tensioner —
   remove the camshaft sprocket together with the timing chain.
8. If fitted, take out the chain tensioner oil strainer.
9. Unlock and slacken the camshaft sprocket securing bolt.
10. Pull off the camshaft sprocket and the timing chain.

<a id="p77"></a>
**[PDF p.77]**

11. Remove the camshaft flange bolts.
12. Remove the camshaft.
13. Pull off the crankshaft sprocket with the tool **Mot.49**; for this, screw a bolt **(V)**
    into the crankshaft (with a centre-punch mark in the middle of the bolt head).
14. Remove the flywheel.
15. Mark the connecting rods: **no. 1 towards the clutch, and on the side away from the
    camshaft.**
16. Slacken the con-rod nuts.
17. Remove the con-rod caps and the bearing shells.
18. Mark the position of the main bearing caps on the cylinder block.
19. Slacken the cap bolts and remove the bearing caps with their bearing shells.

The page figures number the con-rods 1 to 4 and the main bearing caps 1 to 5 from the flywheel
end.

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: a stray printed "B-101" sits at the foot of this page — a section code that
belongs to another document; it is not this page's content. -->

<a id="p78"></a>
**[PDF p.78]**

20. Take out the crankshaft, the bearing shells and the thrust washers.
21. Remove the liner retainer.
22. Take out the liners, pistons and connecting rods together.
23. Take the cylinder block off the engine stand.

> Whether the cylinder block is being reused or a new block is being fitted, it **must** be
> thoroughly cleaned. Check the oil passages with a wire for a clear passage.

> **The liners and pistons supplied as a set by the spare parts stores are matched to each
> other.** Before cleaning, number the matching parts **1 to 4**, so they cannot be mixed up
> later.

**Remove the protective coating with a solvent — never scrape it off!**

## Pressing out the gudgeon pin

1. Lay the piston on the V-block; the gudgeon pin must line up with the bore of the V-block
   (two marks show the centre of the bore and make alignment easier).
2. Press out the gudgeon pin with the removal mandrel **(B)**.

## Pressing in the gudgeon pin

### Preparing the connecting rod

1. Check the con-rod (for bend and twist).
2. If it is fit for reuse, heat the con-rod small end on the hot plate to **250 °C** (heating
   time **10 to 15 minutes**).
3. For this, lay the con-rod small ends on the hot plate, making sure the small ends rest on the
   plate over their whole face.
4. To check the temperature, lay a small piece of solder whose melting point is about
   **250 °C** on the con-rod at **(a)**.

### Preparing the gudgeon pin

1. Check that the gudgeon pin turns correctly in the new piston.
2. Slide the gudgeon pin onto the fitting mandrel and screw on the pilot (do not tighten it, so
   the gudgeon pin can still move).

<a id="p80"></a>
**[PDF p.80]**

3. Lubricate the gudgeon pin with **Molykote oil M 55**.

### Pressing in

The pistons are marked by an arrow (pointing to the flywheel side) and by a machined flat
**(C)** (on the opposite side).

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: printed "einen Pfeil auf dem Kolbenfuss" — literally "an arrow on the piston
foot"; the figure on this page shows the arrow on the piston crown. Rendered "an arrow". -->

Align the con-rod and piston to each other as follows:

- On the V-block, fit the support bush **(C)** of the diameter corresponding to the gudgeon pin,
  and secure the piston to the V-block with the retaining clamp. **The machined flat on the
  piston boss must rest on the bush.**
- Turn the con-rod mark **(D)** made on dismantling towards the side away from the camshaft.

> **The following operations are to be carried out as quickly as possible**, so that the heated
> con-rod does not cool too much.

As soon as the piece of solder reaches its melting point (forms a drop):

1. remove the solder;
2. insert the pilot, fitted on the gudgeon pin and the fitting mandrel, into the piston;
3. with one hand, insert the con-rod into the piston, observing the marks;
4. with the other hand, quickly press in the gudgeon pin with the pilot and the fitting mandrel.
   Pressing in is complete when the pilot bottoms on the V-block.

<a id="p81"></a>
**[PDF p.81]**

5. After a few seconds, take the con-rod and piston off the V-block, slacken the centring sleeve
   and remove the fitting mandrel.
6. Move the con-rod sideways in the piston to check that the gudgeon pin cannot stand out of the
   piston boss on either side.

## Fitting the liners and pistons

This engine is fitted with **Excelnyl** liner seat seals.

Lay the liner seat seals on: use seals of **0.08 mm** thickness (blue marking).

The liner seat seals are supplied in several thicknesses for setting the protrusion:

| Thickness | Marking |
|---|---|
| 0.08 mm | blue |
| 0.10 mm | red |
| 0.12 mm | green |

1. Insert the liners with the pistons and con-rods into the cylinder block.
2. Press the liners onto their seats by hand.
3. Check the protrusion of the liners relative to the joint face of the cylinder block, using
   the base plate **Mot.252** and the dial gauge holder **Mot.251** (dial gauge with extension).

**The correct liner protrusion is between 0.04 and 0.12 mm.**

- the difference in protrusion between two adjacent liners is **0.04 mm maximum** (tolerance);
- the protrusion decreases from cylinder no. 1 to no. 4, or the other way round from no. 4 to

## Fitting the crankshaft

1. Fit the main bearing shells:
   - **they are the ones with the oil holes**;
   - the shells of bearings **1 and 3** are the same;
   - the shells of bearings **2, 4 and 5** are also the same.
2. Oil the bearing shells.
3. Oil the main journals and lay in the crankshaft.
4. Fit the thrust washers (**white metal towards the crankshaft**).

5. Fit the upper bearing shells (**without** oil holes) into the bearing caps.
6. Oil the bearing shells.
7. Fit the bearing caps so that the marks made on dismantling line up.
8. Tighten the bearing cap bolts to **5.5 to 6.5 m.daN** with the torque wrench **Mot.50**.
9. Check that the crankshaft turns freely.
10. Set a dial gauge against the end face of the crankshaft.

## Fitting the crankshaft oil seal

Fit the oil seal of the front main bearing with the tool **Mot.131-02** or **Mot.259-01**.

> **CAUTION:** the sealing lip of this seal is very delicate, so the utmost care must be taken
> when fitting it.

1. Lay the seal on the tool.
2. Oil the seal on its outer circumference.

<a id="p85"></a>
**[PDF p.85]**

Two cases can arise:

### New crankshaft

The seal is fitted in the original position. Offer the tool with the seal up to the cylinder
block and seat the tool with light taps on the tool knob.

### Crankshaft already run

In this case the seal is fitted **C = approx. 3 mm** deeper in its housing, so that the sealing
lip does not run on the groove left by the old seal.

1. Offer the tool with the seal on it and seat it against the housing.
2. Remove the tool and lay a spacer washer **3 mm** thick between the tool and the seal.
3. Seat the tool against the cylinder block again.

> The tool **Mot.131-02** must always be kept in its container, so that the seal-locating face is
> not damaged. It is advisable to fit an old seal onto the seal collar of the tool to protect it.

## Fitting the camshaft and timing gear

1. Oil the camshaft bearings and insert the shaft.
2. Tighten the camshaft flange bolts.
3. Fit the camshaft sprocket, **mark facing outwards**.
4. Align the marks of the two timing sprockets with the centres of the crankshaft and camshaft.

## Fitting the oil sump and the distributor drive pinion

1. Fit the front and rear rubber seals of the oil sump.
2. Fit the side gaskets: they must overlap the ends of the rubber seals fitted previously.
3. Centre the gaskets with 4 centring studs.
4. Fit the oil sump.
5. Insert the oiled tappets in the order they were laid out.
6. Remove the liner retainer.
7. Fit the cylinder head and adjust the valve clearance.
8. Fit the distributor drive pinion:
   - set cylinder no. 1 to its firing point (valves of no. 4 cylinder on overlap);
   - insert the pinion using a bolt of **12 x 1.75**;
   - **slot of the pinion at right angles to the longitudinal axis of the engine, with the
     larger half-circle (D) towards the clutch.**

## Replacing the camshaft

### Removal

Remove:

- the cylinder head (fit the liner retainer **Mot.521**);
- the tappets;
- the distributor drive pinion;
- the oil sump;
- the timing cover;
- the timing chain.

Remove the two camshaft flange securing bolts. Remove the camshaft.

### Refitting

1. Fit a new securing flange.
2. Press a new spacer ring on with a tube, up to the shoulder.
3. Check the end float **(J)** at the flange; first fit the camshaft sprocket and tighten its bolt
   to **2 m.daN**:
   - **J = 0.06 to 0.11 mm**.
4. Remove the camshaft sprocket.
5. Then carry out the removal operations in reverse order.

## Removing, refitting and overhauling the oil pump

The page opens with an exploded view of the oil pump (housing, relief valve parts, gears, drive
shaft, pick-up and cover), without item numbers.

### Removal

Remove:

- the oil sump (the sump bolts are arrowed in the figure).

<a id="p93"></a>
**[PDF p.93]**

- Turn the engine so that the front counterweight of the crankshaft takes up the position shown.
- Remove the gaskets and clean the joint faces.
- Remove the three securing bolts of the oil pump and take the pump off.

### Overhaul

1. Slacken the pump cover securing bolts, taking care that the ball, the ball seat and the
   pressure-relief spring do not spring out.
2. Take out the two pump gears with the drive shaft.
3. Clean and check all parts.
4. Clearance between the gears and the pump body: **if the clearance exceeds 0.2 mm, the gears
   must be replaced.**

(See also [Engine types 807 / 843 / 844 — overhaul](04-engine-807-843-844.md), "see also engine
807".)

### Refitting

1. Offer up the oil pump; **do not fit a gasket between the pump flange and the cylinder block.**
2. **Insert the three securing bolts and tighten them.**
3. Fit:
   - the rubber seals of the oil sump;
   - the side gaskets of the oil sump: coat the ends with **"Perfect Seal"**.
4. Hold the side gaskets in place with four threaded studs screwed into the corners of the
   cylinder block.
5. Make sure the front counterweight of the crankshaft has not moved from its position.
6. Offer up the oil sump, taking care that the gaskets do not slip.
7. Insert the bolts and tighten them.
8. Fill with engine oil.

## Removing and refitting a chain tensioner

### Chain tensioner with manual release

The tensioner is locked or released through the bore exposed by removing the blanking plug
**(b)**, using a **3 mm** hexagon key. The key is turned clockwise or anticlockwise
respectively.

### Chain tensioner with automatic release

| No. | Part |
|---|---|
| 1 | Plunger |
| 2 | Tensioning spring |
| 3 | Tensioning sleeve |
| 4 | Tensioner body |

The tensioning sleeve **(3)** is locked in the plunger when the pin **(e)** of the plunger
cylinder sits in the detent **(c)** of the sleeve's cut-out.

If the plunger **(1)** is fitted into the tensioner body with the tensioning sleeve locked, a gap
of approx. **2 to 3 mm** results between plunger and tensioner body.

The tensioning sleeve **(3)** is released when the plunger is moved towards the tensioner body
**(4)**.

<a id="p96"></a>
**[PDF p.96]**

### Spring-loaded mechanical chain tensioner

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: the page is headed "KETTENSPANNER MIT HANDENTRIEGELUNG" (chain tensioner with

A spring fixed to the cylinder block presses the shoe against the timing chain. The chain
tensioner consists of:

| No. | Part |
|---|---|
| 1 | Shoe |
| 2 | Tensioning spring |
| 3 | Guide bush |
| 4 | Retaining washer |
| 5 | Securing bolt and serrated lock washer |

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: printed "Druckstück" (rendered "plunger" for the p.95 tensioners and "shoe"

"Fächerscheibe" (serrated lock washer). -->

This version of the chain tensioner is fitted only to cylinder blocks equipped with an oil
pocket. The timing chain is lubricated from the return line of the oil pocket.

<!-- NEEDS REVIEW: printed "Öltasche" — rendered "oil pocket" (an oil reservoir/trough in the
block); the exact feature is not shown on this page. -->

To fit this chain tensioner, the following modifications must be made to the cylinder block:

- blank off the oil hole **(A)** for the hydraulic chain tensioner with a plug;
- fit a pin **(B)**, **⌀ 3 mm**, to locate the guide bush.

The spring is anchored in the cylinder block in the hole **(C)**.

<a id="p97"></a>
**[PDF p.97]**

#### Removal

1. Swing the shoe as far away from the timing chain as possible and fit the sleeve of the tool
   **Mot.761**.
2. Remove the tensioner together with the sleeve.
3. Separate the tensioner from the sleeve using the holding plate: secure the tensioner and
   sleeve on the holding plate, and remove the sleeve while swinging the shoe to its maximum.

#### Refitting

1. Assemble the individual parts of the tensioner on the holding plate of the tool **Mot.761**.
   Fit on the holding plate:
   - the washer **(4)**;
   - the shoe **(1)** with spring **(2)** — locate the spring in the corresponding hole **(C)** of
     the holding plate;
   - the guide bush of the tensioner **(3)** on the corresponding locating pin of the holding
     plate;
   - the securing bolt with serrated lock washer **(5)**.
2. With the chain tensioner secured on the holding plate, fit the sleeve of the tool **Mot.761**,
   swinging the shoe to its maximum.
3. Take the chain tensioner with its sleeve off the holding plate and secure it to the cylinder
   block, engaging the locating pin in the block in the groove of the guide bush and the spring
   in the hole in the block.
4. Pull off the sleeve.
