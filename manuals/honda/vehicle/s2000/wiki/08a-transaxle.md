# Clutch System Description
## Delay Orifice Mechanism ('04-08 models)

### Function
The delay orifice mechanism improves clutch operation by delaying the slave cylinder release speed when the clutch pedal is suddenly released. The delay orifice mechanism is built into the slave cylinder.

# Operation

When the clutch pedal is pressed, the fluid pressure from the master cylinder moves the one-way valve in the direction shown in the illustration. The fluid flows through two passages: the orifice part and the filter part. It then flows out to the slave cylinder to release the pressure plate and clutch disc joint.

**ORIFICE PART ONE-WAY VALVE**
From **MASTER CYLINDER**

**FILTER PART**

When the clutch pedal is released, the fluid pressure from the slave cylinder moves the one-way valve in the direction shown in the illustration. The one-way valve blocks the filter part passage and delays the clutch release speed by returning the fluid to the master cylinder through only the orifice part passage.

**FILTER PART**

## Clutch Hydraulic System Bleeding

**Notes:** Do not reuse drained fluid. Use Honda DOT 3 Brake Fluid from an unopened container; other fluid may cause corrosion and shorten system life. Do not mix brands. Keep dirt out of the fluid. Wash spilled fluid off paint immediately with water and use fender covers. It may be necessary to limit release-fork movement with a block of wood to remove all air.

1. Check that the reservoir fluid level is at the MAX (upper) line.
2. Attach a clear tube to the bleeder screw and put the other end in a container of brake fluid. Loosen the screw to let air escape. The source diagrams show bleeder-screw locations for both '00–03 and '04–08 models; each labels **9.8 N·m (1.0 kgf·m, 7.2 lbf·ft)**.
3. Keep adequate fluid in the reservoir and slowly pump the clutch pedal until bubbles stop appearing in the tube.
4. Tighten the bleeder screw securely.
5. Refill the reservoir to the MAX line.

## Clutch Pedal, Clutch Pedal Position Switch, and Clutch Interlock Switch Adjustment

**Notes:** Check the pedal position switch (p. 4-72) and interlock switch (p. 4-9). The clutch self-adjusts for wear. Clearance between the master cylinder piston and pushrod is essential; without it, the release bearing presses on the diaphragm spring and can cause clutch slippage.

1. Loosen the clutch pedal position switch locknut (A) and back off the switch (B) until it no longer touches the pedal (C).
2. Loosen the clutch pushrod locknut (D) and turn pushrod (E) to set pedal height (F) to **189 mm (7.44 in.)** and stroke (G) to **115–125 mm (4.53–4.92 in.)**.
3. Tighten the pushrod locknut.
4. With the pedal released, turn the pedal position switch in until it touches the pedal.
5. Turn the switch in a further **3/4 to 1 turn**.
6. Tighten the pedal position switch locknut.
7. Loosen the interlock switch locknut (H) and switch (I).
8. Press the pedal to the floor.
9. Release it **15–20 mm (0.59–0.79 in.)** from the fully pressed position and hold it. Adjust the interlock switch so the engine starts at this position.
10. Tighten the interlock switch locknut.
11. Check clutch operation.

The source diagram specifies **9.8 N·m (1.0 kgf·m, 7.2 lbf·ft)** for locknuts A and H, and **18 N·m (1.8 kgf·m, 13 lbf·ft)** for pushrod locknut D.

## Clutch Master Cylinder Replacement

**Notes:** Use fender covers. Wash spilled brake fluid off painted surfaces immediately with water.

1. Remove brake fluid from the master-cylinder reservoir (A) with a syringe.
2. Disconnect clutch line (B); plug or wrap its end to prevent leakage.
3. Pry out lock pin (A), pull clevis pin (B) from the yoke, and remove master-cylinder mounting nuts (C). The source diagram identifies the nuts as **8 × 1.25 mm, 13 N·m (1.3 kgf·m, 9.4 lbf·ft)**.
4. Remove the master cylinder (A).
5. Install it in reverse order.
6. Bleed the clutch hydraulic system (p. 12-6).
7. Adjust clutch pedal and switches (p. 12-7).
8. Check clutch operation and leaks.
9. Test-drive the vehicle.

## Slave Cylinder Replacement

**Notes:** Use fender covers. Wash spilled brake fluid off paint immediately with water.

1. Remove banjo bolt (A) and washers (B); disconnect clutch hose (C) from slave cylinder (D). Plug or wrap the hose end to prevent leakage. Source callouts: **'00–03 models:** 8 × 1.25 mm, 22 N·m (2.2 kgf·m, 16 lbf·ft); **'04–08 models:** 10 × 1.0 mm, 29 N·m (3.0 kgf·m, 22 lbf·ft). Replace the washers.
2. Remove two bolts (E) and the slave cylinder.
3. Pull back boot (A). Apply M-77 assembly paste (P/N 08798-9010), or equivalent rubber grease, to the boot and slave-cylinder rod (B), then reinstall the boot.
4. Apply super high temp urea grease (P/N 08798-9002) to the rod tip.
5. Install the cylinder in reverse order with new banjo-bolt washers. Make sure the boot is installed.
6. Bleed the hydraulic system (p. 12-6).
7. Check operation and leaks.
8. Test-drive.

### Pressure Plate Removal and Inspection
1. Transmission removal (see page 13-6).
2. Check the height-variation of the diaphragm spring fingers using the dial indicator (A). If the variation is more than the service limit, replace the pressure plate.
    *   Standard (New): 0.4 mm (0.016 in.) max.
    *   Service Limit: 0.6 mm (0.024 in.)
3. Install the ring gear holder (A), clutch alignment shaft (B), and remover handle (C).
    *   07LAB-PV00100 or 07924-PD20003
4. To prevent warping, remove the pressure plate mounting bolts (D) in a crisscross pattern in several steps, then remove the pressure plate (E).
5. Remove the release bearing (F) from the pressure plate.
6. Inspect the fingers of the diaphragm spring (A) for wear at the release bearing contact area.
7. Inspect the surface of the pressure (B) plate for wear, cracks, and burning.
8. Inspect for warpage using a straight edge (A) and feeler gauge (B). Measure across the pressure plate (C). If the warpage is more than the service limit, replace the pressure plate.
    *   Standard (New): 0.03 mm (0.001 in.) max.
    *   Service Limit: 0.15 mm (0.006 in.)

## Supplemental Restraint System (SRS) Precautions

The S2000 has driver and passenger airbags and seat-belt tensioners. Items marked with an asterisk in the manual contents include or are near SRS components; service requires special precautions and tools and should be done by an authorized Honda dealer.

- SRS service by an authorized Honda dealer helps avoid disabling the system and risking serious injury or death in a frontal collision.
- Incorrect removal or installation may unintentionally deploy airbags or seat-belt tensioners.
- Do not bump the SRS unit or front impact sensors while ignition is ON (II), or for at least **3 minutes** after switching it OFF.
- SRS electrical connectors are yellow. Related components are in the steering column, console, dashboard, lower dashboard panel, and above the glove box. **Do not use electrical test equipment on these circuits.**

## Transmission Fluid Inspection and Replacement

1. Raise the vehicle on a lift.
2. Remove filler plug (A) and sealing washer (B). Check fluid condition and proper level (C).
3. If dirty, remove drain plug (A) and drain the fluid.
4. Install drain plug with a **new sealing washer (B)**. Tighten the drain plug to **39 N·m (4.0 kgf·m, 29 lbf·ft)**. Refill to the proper level: **1.48 L (1.56 US qt)** for a fluid change, or **1.62 L (1.71 US qt)** at overhaul. Use Honda manual transmission fluid (MTF); engine oil can cause stiff shifting because it lacks the required additives.
5. Install filler plug (C) with a **new sealing washer (D)**. Tighten the filler plug to **44 N·m (4.5 kgf·m, 33 lbf·ft)**.

The source diagram identifies the fluid-level position and both plug locations.

# Mainshaft Thrust Clearance Adjustment

## Special Tools Required
*   Mainshaft holder 07GAJ-PG20110
*   Mainshaft base 07GAJ-PG20130

### Procedure
1. Install the mainshaft assembly in the clutch housing.
2. Install oil guide plate M and 82 mm shim(s) (A) into the transmission housing.
3. Install the transmission housing onto the clutch housing, then tighten the 8 mm flange bolts in a crisscross pattern in several steps.

**Specified Torque:**
*   8 x 1.25 mm
*   27 N-m (2.8 kgf-m, 20 lb-ft)

4. Attach the mainshaft holder (A) and mainshaft base (B) to the mainshaft as follows:
    *   Back out the mainshaft holder bolt (C), and loosen the two hex bolts (D).
    *   Fit the holder over the mainshaft so its lip is towards the transmission.
    *   Align the mainshaft holder's lip around the groove at the inside of the mainshaft splines, then tighten the hex bolts.

5. Fully seat the mainshaft by tapping its end with a plastic hammer.
6. Thread the mainshaft holder bolt in until it just contacts the wide surface of the mainshaft base.
7. Zero a dial gauge (A) on the end of the mainshaft.
